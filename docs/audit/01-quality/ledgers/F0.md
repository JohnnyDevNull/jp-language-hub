### Brief for the `de` re-translation

1. Field names never carry a German sentence. School term first
   ("konjugiertes Verb", "Subjekt", "Satzglied", "Grundwort"), field name
   once in parentheses.
2. No imperative fragments in `description`, headings or link
   descriptions ("Verstehe, warum …", "Lerne …", "Route wählen").
3. Every "Warum hier" note is a complete sentence addressed to the reader
   ("Hier lernst du …", "Damit kannst du …").
4. No developer or academic words: Frontmatter, kuratiert, deklariert,
   kanonisch, Konstituente, Kongruenz, Paradigma, produktiv, Domäne, Skript,
   Musterseite, Check.
5. A Swedish acronym is decoded word by word for the German reader.

### Brief for the `sv` re-translation

1. Swedish school terms first: `satsdel`, `rak`/`omvänd ordföljd`,
   `efterled`/`förled`, `bindeord`, `bisatsinledare`, `meningsmönster`.
   Technical terms (`finit`, `satsadverbial`) get a plain explanation at
   first use, also for Swedish readers.
2. Check every English word with a Swedish look-alike: ensidig, vinst,
   kurera, konsekvent, standardsvenska, önskemål, lära vs lära sig.
3. No coined compounds built from an English phrase; if no established
   word exists, describe the thing.
4. Same name for the same thing on every page (`fusklappar`).
5. Needs a native Swedish reader before the batch counts as passed (N).
