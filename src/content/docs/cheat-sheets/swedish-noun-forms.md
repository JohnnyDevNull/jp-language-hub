---
title: Swedish noun forms
description: A compact guide to indefinite, definite, singular, and plural Swedish nouns.
---

Learn a Swedish noun as a four-form chain.

| Meaning | Indefinite singular | Definite singular | Indefinite plural | Definite plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| car | <span lang="sv">en bil</span> | <span lang="sv">bilen</span> | <span lang="sv">bilar</span> | <span lang="sv">bilarna</span> |
| house | <span lang="sv">ett hus</span> | <span lang="sv">huset</span> | <span lang="sv">hus</span> | <span lang="sv">husen</span> |
| apple | <span lang="sv">ett äpple</span> | <span lang="sv">äpplet</span> | <span lang="sv">äpplen</span> | <span lang="sv">äpplena</span> |
| girl | <span lang="sv">en flicka</span> | <span lang="sv">flickan</span> | <span lang="sv">flickor</span> | <span lang="sv">flickorna</span> |

## Basic pattern

- The indefinite singular uses <span lang="sv">en</span> or
  <span lang="sv">ett</span>: <span lang="sv">en bil</span>,
  <span lang="sv">ett hus</span>.
- The definite article is normally a suffix:
  <span lang="sv">bilen</span>, <span lang="sv">huset</span>.
- Plural endings vary. Learn the plural with the noun.
- The definite plural adds a definite ending to the plural form:
  <span lang="sv">bilarna</span>, <span lang="sv">husen</span>.

## With a definite adjective

Swedish normally uses both a separate article and the definite noun ending:

- <span lang="sv">den stora bilen</span>
- <span lang="sv">det stora huset</span>
- <span lang="sv">de stora bilarna</span>

This pattern is often called double definiteness.

:::tip[Fast recall]
Record nouns as <span lang="sv">en bil — bilen — bilar — bilarna</span>, not as
an isolated translation pair.
:::
