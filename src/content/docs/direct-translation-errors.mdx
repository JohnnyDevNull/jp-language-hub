<KeyTakeaway>
- Translate the whole construction, including its verb and its preposition, not
  the individual words.
- German *haben* phrases for physical states become <span lang="sv">vara</span> plus an
  adjective: <span lang="sv">jag är hungrig</span>, <span lang="sv">jag är törstig</span>.
- Swedish uses <span lang="sv">jo</span> to contradict a negative question; <span lang="sv">nej</span>
  confirms the negative proposition.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

These are not vocabulary gaps. Every word is known; the construction around it
is different.

## Existence: *es gibt*

<LanguageComparison title="There is / there are">
	<p slot="de">Es gibt ein Problem.</p>
	<p slot="en">There is a problem.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Det finns ett problem.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<span lang="sv">finns</span> states that something exists. Use <span lang="sv">det är</span> only for a
description of a situation: <span lang="sv">Det är kallt idag.</span> Never translate *gibt*
with <span lang="sv">ger</span>.

## States: *ich habe Hunger*

<LanguageComparison title="Hunger and thirst">
	<p slot="de">Ich habe Hunger.</p>
	<p slot="en">I am hungry.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag är hungrig.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

The same pattern holds for <span lang="sv">Jag är törstig</span> (thirsty) and
<span lang="sv">Jag är trött</span> (tired). Swedish takes the English side here.

## Well-being: *wie geht es dir*

<LanguageComparison title="How are you?">
	<p slot="de">Wie geht es dir?</p>
	<p slot="en">How are you?</p>
	<p slot="sv">Hur mår du?</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<span lang="sv">Hur är det?</span> works as a casual variant. <span lang="sv">Hur går det?</span> exists,
but asks how something is *going* — a task, a project — not how you feel.

## Anticipation and absence

<LanguageComparison title="Looking forward to something">
	<p slot="de">Ich freue mich auf den Sommer.</p>
	<p slot="en">I am looking forward to the summer.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag ser fram emot sommaren.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Missing someone">
	<p slot="de">Ich vermisse dich.</p>
	<p slot="en">I miss you.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag saknar dig.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

## The rest in one table

| German | Idiomatic Swedish | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spaß haben | <span lang="sv">ha roligt</span> | *ha spass* |
| spazieren gehen | <span lang="sv">ta en promenad</span>, <span lang="sv">gå en promenad</span> | *gå spatsera* |
| Ich bin einverstanden. | <span lang="sv">Jag håller med.</span> | <span lang="sv">jag är överens</span> without <span lang="sv">med</span> or group context |
| Wie heißt du? | <span lang="sv">Vad heter du?</span> | *hur heter du* |
| Ich hätte gerne einen Kaffee. | <span lang="sv">Jag skulle vilja ha en kaffe.</span> | *jag har gärna* |
| Gern! (as a reply to thanks) | <span lang="sv">Ingen orsak!</span>, <span lang="sv">Varsågod!</span> | *gärna* alone |
| Ich warte auf den Bus. | <span lang="sv">Jag väntar på bussen.</span> | *väntar för* |
| Ich muss nach Hause. | <span lang="sv">Jag måste hem.</span> | *måste gå hem till* |

<span lang="sv">gärna</span> does exist, but it modifies a verb rather than standing alone:
<span lang="sv">Jag kommer gärna.</span>

<span lang="sv">Överens</span> is not a blanket mistake. It works when people are in agreement
with each other: <span lang="sv">Vi är överens</span>, <span lang="sv">Jag är överens med dig</span>. For the
solo response "I agree", <span lang="sv">Jag håller med</span> is the default.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="— Kommer du inte? — Ja, jag kommer."
	correct="— Kommer du inte? — Jo, jag kommer."
	lang="sv"
>
	A negative question gets <span lang="sv">jo</span> when you contradict it, exactly like
	German *doch*. Use <span lang="sv">nej</span> when you confirm the negative proposition:
	<span lang="sv">Nej, jag kommer inte.</span> English has no single-word match for this
	contrast.
</MistakeBox>
