<KeyTakeaway>
- Swedish has one present tense; there is no progressive and no auxiliary
  *do*.
- The definite article is a suffix on the noun, not a separate word:
  <Sv>huset</Sv>.
- Swedish inflects adjectives and inverts the verb after a fronted element,
  where English does neither.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

English gives you most of the vocabulary and almost none of the morphology.
These are the patterns it exports by mistake.

## Building a progressive

<MistakeBox incorrect="Jag är arbetande nu." correct="Jag arbetar nu." lang="sv">
	One present tense covers both "I work" and "I am working". When the ongoing
	action really matters, Swedish uses a construction, not a participle:
	<Sv>Jag håller på att arbeta.</Sv> or <Sv>Jag sitter och arbetar.</Sv> See
	[present tense](../../grammar/verbs/present/).
</MistakeBox>

## Reaching for do-support

| English | Swedish |
| --- | --- |
| Do you speak Swedish? | <Sv>Talar du svenska?</Sv> |
| I don't know. | <Sv>Jag vet inte.</Sv> |
| Where does she live? | <Sv>Var bor hon?</Sv> |
| Didn't you see it? | <Sv>Såg du det inte?</Sv> |

Questions are formed by inversion alone, and negation by adding
<Sv>inte</Sv>. See
[question words](../../grammar/pronouns-function-words/interrogatives/).

## Writing a separate definite article

<MistakeBox incorrect="den hus, det stora hus" correct="huset, det stora huset" lang="sv">
	Definiteness is a suffix: <Sv>hus</Sv> → <Sv>huset</Sv>, <Sv>bil</Sv> →
	<Sv>bilen</Sv>, plural <Sv>husen</Sv>, <Sv>bilarna</Sv>. With an adjective
	both the article *and* the suffix appear — double definiteness. See
	[noun forms](../../grammar/nouns-articles/noun-forms/).
</MistakeBox>

## Dropping adjective agreement

English adjectives never change. Swedish ones change three ways:

| Form | Example |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>en</Sv> noun | <Sv>en stor bil</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett</Sv> noun | <Sv>ett stort hus</Sv> |
| plural / definite | <Sv>stora hus</Sv>, <Sv>det stora huset</Sv> |

See [adjective agreement](../../grammar/adjectives-adverbs/agreement/).

## for and since

| English | Swedish |
| --- | --- |
| I have lived here for three years. | <Sv>Jag har bott här i tre år.</Sv> |
| I have lived here since 2020. | <Sv>Jag har bott här sedan 2020.</Sv> |
| I haven't seen him for three years. | <Sv>Jag har inte sett honom på tre år.</Sv> |

A stretch of time is <Sv>i</Sv>, a starting point is <Sv>sedan</Sv>, and a
negated stretch takes <Sv>på</Sv>.

## Translating prepositions one by one

| English | Swedish | Not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wait for | <Sv>vänta på</Sv> | *vänta för* |
| listen to | <Sv>lyssna på</Sv> | <Sv>lyssna</Sv> with no preposition |
| think about | <Sv>tänka på</Sv> | *tänka om* (= rethink) |
| ask for | <Sv>be om</Sv> | *be för* |
| interested in | <Sv>intresserad av</Sv> | *intresserad i* |
| on Monday | <Sv>på måndag</Sv> | *i måndag* |
| in the morning | <Sv>på morgonen</Sv> | *i morgonen* |

The preposition belongs to the construction. See
[prepositions](../../grammar/pronouns-function-words/prepositions/).

## Keeping English order after a fronted element

<MistakeBox incorrect="Idag jag köper en bil." correct="Idag köper jag en bil." lang="sv">
	A fronted time expression fills the first position, so the finite verb comes
	next and the subject moves behind it. English keeps subject before verb; do
	not copy that. See [V2](../../grammar/sentence-structure/v2/).
</MistakeBox>
