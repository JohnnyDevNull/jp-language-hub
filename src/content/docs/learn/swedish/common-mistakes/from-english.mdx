<KeyTakeaway>
- Swedish has one present tense; there is no progressive and no auxiliary
  *do*.
- The definite article is a suffix on the noun, not a separate word:
  <span lang="sv">huset</span>.
- Swedish inflects adjectives and inverts the verb after a fronted element,
  where English does neither.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

English gives you many recognizable words, but very little help with Swedish
morphology. These are the patterns it exports by mistake.

## Building a progressive

<MistakeBox incorrect="Jag är arbetande nu." correct="Jag arbetar nu." lang="sv">
	One present tense covers both "I work" and "I am working". When the ongoing
	action really matters, Swedish uses a construction, not a participle:
	<span lang="sv">Jag håller på att arbeta.</span> or <span lang="sv">Jag sitter och arbetar.</span> See
	[present tense](../../grammar/verbs-time-forms/present/).
</MistakeBox>

## Reaching for do-support

<LanguageComparison title="Question without do" languages={['en', 'sv']}>
	<p slot="en">Do you speak Swedish?</p>
	<p slot="sv">Talar du svenska?</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Negation without do" languages={['en', 'sv']}>
	<p slot="en">I don't know.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag vet inte.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Question word plus inversion" languages={['en', 'sv']}>
	<p slot="en">Where does she live?</p>
	<p slot="sv">Var bor hon?</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Negative question" languages={['en', 'sv']}>
	<p slot="en">Didn't you see it?</p>
	<p slot="sv">Såg du det inte?</p>
</LanguageComparison>

Direct questions use the existing <span lang="sv">finit verb</span> instead of English *do*. See
[Questions](../../grammar/questions-negation/questions/) for the reusable
frame. Swedish clause negation adds <span lang="sv">inte</span>; choose the negative form on
[Negation](../../grammar/questions-negation/negation/) and place <span lang="sv">inte</span>
with [Placement of *inte*](../../grammar/sentence-structure/inte-placement/).

## Writing a separate definite article

<MistakeBox incorrect="den hus, det stora hus" correct="huset, det stora huset" lang="sv">
	Definiteness is a suffix: <span lang="sv">hus</span> → <span lang="sv">huset</span>, <span lang="sv">bil</span> →
	<span lang="sv">bilen</span>, plural <span lang="sv">husen</span>, <span lang="sv">bilarna</span>. With an adjective
	both the article *and* the suffix appear — double definiteness. See
	[noun forms](../../grammar/nouns-articles/noun-forms/).
</MistakeBox>

## Dropping adjective agreement

English usually leaves adjectives uninflected in the patterns beginners rely
on. Swedish adjectives change by gender, number, and definiteness:

| Form | Example |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">en</span> noun | <span lang="sv">en stor bil</span> |
| <span lang="sv">ett</span> noun | <span lang="sv">ett stort hus</span> |
| plural / definite | <span lang="sv">stora hus</span>, <span lang="sv">det stora huset</span> |

See [adjective agreement](../../grammar/adjectives-adverbs/agreement/).

## for and since

<LanguageComparison title="Duration" languages={['en', 'sv']}>
	<p slot="en">I have lived here for three years.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag har bott här i tre år.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Starting point" languages={['en', 'sv']}>
	<p slot="en">I have lived here since 2020.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag har bott här sedan 2020.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Negated stretch of time" languages={['en', 'sv']}>
	<p slot="en">I haven't seen him for three years.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag har inte sett honom på tre år.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

A stretch of time is <span lang="sv">i</span>, a starting point is <span lang="sv">sedan</span>, and a
negated stretch takes <span lang="sv">på</span>.

## Translating prepositions one by one

These are diagnostic traps, not a second preposition reference. When a phrase
is not listed here, use the focused
[prepositions](../../grammar/prepositions-function-words/prepositions/) page.

| English pattern | Swedish repair | Do not diagnose it as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wait for | <span lang="sv">vänta på</span> | *vänta för* |
| listen to | <span lang="sv">lyssna på</span> | <span lang="sv">lyssna</span> with no preposition |
| think about | <span lang="sv">tänka på</span> | *tänka om* (= rethink) |
| ask for | <span lang="sv">be om</span> | *be för* |
| interested in | <span lang="sv">intresserad av</span> | *intresserad i* |
| on Monday | <span lang="sv">på måndag</span> | *i måndag* |
| in the morning | <span lang="sv">på morgonen</span> | *i morgonen* |

The preposition belongs to the construction. Use this table to spot an English
transfer error, then check
[prepositions](../../grammar/prepositions-function-words/prepositions/) for
the broader choice.

## Keeping English order after a fronted element

<MistakeBox incorrect="Idag jag köper en bil." correct="Idag köper jag en bil." lang="sv">
	A fronted time expression fills the first position, so the <span lang="sv">finit verb</span> comes
	next and the <span lang="sv">subjekt</span> moves behind it. English keeps subject before verb; do
	not copy that. See [V2](../../grammar/sentence-structure/v2/).
</MistakeBox>
