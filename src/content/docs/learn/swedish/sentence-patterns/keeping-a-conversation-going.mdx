<KeyTakeaway>
- Swedish keeps a conversation alive with very short questions:
  <span lang="sv">Hur så?</span>, <span lang="sv">Och sen då?</span>, <span lang="sv">Hur menar du?</span>
- <span lang="sv">Förresten</span> and <span lang="sv">apropå</span> are the two standard topic-shift
  openers; <span lang="sv">apropå</span> links to what was just said, <span lang="sv">förresten</span> does
  not have to.
- An interruption is prefaced, not just started:
  <span lang="sv">Förlåt att jag avbryter, men …</span>
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

These are the phrases that make a conversation feel two-sided. None of them
carries content, which is exactly why they can be learned as fixed units.

## Asking a follow-up question

| Pattern | What it does | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">Hur menar du?</span> | what do you mean | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Hur så?</span> | why is that, how come | spoken |
| <span lang="sv">Och sen då?</span> | and then what | spoken |
| <span lang="sv">Vad hände sen?</span> | what happened next | spoken |
| <span lang="sv">Berätta mer!</span> | tell me more | spoken |
| <span lang="sv">Menar du att …?</span> | checking your understanding | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Kan du utveckla det?</span> | can you elaborate | neutral to formal |

## Continuing and changing topic

| Pattern | What it does | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">Just det, och …</span> | agree, then add | spoken |
| <span lang="sv">Som sagt …</span> | as I said, returning to your point | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Apropå det …</span> | speaking of that | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Förresten …</span> | by the way | spoken |
| <span lang="sv">En annan sak …</span> | another thing | spoken |
| <span lang="sv">För att byta ämne …</span> | to change the subject | neutral |

## Interrupting politely

| Pattern | What it does | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">Förlåt att jag avbryter, men …</span> | sorry to interrupt, but | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Ursäkta, men …</span> | excuse me, but | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Får jag säga en sak?</span> | may I say one thing | neutral |

## Summarizing

| Pattern | What it does | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">Kort sagt …</span> | in short | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Sammanfattningsvis …</span> | in summary | formal, written |
| <span lang="sv">Så det du säger är att …</span> | so what you're saying is | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Om jag förstår dig rätt …</span> | if I understand you correctly | neutral |
| <span lang="sv">Med andra ord …</span> | in other words | neutral |

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="By the way">
	<p slot="de">Übrigens, ich habe Anna getroffen.</p>
	<p slot="en">By the way, I met Anna.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Förresten, jag träffade Anna.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Prefacing an interruption">
	<p slot="de">Entschuldige, dass ich unterbreche, aber …</p>
	<p slot="en">Sorry to interrupt, but …</p>
	<p slot="sv">Förlåt att jag avbryter, men …</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<span lang="sv">Förlåt</span> apologizes for something you are doing; <span lang="sv">ursäkta</span> asks
for attention. Both work here, and the <span lang="sv">att</span>-clause is obligatory in the
first pattern.

<LanguageComparison title="Playing a point back">
	<p slot="de">Du sagst also, dass es zu teuer ist.</p>
	<p slot="en">So what you're saying is that it's too expensive.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Så det du säger är att det är för dyrt.</p>
</LanguageComparison>
