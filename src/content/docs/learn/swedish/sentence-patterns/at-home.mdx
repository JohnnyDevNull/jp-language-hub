<KeyTakeaway>
- Use <Sv>hemma</Sv> for being at home and <Sv>hem</Sv> for going home:
  <Sv>Jag är hemma</Sv>, <Sv>Jag går hem</Sv>.
- Use <Sv>Var är …?</Sv> for missing objects and <Sv>Den ligger …</Sv> /
  <Sv>Det står …</Sv> for where things are.
- Home routines often start with time:
  <Sv>På morgonen dricker jag kaffe.</Sv>
</KeyTakeaway>

This page covers the home situation: finding things, saying what you do there,
and making small guest or household requests without building a long sentence.

## Finding things

| Pattern | Use it when | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>Var är …?</Sv> | asking where something is | neutral |
| <Sv>Har du sett …?</Sv> | asking whether someone has seen it | neutral |
| <Sv>Den ligger på bordet.</Sv> | saying where an item is lying | neutral |
| <Sv>Det står i köket.</Sv> | saying where something stands or is placed | neutral |
| <Sv>Jag hittar inte …</Sv> | saying you cannot find something | neutral |
| <Sv>Kan du lägga den på bordet?</Sv> | asking someone to put it on the table | neutral |

Use <Sv>ligger</Sv> for many loose objects and <Sv>står</Sv> for things that
stand upright, are parked, or are placed somewhere as fixtures.

## Home routines and needs

| Pattern | Use it when | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>Jag är hemma idag.</Sv> | saying you are at home today | neutral |
| <Sv>Jag går hem nu.</Sv> | saying you are going home now | neutral |
| <Sv>På morgonen …</Sv> | talking about morning routines | neutral |
| <Sv>Jag måste städa.</Sv> | saying you have to clean | neutral |
| <Sv>Kan du hjälpa mig?</Sv> | asking for help | neutral |
| <Sv>Det är slut på kaffe.</Sv> | saying something has run out | spoken |

## Guest phrases

| Pattern | Use it when | Register |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>Kom in!</Sv> | inviting someone in | spoken |
| <Sv>Känn dig som hemma.</Sv> | make yourself at home | neutral |
| <Sv>Vill du ha kaffe?</Sv> | offering coffee | neutral |
| <Sv>Kan jag få lite vatten?</Sv> | asking for water | neutral |
| <Sv>Var är toaletten?</Sv> | asking for the bathroom | neutral |

## Tiny home exchange

<p lang="sv">
	A: Var är nycklarna?
	<br />
	B: De ligger på bordet.
	<br />
	A: Tack. Jag går hem nu.
	<br />
	B: Okej, vi ses imorgon!
</p>

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="At home vs going home">
	<p slot="de">Ich bin zu Hause. / Ich gehe nach Hause.</p>
	<p slot="en">I am at home. / I am going home.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag är hemma. / Jag går hem.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German and English both change the whole phrase. Swedish changes the adverb:
<Sv>hemma</Sv> is location, <Sv>hem</Sv> is direction.

<LanguageComparison title="Something has run out">
	<p slot="de">Der Kaffee ist alle.</p>
	<p slot="en">We're out of coffee.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Det är slut på kaffe.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

The Swedish phrase is impersonal: literally "it is finished on coffee". Learn
<Sv>slut på</Sv> as one household pattern.
