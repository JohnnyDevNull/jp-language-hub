<KeyTakeaway>
- Start with pronunciation and en- and ett-words, then word order, then the
  verb tenses. Each step builds on the one before.
- If the full list feels too long, start with the first five pages below.
- The levels A1 to B2 show how far along a page is: A1 is for beginners, B2
  for confident speakers.
</KeyTakeaway>

This page lists the Swedish pages in an order that makes sense for learning.
Each step links to the page that explains the topic; the note next to it says
why the step comes at that point. You can follow the list from the top, or jump
to a short route for one goal.

The steps are grouped by language level, from the scale used for language
courses across Europe
([CEFR levels](../glossary/#cefr)):

| Level | What you can do |
| --- | --- |
| A1 | Beginner: simple sentences about yourself and everyday things. |
| A2 | Basic: short conversations about familiar topics. |
| B1 | Intermediate: you manage most everyday situations. |
| B2 | Upper intermediate: you speak and write with confidence. |

## Your first five pages

If you want a short start, read these five pages first:

1. [Pronunciation basics](../pronunciation-basics/) — in Swedish, a long or a
   short vowel can change what a word means.
2. [Noun gender: en- and ett-words](../grammar/nouns-articles/en-ett/) — every
   noun has <span lang="sv">en</span> or <span lang="sv">ett</span>, and you
   learn it with the word.
3. [Basic word order](../grammar/sentence-structure/basic-word-order/) — how a
   normal Swedish sentence is built.
4. [Verb in second position (V2)](../grammar/sentence-structure/v2/) — the verb
   stays in second place, even when the sentence starts with a time or a place.
5. [Present tense](../grammar/verbs-time-forms/present/) — one verb form for
   everyone: <span lang="sv">jag köper, du köper, hon köper</span>.
