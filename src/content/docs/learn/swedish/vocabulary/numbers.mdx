<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish numbers are practical vocabulary for prices, dates, time, addresses,
and quantities. Once the first twenty are secure, most everyday forms follow a
predictable pattern.

## Use this now

| If you need to… | Use |
| --- | --- |
| count from 0 to 20 | memorize the base list |
| build 21, 42, or 156 | ten before unit, no <Sv>och</Sv>: <Sv>tjugoett</Sv>, <Sv>fyrtiotvå</Sv>, <Sv>hundrafemtiosex</Sv> |
| count one noun by itself | choose <Sv>en</Sv> or <Sv>ett</Sv> by noun gender |
| build 21, 31, 41 before a noun | normally use final <Sv>-en</Sv>: <Sv>trettioen fartyg</Sv> |
| use conventional t-word number contexts | use <Sv>-ett</Sv>: <Sv>tjugoett år</Sv>, <Sv>nummer tjugoett</Sv>, <Sv>tjugoett öre</Sv> |
| write dates | use the ordinal after <Sv>den</Sv>: <Sv>den tredje maj</Sv> |
| write decimals | use a comma: <Sv>3,5</Sv> |

## Cardinal numbers

| Number | Swedish | Number | Swedish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | <Sv>noll</Sv> | 11 | <Sv>elva</Sv> |
| 1 | <Sv>en / ett</Sv> | 12 | <Sv>tolv</Sv> |
| 2 | <Sv>två</Sv> | 13 | <Sv>tretton</Sv> |
| 3 | <Sv>tre</Sv> | 14 | <Sv>fjorton</Sv> |
| 4 | <Sv>fyra</Sv> | 15 | <Sv>femton</Sv> |
| 5 | <Sv>fem</Sv> | 16 | <Sv>sexton</Sv> |
| 6 | <Sv>sex</Sv> | 17 | <Sv>sjutton</Sv> |
| 7 | <Sv>sju</Sv> | 18 | <Sv>arton</Sv> |
| 8 | <Sv>åtta</Sv> | 19 | <Sv>nitton</Sv> |
| 9 | <Sv>nio</Sv> | 20 | <Sv>tjugo</Sv> |
| 10 | <Sv>tio</Sv> | | |

The tens are formed with <Sv>-tio</Sv>:

| Number | Swedish | Number | Swedish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 30 | <Sv>trettio</Sv> | 70 | <Sv>sjuttio</Sv> |
| 40 | <Sv>fyrtio</Sv> | 80 | <Sv>åttio</Sv> |
| 50 | <Sv>femtio</Sv> | 90 | <Sv>nittio</Sv> |
| 60 | <Sv>sextio</Sv> | 100 | <Sv>hundra</Sv> |

Note the spelling <Sv>fyrtio</Sv> for 40, which does not follow
<Sv>fyra</Sv>. Several number words also contain the sj- and
tj-sounds: <Sv>sju</Sv>, <Sv>sjutton</Sv>,
<Sv>sjuttio</Sv>, <Sv>tjugo</Sv>. See
[pronunciation basics](../../pronunciation-basics/).

## Building larger numbers

Everyday compound numbers are assembled from the largest part down. Two-part
and three-part compounds are normally written as one word:

- 21 — <Sv>tjugoett</Sv>
- 42 — <Sv>fyrtiotvå</Sv>
- 156 — <Sv>hundrafemtiosex</Sv>
- 3 000 — <Sv>tretusen</Sv>

<RuleBox title="Swedish does not reverse the digits">
	German says <span lang="de">einundzwanzig</span>, with the unit first.
	Swedish, like English, says the ten first:
	<Sv>tjugoett</Sv> — *twenty-one*. There is no
	<Sv>och</Sv> between the parts.
</RuleBox>

<Sv>Hundra</Sv> and <Sv>tusen</Sv> can stand alone
or take <Sv>ett</Sv> for emphasis or clarity:
<Sv>hundra kronor</Sv> or
<Sv>etthundra kronor</Sv>.

## en or ett for "one"

By itself, the number 1 agrees with the gender of the noun it counts, exactly
like the indefinite article:

- <Sv>en bil</Sv> — one car
- <Sv>ett hus</Sv> — one house

After a ten, however, the final 1 is normally <Sv>-en</Sv>, even before many
ett-words:

- <Sv>tjugoen kronor</Sv> — 21 kronor
- <Sv>trettioen fartyg</Sv> — 31 vessels, even though <Sv>ett fartyg</Sv> is
  an ett-word

Use <Sv>-ett</Sv> in conventional t-word number contexts, especially
<Sv>år</Sv>, <Sv>nummer</Sv>, <Sv>öre</Sv>, and clock readings:

- <Sv>tjugoett år</Sv>
- <Sv>nummer tjugoett</Sv>
- <Sv>tjugoett öre</Sv>
- <Sv>klockan tjugoett</Sv>

Every other number is uninflected. See
[en / ett](../../grammar/nouns-articles/en-ett/) for noun gender.

## Ordinal numbers

| Number | Swedish | Number | Swedish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st | <Sv>första</Sv> | 11th | <Sv>elfte</Sv> |
| 2nd | <Sv>andra</Sv> | 12th | <Sv>tolfte</Sv> |
| 3rd | <Sv>tredje</Sv> | 13th | <Sv>trettonde</Sv> |
| 4th | <Sv>fjärde</Sv> | 14th | <Sv>fjortonde</Sv> |
| 5th | <Sv>femte</Sv> | 15th | <Sv>femtonde</Sv> |
| 6th | <Sv>sjätte</Sv> | 16th | <Sv>sextonde</Sv> |
| 7th | <Sv>sjunde</Sv> | 17th | <Sv>sjuttonde</Sv> |
| 8th | <Sv>åttonde</Sv> | 18th | <Sv>artonde</Sv> |
| 9th | <Sv>nionde</Sv> | 19th | <Sv>nittonde</Sv> |
| 10th | <Sv>tionde</Sv> | 20th | <Sv>tjugonde</Sv> |

The first twelve are worth memorizing. From 13 to 19, Swedish usually adds
<Sv>-de</Sv> to the cardinal number:
<Sv>trettonde</Sv>, <Sv>fjortonde</Sv>,
<Sv>nittonde</Sv>. Full tens have their own forms, such as
<Sv>tjugo → tjugonde</Sv> and
<Sv>trettio → trettionde</Sv>.

In a compound ordinal, **only the last element** becomes an ordinal. If the
final element is 1 or 2, use <Sv>första</Sv> or <Sv>andra</Sv>:

- 21st — <Sv>tjugoförsta</Sv>
- 42nd — <Sv>fyrtioandra</Sv>
- 100th — <Sv>hundrade</Sv>

## Ordinals in dates and writing

Ordinals normally follow <Sv>den</Sv>:

- <Sv>Vi ses den första maj.</Sv> — *We will meet on 1 May.*
- <Sv>Det är hennes tredje bok.</Sv> — *It is her third book.*

Written as digits, Swedish marks the ordinal with a colon plus the ending:
<Sv>1:a</Sv> and <Sv>2:a</Sv> for the forms in
<Sv>-a</Sv>, and <Sv>3:e</Sv>,
<Sv>4:e</Sv>, <Sv>24:e</Sv> for the rest.

## Millions, billions, and decimals

<Sv>Miljon</Sv> and <Sv>miljard</Sv> are nouns:
they take an article and a plural.

| Value | Swedish | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10⁶ | <Sv>en miljon</Sv> | <Sv>två miljoner</Sv> |
| 10⁹ | <Sv>en miljard</Sv> | <Sv>två miljarder</Sv> |
| 10¹² | <Sv>en biljon</Sv> | <Sv>två biljoner</Sv> |

<MistakeBox title="A dangerous false friend">
Swedish <Sv>en biljon</Sv> is 10¹², not a billion. English
*billion* is <Sv>en miljard</Sv>. Swedish counts the way German
does: <span lang="de">Milliarde</span> → <Sv>miljard</Sv>,
<span lang="de">Billion</span> → <Sv>biljon</Sv>.
</MistakeBox>

Like German, Swedish uses a **comma** as the decimal separator and a space to
group thousands: <Sv>3,5</Sv> is read
<Sv>tre komma fem</Sv>, and 10 000 is written with a space.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <Sv>ettochtjugo</Sv> — German digit order
	  ✅ <Sv>tjugoett</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>tjugo och ett</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>tjugoett</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>tjugoett kronor</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>tjugoen kronor</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>trettioett fartyg</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>trettioen fartyg</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>den 3:a maj</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>den 3:e maj</Sv>
	- Do not make both parts of a compound ordinal an ordinal:
	  <Sv>❌ fyrtiondeandra</Sv>,
	  <Sv>✅ fyrtioandra</Sv>.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Drill the numbers you actually need first: prices, house numbers, years, and
your own phone number. Say them aloud in Swedish rhythm —
<Sv>tjugoett, tjugotvå, tjugotre</Sv> — so the ten-before-unit
order becomes automatic instead of translated.
</MemoryTip>
