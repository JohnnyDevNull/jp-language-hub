<KeyTakeaway>
- Clock time runs through five words only: <Sv>klockan</Sv>, <Sv>över</Sv>,
  <Sv>i</Sv>, <Sv>halv</Sv> and <Sv>kvart</Sv>.
- <Sv>halv</Sv> counts towards the coming hour, exactly like German
  <span lang="de">halb</span>: <Sv>halv tre</Sv> is 2:30, not 3:30.
- Minutes are counted from the nearest reference point, which may be the half
  hour: <Sv>tio i halv nio</Sv> is 8:20.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish clock time is a small closed system. Learn the five words and the two
directions they point in, and every time of day follows.

## Core rule

> The hour is named with <Sv>klockan</Sv> plus the numeral. Minutes attach to a
> reference point with <Sv>över</Sv> (after) or <Sv>i</Sv> (before), and the
> reference point can be a full hour or a half hour.

## Asking and answering

<LanguageComparison title="Asking the time">
	<p slot="de">Wie spät ist es?</p>
	<p slot="en">What time is it?</p>
	<p slot="sv">Vad är klockan?</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Naming a full hour">
	<p slot="de">Es ist neun Uhr.</p>
	<p slot="en">It is nine o'clock.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Klockan är nio.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

Literally, <Sv>Klockan är nio</Sv> is "the clock is nine". <Sv>Klockan</Sv> is
the definite form of <Sv>en klocka</Sv>, so no separate word for "o'clock" is
needed.

## Minutes after and before

Use <Sv>över</Sv> for minutes after the hour and <Sv>i</Sv> for minutes before
the next hour:

<p lang="sv">Klockan är sju över två.</p>
<p lang="sv">Klockan är fem i sex.</p>

| Swedish | Clock |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>sju över två</Sv> | 2:07 |
| <Sv>fem i sex</Sv> | 5:55 |
| <Sv>tjugo över tre</Sv> | 3:20 |

## Half hours

<Sv>halv</Sv> points at the hour it is counting towards, not at the hour just
passed. Minutes can then be measured from that half-hour point in both
directions:

| Swedish | Clock |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>halv nio</Sv> | 8:30 |
| <Sv>tio i halv nio</Sv> | 8:20 |
| <Sv>tio över halv nio</Sv> | 8:40 |
| <Sv>halv tre</Sv> | 2:30 |

## Quarter hours

| Swedish | Clock |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>kvart över fyra</Sv> | 4:15 |
| <Sv>kvart i sju</Sv> | 6:45 |

## Written and spoken time

Timetables, opening hours and appointments are written digitally with a period:
<Sv>14.30</Sv>. Read aloud that becomes <Sv>fjorton och trettio</Sv>, while the
spoken everyday form for the same moment is <Sv>halv tre</Sv>.

## Quick pattern

| Meaning | Swedish pattern |
| --- | --- |
| exact hour | <Sv>Klockan är nio.</Sv> |
| after the hour | <Sv>X över Y</Sv> |
| before the next hour | <Sv>X i Y</Sv> |
| half to the next hour | <Sv>halv Y</Sv> |
| quarter after | <Sv>kvart över Y</Sv> |
| quarter before | <Sv>kvart i Y</Sv> |

## Direct comparison

The half hour is where the three languages genuinely diverge: German counts
forward like Swedish, English counts back from the hour just passed.

<LanguageComparison title="8:30">
	<p slot="de">halb neun</p>
	<p slot="en">half past eight</p>
	<p slot="sv">halv nio</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="8:20">
	<p slot="de">zehn vor halb neun</p>
	<p slot="en">twenty past eight</p>
	<p slot="sv">tio i halv nio</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="6:45">
	<p slot="de">Viertel vor sieben</p>
	<p slot="en">quarter to seven</p>
	<p slot="sv">kvart i sju</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="halv tre = 3:30"
	correct="halv tre = 2:30"
	lang="sv"
>
	English "half three" and Swedish <Sv>halv tre</Sv> name different moments.
	Also do not derive <Sv>över</Sv> and <Sv>i</Sv> from a general preposition
	rule; clock time uses its own pattern.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Take the German route: <Sv>halv</Sv> behaves like
<span lang="de">halb</span> and the numeral is the hour you are heading for.
Coming from English, add "past" and subtract one: <Sv>halv nio</Sv> = "half
past eight".
</MemoryTip>
