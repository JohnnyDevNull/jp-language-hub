<KeyTakeaway>
- Swedish names the route through the family: <span lang="sv">mormor</span> is your mother's
  mother, <span lang="sv">farmor</span> your father's mother.
- Read a kinship compound from the right. The last part is the person, the parts
  before it are the way to them.
- <span lang="sv">mamma</span> and <span lang="sv">pappa</span> are the everyday words.
  <span lang="sv">mor</span> and <span lang="sv">far</span> sound formal outside compounds.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish has no single word for grandmother, aunt, uncle, niece or nephew. It
builds each one out of the relatives in between, so the word already says which
side of the family you mean. That makes the list long but almost free to learn.

## Use this now

| You mean | Swedish |
| --- | --- |
| my father's mother | <span lang="sv">min farmor</span> |
| my mother's mother | <span lang="sv">min mormor</span> |
| my mother's sister | <span lang="sv">min moster</span> |
| my father's brother | <span lang="sv">min farbror</span> |
| my sister's son | <span lang="sv">min systerson</span> |
| my grandchild | <span lang="sv">mitt barnbarn</span> |
| my cousin | <span lang="sv">min kusin</span> |

## Core rule

> A kinship compound names the path, not the relationship. The final element is
> the person; everything in front of it is the chain leading to them, counted
> outwards from you.

<span lang="sv">mor</span> plus <span lang="sv">mor</span> gives <span lang="sv">mormor</span>,
<span lang="sv">far</span> plus <span lang="sv">bror</span> gives <span lang="sv">farbror</span>. Nothing
is inserted between the parts. <span lang="sv">moster</span> and <span lang="sv">faster</span> are
the same rule worn down by use: they started as <span lang="sv">morsyster</span> and
<span lang="sv">farsyster</span>.

## Parents, children and siblings

| Swedish | Plural | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">en mamma, en mor</span> | <span lang="sv">mammor, mödrar</span> | mother |
| <span lang="sv">en pappa, en far</span> | <span lang="sv">pappor, fäder</span> | father |
| <span lang="sv">en förälder</span> | <span lang="sv">föräldrar</span> | parent |
| <span lang="sv">ett barn</span> | <span lang="sv">barn</span> | child |
| <span lang="sv">en dotter</span> | <span lang="sv">döttrar</span> | daughter |
| <span lang="sv">en son</span> | <span lang="sv">söner</span> | son |
| <span lang="sv">ett syskon</span> | <span lang="sv">syskon</span> | sibling |
| <span lang="sv">en syster</span> | <span lang="sv">systrar</span> | sister |
| <span lang="sv">en bror</span> | <span lang="sv">bröder</span> | brother |

## Grandparents

| Swedish | Plural | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">en mormor</span> | <span lang="sv">mormödrar</span> | mother's mother |
| <span lang="sv">en morfar</span> | <span lang="sv">morfäder</span> | mother's father |
| <span lang="sv">en farmor</span> | <span lang="sv">farmödrar</span> | father's mother |
| <span lang="sv">en farfar</span> | <span lang="sv">farfäder</span> | father's father |

Collectively they are <span lang="sv">mor- och farföräldrar</span>, or one side at a time:
<span lang="sv">morföräldrar</span> and <span lang="sv">farföräldrar</span>. One generation further
back, prefix <span lang="sv">gammel-</span>: <span lang="sv">gammelmormor</span>,
<span lang="sv">gammelfarfar</span>.

## Aunts, uncles and cousins

| Swedish | Plural | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">en moster</span> | <span lang="sv">mostrar</span> | mother's sister |
| <span lang="sv">en faster</span> | <span lang="sv">fastrar</span> | father's sister |
| <span lang="sv">en morbror</span> | <span lang="sv">morbröder</span> | mother's brother |
| <span lang="sv">en farbror</span> | <span lang="sv">farbröder</span> | father's brother |
| <span lang="sv">en kusin</span> | <span lang="sv">kusiner</span> | cousin, either side |
| <span lang="sv">en syssling</span> | <span lang="sv">sysslingar</span> | second cousin |

An aunt or uncle by marriage has no word of its own. Name the relative you are
related to: <span lang="sv">min mosters man</span>.

## Nieces, nephews and grandchildren

| Swedish | Plural | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">ett syskonbarn</span> | <span lang="sv">syskonbarn</span> | sibling's child |
| <span lang="sv">en brorsdotter</span> | <span lang="sv">brorsdöttrar</span> | brother's daughter |
| <span lang="sv">en brorson</span> | <span lang="sv">brorsöner</span> | brother's son |
| <span lang="sv">en systerdotter</span> | <span lang="sv">systerdöttrar</span> | sister's daughter |
| <span lang="sv">en systerson</span> | <span lang="sv">systersöner</span> | sister's son |
| <span lang="sv">ett barnbarn</span> | <span lang="sv">barnbarn</span> | grandchild |
| <span lang="sv">ett barnbarnsbarn</span> | <span lang="sv">barnbarnsbarn</span> | great-grandchild |

The same path logic names a single grandchild: <span lang="sv">sonson</span>,
<span lang="sv">sondotter</span>, <span lang="sv">dotterson</span>, <span lang="sv">dotterdotter</span>.

## Partners and in-laws

| Swedish | Plural | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">en man, en make</span> | <span lang="sv">män, makar</span> | husband |
| <span lang="sv">en fru, en maka</span> | <span lang="sv">fruar, makor</span> | wife |
| <span lang="sv">en sambo</span> | <span lang="sv">sambor</span> | live-in partner |
| <span lang="sv">en pojkvän</span> | <span lang="sv">pojkvänner</span> | boyfriend |
| <span lang="sv">en flickvän</span> | <span lang="sv">flickvänner</span> | girlfriend |
| <span lang="sv">en svärmor</span> | <span lang="sv">svärmödrar</span> | mother-in-law |
| <span lang="sv">en svärfar</span> | <span lang="sv">svärfäder</span> | father-in-law |
| <span lang="sv">en svärdotter</span> | <span lang="sv">svärdöttrar</span> | daughter-in-law |
| <span lang="sv">en svärson</span> | <span lang="sv">svärsöner</span> | son-in-law |
| <span lang="sv">en svåger</span> | <span lang="sv">svågrar</span> | brother-in-law |
| <span lang="sv">en svägerska</span> | <span lang="sv">svägerskor</span> | sister-in-law |

<span lang="sv">svär-</span> marks the relatives you gain by marriage, so both together are
<span lang="sv">svärföräldrar</span>.

## Step-family

<span lang="sv">styv-</span> is the formal prefix: <span lang="sv">styvmor</span>,
<span lang="sv">styvfar</span>, <span lang="sv">styvbarn</span>, <span lang="sv">styvsyskon</span>. Everyday
Swedish prefers <span lang="sv">bonus-</span>, which carries no coldness:
<span lang="sv">bonusmamma</span>, <span lang="sv">bonuspappa</span>, <span lang="sv">bonusbarn</span>, and
the household as a whole is <span lang="sv">en bonusfamilj</span>. A sibling sharing one
parent is <span lang="sv">ett halvsyskon</span>.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="One aunt, two words">
	<p slot="de">Meine Tante wohnt in Malmö.</p>
	<p slot="en">My aunt lives in Malmö.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Min moster bor i Malmö. / Min faster bor i Malmö.</p>

You cannot avoid the choice by picking a neutral word, because there is none.
If you do not know the side, ask.

<MistakeBox incorrect="Det här är min mor." correct="Det här är min mamma." lang="sv">
	German <span lang="de">Mutter</span> is the neutral word, so learners reach for
	<span lang="sv">mor</span>. In Swedish it is the other way round:
	<span lang="sv">mamma</span> is neutral in speech and in writing, while
	<span lang="sv">mor</span> and <span lang="sv">far</span> sound formal or old outside compounds
	and fixed phrases.
</MistakeBox>

<SpecialRule language="Swedish" title="Where Swedish splits and merges">
	Swedish splits what German and English merge, and merges what they split.
	Grandparent, aunt, uncle, niece, nephew and grandchild all force a choice of
	side. But <span lang="sv">kusin</span> covers both German <span lang="de">Cousin</span> and
	<span lang="de">Cousine</span>, and <span lang="sv">ett syskon</span> gives Swedish a singular
	that German <span lang="de">Geschwister</span> lacks.
</SpecialRule>

<MemoryTip>
	Read the compound backwards: <span lang="sv">farmor</span> is the <span lang="sv">mor</span> you
	reach through your <span lang="sv">far</span>. The trick keeps working downwards —
	<span lang="sv">dotterdotter</span> is the daughter of your daughter.
</MemoryTip>
