---
title: Swedish en and ett — quick guide
description: A short route into Swedish noun gender, forms, and adjective agreement.
---

Most Swedish nouns belong to one of two grammatical genders:
<span lang="sv">en</span> or <span lang="sv">ett</span>.

- <span lang="sv">en bil</span> → <span lang="sv">bilen</span>
- <span lang="sv">ett hus</span> → <span lang="sv">huset</span>
- <span lang="sv">en stor bil</span>, but <span lang="sv">ett stort hus</span>

The gender is part of the noun and is not always predictable. Learn the article
with the noun: <span lang="sv">en bil</span>, not only
<span lang="sv">bil</span>.

Two shortcuts help when you have to guess:

- A compound always takes the gender of its **last** element:
  <span lang="sv">en cykel</span> + <span lang="sv">ett hjul</span> →
  <span lang="sv">ett cykelhjul</span>. This rule has no exceptions.
- Some endings are regular. <span lang="sv">-else</span>,
  <span lang="sv">-het</span>, <span lang="sv">-ning</span>, and
  <span lang="sv">-tion</span> are normally en-words;
  <span lang="sv">-ande</span>, <span lang="sv">-eri</span>,
  <span lang="sv">-um</span>, and <span lang="sv">-ment</span> are normally
  ett-words. See the
  [full list of endings](../../grammar/nouns-articles/en-ett/#endings-that-point-to-a-gender).
