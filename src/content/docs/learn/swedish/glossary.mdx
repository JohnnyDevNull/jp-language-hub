Every page explains its grammar words where they first appear. This page
collects them, so you can look one up again. Swedish terms are listed under
their Swedish name, because that is the name you meet in Swedish grammar books
and in the sentence tables on these pages.

## <span lang="sv">bisats</span>

**Subordinate clause.** A part-sentence that cannot stand alone. It starts
with a word like <span lang="sv">att</span>, <span lang="sv">eftersom</span> or
<span lang="sv">om</span>.

Example: <span lang="sv">Jag stannar hemma **eftersom jag är sjuk**.</span>

Taught on: [Negation in subordinate clauses (BIFF)](../grammar/sentence-structure/biff/)

## <span lang="sv">bisatsinledare</span>

**The word that starts a subordinate clause**, such as
<span lang="sv">att</span> (that), <span lang="sv">eftersom</span> (because),
<span lang="sv">om</span> (if) or <span lang="sv">när</span> (when).

Example: <span lang="sv">Jag vet **att** hon kommer.</span>

Taught on: [Negation in subordinate clauses (BIFF)](../grammar/sentence-structure/biff/)

## CEFR

**CEFR levels.** A scale used for language courses across Europe: A1
beginner, A2 basic, B1 intermediate, B2 upper intermediate, C1 advanced. Each
page on this site says which level it is for.

Example: a page marked A1 is for your first weeks of Swedish.

Taught on: [Learning path](../learning-path/)

## <span lang="sv">dubbel bestämdhet</span>

**Double definiteness: "the" marked twice.** When an adjective stands before a noun with "the",
Swedish uses a separate word in front and the ending on the noun.

Example: <span lang="sv">**den** stora bil**en**</span> (the big car)

Taught on: [Noun gender: en- and ett-words](../grammar/nouns-articles/en-ett/)

## <span lang="sv">efterled</span>

**The last part of a compound word.** It decides the gender of the whole word.

Example: <span lang="sv">sommar + **hus** → ett sommarhus</span>, because
<span lang="sv">ett hus</span>.

Taught on: [Noun gender: en- and ett-words](../grammar/nouns-articles/en-ett/)

## <span lang="sv">finit verb</span>

**Conjugated verb.** The verb that shows the tense, such as
<span lang="sv">köper</span> (buys) or <span lang="sv">köpte</span> (bought). In
a Swedish statement it always comes second.

Example: <span lang="sv">Idag **köper** jag en bil.</span>

Taught on: [Verb in second position (V2)](../grammar/sentence-structure/v2/)

## <span lang="sv">fundament</span>

**The first part of a Swedish statement**, the box before the conjugated verb.
It can be the subject, a time, a place or something else — but only one
sentence part.

Example: <span lang="sv">**Efter jobbet** köper jag mat.</span>

Taught on: [Verb in second position (V2)](../grammar/sentence-structure/v2/)

## <span lang="sv">genus</span>

**Noun gender.** Every Swedish noun is an en-word or an ett-word. German has
three genders (<span lang="de">der</span>, <span lang="de">die</span>,
<span lang="de">das</span>), English has none.

Example: <span lang="sv">**en** bil</span>, <span lang="sv">**ett** hus</span>

Taught on: [Noun gender: en- and ett-words](../grammar/nouns-articles/en-ett/)

## <span lang="sv">infinit verb</span>

**A verb form without tense.** The basic form (<span lang="sv">köpa</span>) or
the form after <span lang="sv">har</span> (<span lang="sv">köpt</span>). It stays
further back in the sentence.

Example: <span lang="sv">Idag vill jag **köpa** en bil.</span>

Taught on: [Verb in second position (V2)](../grammar/sentence-structure/v2/)

## <span lang="sv">satsadverbial</span>

**A small word that comments on the whole sentence**, such as
<span lang="sv">inte</span> (not), <span lang="sv">aldrig</span> (never) or
<span lang="sv">kanske</span> (maybe).

Example: <span lang="sv">… eftersom jag **inte** kommer.</span>

Taught on: [Negation in subordinate clauses (BIFF)](../grammar/sentence-structure/biff/)
