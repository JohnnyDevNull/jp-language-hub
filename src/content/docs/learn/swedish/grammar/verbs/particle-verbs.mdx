A particle verb is a verb plus a short stressed word such as
<span lang="sv">på</span>, <span lang="sv">av</span>,
<span lang="sv">om</span>, <span lang="sv">ut</span>, or
<span lang="sv">med</span>. Together they form one unit of meaning that the
two parts do not reveal on their own:
<span lang="sv">tycka om</span> means *to like*, not *to think about*.

Particle verbs are extremely frequent in everyday Swedish, so they are worth
learning as complete units from the beginning.

## The particle carries the stress

Stress is the most reliable way to identify a particle:

- <span lang="sv">Jag tycker **om** henne.</span> — *I like her.* The particle
  <span lang="sv">om</span> is stressed.
- <span lang="sv">Jag **tit**tar på henne.</span> — *I am looking at her.*
  Here <span lang="sv">på</span> is an unstressed preposition.

<RuleBox title="Rule">
If the small word after the verb is stressed and changes the meaning of the
verb, it is a particle. If it is unstressed and simply introduces the object,
it is a preposition.
</RuleBox>

## Word order: the particle stays with the verb

The particle follows the verb directly and comes **before** the object, no
matter whether the object is a noun or a pronoun:

- <span lang="sv">Han slog på radion.</span> — *He switched on the radio.*
- <span lang="sv">Han slog på den.</span> — *He switched it on.*

This is the point where English habits cause errors: English can split the
verb (*switch it on*), Swedish cannot.

In a compound tense the particle follows the supine:

<p lang="sv">Jag har tagit med boken.</p>

### Negation goes between verb and particle

<span lang="sv">Inte</span> is placed after the finite verb and therefore
**before** the particle:

- <span lang="sv">Jag tycker inte om henne.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Han slog inte på radion.</span>

See [Placement of *inte*](../../sentence-structure/inte-placement/) for
the general pattern.

## Direct comparison

| Language | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| German | separable prefix moves to the end of the clause | <span lang="de">Ich rufe ihn an.</span> |
| English | particle can be split from the verb by a pronoun | *I call him up.* |
| Swedish | particle stays next to the verb, before the object | <span lang="sv">Jag ringer upp honom.</span> |

For German speakers this is good news: nothing has to be moved to the end of
the sentence. The particle simply stays where the verb is.

## Frequent particle verbs

| Swedish | English | German |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">tycka om</span> | to like | <span lang="de">mögen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">komma ihåg</span> | to remember | <span lang="de">sich erinnern</span> |
| <span lang="sv">komma på</span> | to think of, to realize | <span lang="de">einfallen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">se ut</span> | to look, to appear | <span lang="de">aussehen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">känna igen</span> | to recognize | <span lang="de">wiedererkennen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">ta med</span> | to bring along | <span lang="de">mitnehmen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">ta på sig</span> | to put on (clothes) | <span lang="de">anziehen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">ta av sig</span> | to take off (clothes) | <span lang="de">ausziehen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">ta reda på</span> | to find out | <span lang="de">herausfinden</span> |
| <span lang="sv">slå på</span> | to switch on | <span lang="de">einschalten</span> |
| <span lang="sv">stänga av</span> | to switch off | <span lang="de">ausschalten</span> |
| <span lang="sv">tala om</span> | to tell, to inform | <span lang="de">erzählen, mitteilen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">hålla på</span> | to be busy doing something | <span lang="de">gerade dabei sein</span> |
| <span lang="sv">passa på</span> | to take the opportunity, to watch out | <span lang="de">aufpassen, die Gelegenheit nutzen</span> |
| <span lang="sv">hitta på</span> | to make up, to come up with | <span lang="de">sich ausdenken</span> |

Store each entry with its particle, exactly as you would store a noun with its
gender. Several of these entries cannot be reduced to a single translation:
what follows the particle decides the meaning.

## The same verb in different constructions

### <span lang="sv">hålla på</span>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">hålla på med</span> + noun | to be busy with | <span lang="sv">Vad håller du på med?</span> — *What are you doing?* |
| <span lang="sv">hålla på att</span> + infinitive | to be in the middle of doing | <span lang="sv">Jag håller på att laga mat.</span> — *I am cooking right now.* |
| <span lang="sv">hålla på att</span> + infinitive | to almost do something | <span lang="sv">Jag höll på att ramla.</span> — *I almost fell.* |
| on its own | to keep at it | <span lang="sv">Han höll på i timmar.</span> — *He kept at it for hours.* |

The last two rows share the same construction, so only the context separates
them. A present tense usually describes an ongoing action, while a preterite
with a verb such as <span lang="sv">ramla</span> or
<span lang="sv">tappa</span> usually means *almost*.

### <span lang="sv">passa på</span>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">passa på att</span> + infinitive | to seize the opportunity | <span lang="sv">Passa på att köpa biljetter nu!</span> — *Take the chance to buy tickets now!* |
| on its own, often imperative | to pay attention, to watch out | <span lang="sv">Passa på när du går över gatan.</span> — *Pay attention when you cross the street.* |

Without the particle, <span lang="sv">passa</span> is a different verb
altogether: <span lang="sv">Byxorna passar.</span> — *The trousers fit.*

### <span lang="sv">komma på</span>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| + noun | to come up with | <span lang="sv">Jag kom på en bra idé.</span> — *I came up with a good idea.* |
| + <span lang="sv">att</span> clause | to realize, to remember suddenly | <span lang="sv">Jag kom på att jag glömt nyckeln.</span> — *I realized I had forgotten the key.* |

### <span lang="sv">se ut</span>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">se ut som</span> | to look like | <span lang="sv">Han ser ut som sin pappa.</span> — *He looks like his father.* |
| <span lang="sv">se ut att</span> | to seem to | <span lang="sv">Det ser ut att bli regn.</span> — *It looks like rain.* |
| <span lang="sv">hur ser … ut</span> | how something looks or stands | <span lang="sv">Hur ser det ut med tiden?</span> — *How are we doing for time?* |

### <span lang="sv">ta på sig</span>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| + clothing | to put on | <span lang="sv">Ta på dig jackan.</span> — *Put on your jacket.* |
| + abstract noun | to take on, to accept | <span lang="sv">Han tog på sig ansvaret.</span> — *He took on the responsibility.* |

## Separated and prefixed forms

Some particle verbs also exist as a written-together prefixed verb. The
prefixed form is usually more formal and often means something noticeably
different:

| Separated | Prefixed |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">tala om</span> — to tell | <span lang="sv">tilltala</span> — to address someone, to appeal to |
| <span lang="sv">gå av</span> — to get off, to break | <span lang="sv">avgå</span> — to resign, to depart |
| <span lang="sv">gå ut</span> — to go out | <span lang="sv">utgå</span> — to be omitted, to proceed from |

Do not treat the two forms as interchangeable. Learn the one you actually need
and check the other separately.

When a particle verb becomes an adjective or a noun, the parts are written
together: <span lang="sv">radion är påslagen</span>,
<span lang="sv">tågets avgång</span>.

<MemoryTip>
Say the verb aloud and listen for the beat. <span lang="sv">tycka **OM**</span>
has its weight on the particle; <span lang="sv">**TIT**ta på</span> has it on
the verb. The stress tells you which of the two you are dealing with.
</MemoryTip>

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Jag tycker om inte henne.</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Jag tycker inte om henne.</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Han slog den på.</span> — English word order
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Han slog på den.</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Jag tilltalar dig att jag kommer.</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Jag talar om att jag kommer.</span>
	- Do not translate a German separable verb by moving the particle to the end
	  of the clause. Swedish keeps it next to the verb.
	- Do not learn a single translation for
	  <span lang="sv">hålla på</span> or <span lang="sv">passa på</span>. Learn
	  the construction that follows the particle.
</MistakeBox>
