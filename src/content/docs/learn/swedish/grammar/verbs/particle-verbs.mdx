<KeyTakeaway>
- A particle is stressed and changes the meaning of the verb; an unstressed
  small word after the verb is a preposition.
- The particle stays next to the verb and before the object, also before a
  pronoun: <Sv>Han slog på den.</Sv>
- <Sv>Inte</Sv> comes between the finite verb and the particle:
  <Sv>Jag tycker inte om henne.</Sv>
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

A particle verb is a verb plus a short stressed word such as
<Sv>på</Sv>, <Sv>av</Sv>,
<Sv>om</Sv>, <Sv>ut</Sv>, or
<Sv>med</Sv>. Together they form one unit of meaning that the
two parts do not reveal on their own:
<Sv>tycka om</Sv> means *to like*, not *to think about*.

Particle verbs are extremely frequent in everyday Swedish, so they are worth
learning as complete units from the beginning.

## The particle carries the stress

Stress is the most reliable way to identify a particle:

- <Sv>Jag tycker **om** henne.</Sv> — *I like her.* The particle
  <Sv>om</Sv> is stressed.
- <Sv>Jag **tit**tar på henne.</Sv> — *I am looking at her.*
  Here <Sv>på</Sv> is an unstressed preposition.

<RuleBox title="Rule">
If the small word after the verb is stressed and changes the meaning of the
verb, it is a particle. If it is unstressed and simply introduces the object,
it is a preposition.
</RuleBox>

## Word order: the particle stays with the verb

The particle follows the verb directly and comes **before** the object, no
matter whether the object is a noun or a pronoun:

- <Sv>Han slog på radion.</Sv> — *He switched on the radio.*
- <Sv>Han slog på den.</Sv> — *He switched it on.*

This is the point where English habits cause errors: English can split the
verb (*switch it on*), Swedish cannot.

In a compound tense the particle follows the supine:

<p lang="sv">Jag har tagit med boken.</p>

### Negation goes between verb and particle

<Sv>Inte</Sv> is placed after the finite verb and therefore
**before** the particle:

- <Sv>Jag tycker inte om henne.</Sv>
- <Sv>Han slog inte på radion.</Sv>

See [Placement of *inte*](../../sentence-structure/inte-placement/) for
the general pattern.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Particle and pronoun object">
	<p slot="de">Ich rufe ihn an.</p>
	<p slot="en">I call him up.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag ringer upp honom.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German moves the separable prefix to the end of the clause, English may split
the particle from the verb with a pronoun, and Swedish keeps the particle
directly after the verb and before the object.

<LanguageComparison title="Negated particle verb">
	<p slot="de">Ich mag sie nicht.</p>
	<p slot="en">I do not like her.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag tycker inte om henne.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

For German speakers this is good news: nothing has to be moved to the end of
the sentence. The particle simply stays where the verb is.

| <Sv>komma ihåg</Sv> | to remember | <span lang="de">sich erinnern</span> |
| <Sv>komma på</Sv> | to think of, to realize | <span lang="de">einfallen</span> |
| <Sv>se ut</Sv> | to look, to appear | <span lang="de">aussehen</span> |

| <Sv>ta på sig</Sv> | to put on (clothes) | <span lang="de">anziehen</span> |
| <Sv>ta av sig</Sv> | to take off (clothes) | <span lang="de">ausziehen</span> |
| <Sv>ta reda på</Sv> | to find out | <span lang="de">herausfinden</span> |
| <Sv>slå på</Sv> | to switch on | <span lang="de">einschalten</span> |
| <Sv>stänga av</Sv> | to switch off | <span lang="de">ausschalten</span> |
| <Sv>tala om</Sv> | to tell, to inform | <span lang="de">erzählen, mitteilen</span> |
| <Sv>hålla på</Sv> | to be busy doing something | <span lang="de">gerade dabei sein</span> |
| <Sv>passa på</Sv> | to take the opportunity, to watch out | <span lang="de">aufpassen, die Gelegenheit nutzen</span> |
| <Sv>hitta på</Sv> | to make up, to come up with | <span lang="de">sich ausdenken</span> |

Store each entry with its particle, exactly as you would store a noun with its
gender. Several of these entries cannot be reduced to a single translation:
what follows the particle decides the meaning.

## The same verb in different constructions

### <Sv>hålla på</Sv>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>hålla på med</Sv> + noun | to be busy with | <Sv>Vad håller du på med?</Sv> — *What are you doing?* |
| <Sv>hålla på att</Sv> + infinitive | to be in the middle of doing | <Sv>Jag håller på att laga mat.</Sv> — *I am cooking right now.* |
| <Sv>hålla på att</Sv> + infinitive | to almost do something | <Sv>Jag höll på att ramla.</Sv> — *I almost fell.* |
| on its own | to keep at it | <Sv>Han höll på i timmar.</Sv> — *He kept at it for hours.* |

The last two rows share the same construction, so only the context separates
them. A present tense usually describes an ongoing action, while a preterite
with a verb such as <Sv>ramla</Sv> or
<Sv>tappa</Sv> usually means *almost*.

### <Sv>passa på</Sv>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>passa på att</Sv> + infinitive | to seize the opportunity | <Sv>Passa på att köpa biljetter nu!</Sv> — *Take the chance to buy tickets now!* |
| on its own, often imperative | to pay attention, to watch out | <Sv>Passa på när du går över gatan.</Sv> — *Pay attention when you cross the street.* |

Without the particle, <Sv>passa</Sv> is a different verb
altogether: <Sv>Byxorna passar.</Sv> — *The trousers fit.*

### <Sv>komma på</Sv>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| + noun | to come up with | <Sv>Jag kom på en bra idé.</Sv> — *I came up with a good idea.* |
| + <Sv>att</Sv> clause | to realize, to remember suddenly | <Sv>Jag kom på att jag glömt nyckeln.</Sv> — *I realized I had forgotten the key.* |

### <Sv>se ut</Sv>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>se ut som</Sv> | to look like | <Sv>Han ser ut som sin pappa.</Sv> — *He looks like his father.* |
| <Sv>se ut att</Sv> | to seem to | <Sv>Det ser ut att bli regn.</Sv> — *It looks like rain.* |
| <Sv>hur ser … ut</Sv> | how something looks or stands | <Sv>Hur ser det ut med tiden?</Sv> — *How are we doing for time?* |

### <Sv>ta på sig</Sv>

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| + clothing | to put on | <Sv>Ta på dig jackan.</Sv> — *Put on your jacket.* |
| + abstract noun | to take on, to accept | <Sv>Han tog på sig ansvaret.</Sv> — *He took on the responsibility.* |

## Separated and prefixed forms

Some particle verbs also exist as a written-together prefixed verb. The
prefixed form is usually more formal and often means something noticeably
different:

| Separated | Prefixed |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>tala om</Sv> — to tell | <Sv>tilltala</Sv> — to address someone, to appeal to |
| <Sv>gå av</Sv> — to get off, to break | <Sv>avgå</Sv> — to resign, to depart |
| <Sv>gå ut</Sv> — to go out | <Sv>utgå</Sv> — to be omitted, to proceed from |

Do not treat the two forms as interchangeable. Learn the one you actually need
and check the other separately.

When a particle verb becomes an adjective or a noun, the parts are written
together: <Sv>radion är påslagen</Sv>,
<Sv>tågets avgång</Sv>.

<MemoryTip>
Say the verb aloud and listen for the beat. <Sv>tycka **OM**</Sv>
has its weight on the particle; <Sv>**TIT**ta på</Sv> has it on
the verb. The stress tells you which of the two you are dealing with.
</MemoryTip>

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag tycker om inte henne.</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag tycker inte om henne.</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Han slog den på.</Sv> — English word order
	  ✅ <Sv>Han slog på den.</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag tilltalar dig att jag kommer.</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag talar om att jag kommer.</Sv>
	- Do not translate a German separable verb by moving the particle to the end
	  of the clause. Swedish keeps it next to the verb.
	- Do not learn a single translation for
	  <Sv>hålla på</Sv> or <Sv>passa på</Sv>. Learn
	  the construction that follows the particle.
</MistakeBox>
