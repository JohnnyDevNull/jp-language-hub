<KeyTakeaway>
- The present form is the same for every subject: <Sv>jag arbetar</Sv>,
  <Sv>hon arbetar</Sv>, <Sv>de arbetar</Sv>.
- The ending follows the verb group: <Sv>-ar</Sv>, <Sv>-er</Sv> or plain
  <Sv>-r</Sv>.
- Swedish has no progressive form; the ordinary present also covers
  <span lang="en">I am working</span> and, in a clear context, a planned future.
</KeyTakeaway>

The Swedish present tense describes what happens now, what happens regularly,
and—in a clear context—what is planned or expected to happen later.

## Core rule

The present form is identical for every subject:

| Subject | Example |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>jag</Sv> | <Sv>Jag arbetar.</Sv> |
| <Sv>du</Sv> | <Sv>Du arbetar.</Sv> |
| <Sv>hon / han / hen</Sv> | <Sv>Hon arbetar.</Sv> |
| <Sv>vi / ni / de</Sv> | <Sv>De arbetar.</Sv> |

The ending depends on the verb group. One representative verb per group:

Some frequent verbs are irregular, for example <Sv>vara → är</Sv> and
<Sv>göra → gör</Sv>. See [verb groups](../verb-groups/) for the full patterns.

## Main uses

### Happening now

<Sv>Jag läser just nu.</Sv> — I am reading right now.

Swedish normally uses the ordinary present here. It does not require an English-style
<span lang="en">am/is/are + -ing</span> form.

### Habits and repeated events

<Sv>Vi tränar varje torsdag.</Sv> — We train every Thursday.

### General facts and states

<Sv>Stockholm ligger i Sverige.</Sv> — Stockholm is in Sweden.

### Future meaning from context

<Sv>Jag åker till Malmö i morgon.</Sv> — I am going to Malmö tomorrow.

The time expression makes the future meaning clear. Other future constructions
are used when the context requires them.

## Negation and word order

In a main clause, <Sv>inte</Sv> normally follows the finite verb:

<Sv>Jag arbetar inte idag.</Sv>

In a subordinate clause, the order normally changes:

<Sv>… eftersom jag inte arbetar idag.</Sv>

This is a word-order rule, not a different present-tense form. See the pages on
[V2](../../sentence-structure/v2/) and
[BIFF](../../sentence-structure/biff/).

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- Do not add a person ending modeled on English or German: Swedish uses
	  <Sv>jag kommer</Sv> and <Sv>hon kommer</Sv>.
	- Do not translate the English progressive word for word. Usually
	  <span lang="en">I am working</span> becomes <Sv>jag arbetar</Sv>.
	- Learn irregular present forms individually.
</MistakeBox>
