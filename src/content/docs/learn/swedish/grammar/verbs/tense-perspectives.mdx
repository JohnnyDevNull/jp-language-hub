Swedish tense choices become easier when they are viewed from two perspectives:
what is anchored around **now** and what is anchored around a point in the
**past**.

## The now perspective

| Position | Swedish tense | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| before now | perfect | <span lang="sv">Jag har skrivit mejlet.</span> | I have written the email. |
| now | present | <span lang="sv">Jag läser svaret.</span> | I am reading the answer. |
| after now | future | <span lang="sv">Jag ska ringa Anna.</span> | I am going to call Anna. |

The present is the basic finite form for present-time statements. The perfect
uses <span lang="sv">har</span> + supine to connect an earlier action with the
present. The future is often built with <span lang="sv">ska</span> +
infinitive, though Swedish can also use the present when the future meaning is
clear from context.

## The past perspective

| Position | Swedish tense | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| before then | pluperfect | <span lang="sv">Jag hade packat väskan.</span> | I had packed the bag. |
| then | preterite | <span lang="sv">Resan började.</span> | The trip began. |
| after then | future in the past | <span lang="sv">Jag skulle köpa biljetten.</span> | I was going to buy the ticket. |

The past perspective is useful when telling a story. First establish a past
reference point, then choose whether another action happened before, at, or
after that point.

The forms look familiar if you know German or English, but the usage boundaries
do not match perfectly. Treat this page as a map; use the individual tense
pages for the details.

## What is finite?

For word order, identify the finite verb:

- <span lang="sv">Jag skriver mejlet.</span> — <span lang="sv">skriver</span> is finite.
- <span lang="sv">Jag har skrivit mejlet.</span> — <span lang="sv">har</span> is finite.
- <span lang="sv">Jag hade skrivit mejlet.</span> — <span lang="sv">hade</span> is finite.
- <span lang="sv">Jag ska skriva mejlet.</span> — <span lang="sv">ska</span> is finite.

This matters for [V2](../../sentence-structure/v2/) and for the
placement of <span lang="sv">inte</span>.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- Do not use the infinitive after <span lang="sv">har</span> or
	  <span lang="sv">hade</span>; use the supine:
	  <span lang="sv">har skrivit</span>, <span lang="sv">hade skrivit</span>.
	- Do not treat English progressive forms as separate Swedish tense forms.
	  Swedish usually uses the ordinary present.
	- Do not choose a tense only by translating German names. First ask where
	  the action sits relative to now or a past reference point.
</MistakeBox>
