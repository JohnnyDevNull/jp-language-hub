<KeyTakeaway>
- The s-passive adds <Sv>-s</Sv> to every verb form and is the default for
  routines, rules and notices.
- The bli-passive, <Sv>bli</Sv> + participle, describes a single event, and the
  participle agrees with the subject.
- An <Sv>-s</Sv> form is not always a passive: <Sv>hoppas</Sv> and
  <Sv>Vi ses!</Sv> are not.
</KeyTakeaway>

Swedish has two passive constructions. The **s-passive** adds
<Sv>-s</Sv> to the verb and has no German equivalent. The
**bli-passive** uses <Sv>bli</Sv> plus a participle and works
like German <span lang="de">werden</span> plus participle.

German speakers tend to overuse the second one, because it is the familiar
shape. In Swedish the s-passive is the more common of the two.

## The s-passive

The <Sv>-s</Sv> attaches to every form of the verb. In the
present tense it replaces the active ending:

| Form | Active | Passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | <Sv>öppna</Sv> | <Sv>öppnas</Sv> |
| Present | <Sv>öppnar</Sv> | <Sv>öppnas</Sv> |
| Preterite | <Sv>öppnade</Sv> | <Sv>öppnades</Sv> |
| Supine | <Sv>öppnat</Sv> | <Sv>öppnats</Sv> |

The same works for other verb groups:

| Verb | Present | Preterite | Supine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>köpa</Sv> | <Sv>köps</Sv> | <Sv>köptes</Sv> | <Sv>köpts</Sv> |
| <Sv>skriva</Sv> | <Sv>skrivs</Sv> | <Sv>skrevs</Sv> | <Sv>skrivits</Sv> |

Examples in use:

- <Sv>Rummet städas varje dag.</Sv> — *The room is cleaned every
  day.*
- <Sv>Cykeln stals i går.</Sv> — *The bicycle was stolen
  yesterday.*
- <Sv>Boken skrevs av Astrid Lindgren.</Sv> — *The book was
  written by Astrid Lindgren.*

The agent, when mentioned at all, is introduced by
<Sv>av</Sv>.

<RuleBox title="Where the s-passive belongs">
	Use it for general statements, routines, rules, instructions, and signs —
	anything where the action matters more than who performs it:
	<Sv>Dörrarna stängs automatiskt.</Sv>
</RuleBox>

## The bli-passive

<Sv>Bli</Sv> plus the past participle describes a single event
and the change of state it causes:

- <Sv>Dörren blev öppnad.</Sv> — *The door was opened.*
- <Sv>Han blev vald till ordförande.</Sv> — *He was elected
  chairman.*

The participle behaves like an adjective and agrees with the subject:

| Subject | Example |
| --- | --- |
| en-word | <Sv>Bilen blev såld.</Sv> |
| ett-word | <Sv>Huset blev sålt.</Sv> |
| plural | <Sv>Böckerna blev sålda.</Sv> |

See [adjective agreement](../../adjectives-adverbs/agreement/)
for the endings.

With <Sv>vara</Sv> instead of <Sv>bli</Sv>, the
same participle describes a resulting state rather than the event:
<Sv>Huset är sålt.</Sv>

## Choosing between them

| Situation | Preferred | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| routine, rule, general truth | s-passive | <Sv>Maten lagas i köket.</Sv> |
| written instructions and notices | s-passive | <Sv>Biljetter köps i appen.</Sv> |
| one specific event, spoken language | bli-passive | <Sv>Han blev bjuden på fest.</Sv> |
| emphasis on the resulting state | <Sv>vara</Sv> + participle | <Sv>Dörren är stängd.</Sv> |

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Routine, no agent">
	<p slot="de">Das Zimmer wird jeden Tag geputzt.</p>
	<p slot="en">The room is cleaned every day.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Rummet städas varje dag.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Single event">
	<p slot="de">Die Tür wurde geöffnet.</p>
	<p slot="en">The door was opened.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Dörren blev öppnad.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German uses <span lang="de">werden</span> plus participle in both cases;
Swedish splits them between the s-passive and the bli-passive.

Both passives are often avoidable. Swedish frequently prefers an active
sentence with <Sv>man</Sv>:
<Sv>Man säger att…</Sv> — *It is said that…*

## s-verbs that are not passive

Some verbs simply end in <Sv>-s</Sv> and have no active form.
They are not passives, and translating them as one leads nowhere:

| Verb | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>finns</Sv> | there is, exists |
| <Sv>hoppas</Sv> | to hope |
| <Sv>trivs</Sv> | to feel at home, to enjoy being somewhere |
| <Sv>minns</Sv> | to remember |
| <Sv>lyckas</Sv> | to succeed |
| <Sv>andas</Sv> | to breathe |

- <Sv>Jag hoppas att du mår bra.</Sv>
- <Sv>Jag trivs i Malmö.</Sv>

## The reciprocal -s

An <Sv>-s</Sv> form can also mean that two or more people do
something *to each other*:

- <Sv>Vi ses!</Sv> — *See you!*
- <Sv>De träffas varje vecka.</Sv> — *They meet every week.*
- <Sv>De kramades.</Sv> — *They hugged.*

<MemoryTip>
Three short sentences cover the whole system:
<Sv><strong>Rummet städas varje dag</strong></Sv> — routine.
<Sv><strong>Dörren blev öppnad</strong></Sv> — single event.
<Sv><strong>Vi ses!</strong></Sv> — each other, not passive at
all.
</MemoryTip>

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <Sv>Rummet blir städat varje dag</Sv> as a default
	  ✅ <Sv>Rummet städas varje dag.</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Huset blev sålld</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Huset blev sålt.</Sv> — the participle agrees with the
	  subject
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag hoppar att du mår bra</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag hoppas att du mår bra.</Sv> —
	  <Sv>hoppas</Sv> has no active form
	- ❌ <Sv>Boken skrevs från Astrid Lindgren</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Boken skrevs av Astrid Lindgren.</Sv>
	- Do not reach for <Sv>bli</Sv> just because German uses
	  <span lang="de">werden</span>.
</MistakeBox>
