<KeyTakeaway>
- A yes/no question normally starts with the finite verb, followed by the
  subject: <span lang="sv">Kommer du?</span>
- Swedish moves the existing finite verb; it does not add an equivalent of
  English *do*.
- In a direct question with a non-subject question word, that expression comes
  first and the same verb–subject frame follows it.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Use a yes/no question when the answer can be confirmation or denial. The finite
verb moves before the subject; intonation supports the question, but word order
already marks it.

## Use this now

Turn a neutral statement into a yes/no question by moving the finite verb to
the front:

- <span lang="sv">Du **bor** här.</span> → <span lang="sv">**Bor** du här?</span>
- <span lang="sv">Hon **har** köpt biljetten.</span> → <span lang="sv">**Har** hon köpt biljetten?</span>
- <span lang="sv">Ni **kan** komma imorgon.</span> → <span lang="sv">**Kan** ni komma imorgon?</span>

Only the finite verb moves. A supine or infinitive stays later in the clause.
For the position and pragmatic tone of <span lang="sv">inte</span>, see
[Placement of *inte*](../../sentence-structure/inte-placement/).

## Indirect yes/no questions with *om*

When a yes/no question becomes part of a larger sentence, introduce it with
<span lang="sv">om</span> (“whether / if”) and use subordinate-clause order:

<SentenceSchema

	rows={[
		{ label: 'Neutral', cells: ['om', 'du', '', 'kommer', 'imorgon'] },
		{ label: 'Negative', cells: ['om', 'du', 'inte', 'kommer', 'imorgon'] },

- <span lang="sv">Kommer du imorgon?</span>
- <span lang="sv">Jag undrar om du kommer imorgon.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Jag vet inte om du kommer imorgon.</span>

See [Subordinate clauses](../../sentence-structure/subordinate-clauses/) for the
complete dependent-clause frame.

## Short answers

Swedish often answers with <span lang="sv">ja</span> or <span lang="sv">nej</span> plus a compact clause:

- <span lang="sv">Kommer du imorgon? — Ja, det gör jag.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Har hon ringt? — Nej, det har hon inte.</span>

In everyday conversation a bare <span lang="sv">ja</span> or <span lang="sv">nej</span> is also possible
when the answer is clear.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Present-tense yes/no question">
	<p slot="de">Wohnst du hier?</p>
	<p slot="en">Do you live here?</p>
	<p slot="sv">Bor du här?</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German and Swedish move the lexical finite verb. English normally adds *do* in
the simple present and past unless an auxiliary or *be* is already present.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Gör du bor här?"
	correct="Bor du här?"
	lang="sv"
>
Do not copy English *do*-support into Swedish. Move the finite verb that the
statement already contains.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
For a yes/no question, let the finite verb ask first: **finite verb → subject →
the rest**.
</MemoryTip>
