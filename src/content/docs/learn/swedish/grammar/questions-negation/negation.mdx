<KeyTakeaway>
- Use <Sv>inte</Sv> to negate a clause: <Sv>Jag kommer inte.</Sv>
- Use one negative noun or adverb expression when it carries the negative
  meaning itself: <Sv>Ingen kom.</Sv>, <Sv>Jag såg ingenting.</Sv>
- In ordinary standard Swedish, do not add <Sv>inte</Sv> when another negative
  expression already negates the same clause.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Negation is first a choice of meaning and form. This page helps you choose the
negative expression; [Placement of *inte*](../../sentence-structure/inte-placement/)
owns where <Sv>inte</Sv> goes in each clause type.

## Use this now

| Meaning | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| not | <Sv>inte</Sv> | <Sv>Jag arbetar inte idag.</Sv> |
| nobody / no one | <Sv>ingen</Sv> | <Sv>Ingen ringde.</Sv> |
| nothing | <Sv>ingenting</Sv> or <Sv>inget</Sv> + noun | <Sv>Jag hörde ingenting.</Sv> / <Sv>Jag hörde inget ljud.</Sv> |
| never | <Sv>aldrig</Sv> | <Sv>Hon röker aldrig.</Sv> |
| nowhere | <Sv>ingenstans</Sv> | <Sv>Jag hittar den ingenstans.</Sv> |
| not … either | <Sv>inte … heller</Sv> | <Sv>Jag kommer inte heller.</Sv> |

## Core rule

Use <Sv>inte</Sv> when you negate the clause as a whole or reject one part in
context:

- <Sv>Jag **kommer inte** imorgon.</Sv>
- <Sv>Hon köpte **inte den röda** bilen.</Sv>

Use a negative expression when the missing person, thing, time, or place itself
is negative:

- <Sv>**Ingen** väntar utanför.</Sv>
- <Sv>Jag såg **ingenting**.</Sv>
- <Sv>Vi går **aldrig** dit.</Sv>
- <Sv>Nyckeln finns **ingenstans**.</Sv>

## Indefinite under clause negation

When <Sv>inte</Sv> negates the clause, Swedish normally uses a non-negative
indefinite expression inside its scope:

- <Sv>Jag har **inte någon** bil.</Sv>
- <Sv>Jag såg **inte något** problem.</Sv>
- <Sv>Vi har **inte några** frågor.</Sv>

The compact negative noun phrase often gives the same real-world result:

- <Sv>Jag har **ingen** bil.</Sv>
- <Sv>Jag såg **inget** problem.</Sv>
- <Sv>Vi har **inga** frågor.</Sv>

The two patterns are not mechanically interchangeable in every context.
<Sv>Ingen / inget / inga</Sv> packages the absence inside the noun phrase;
<Sv>inte någon / något / några</Sv> keeps clause negation visible and can carry
different focus or contrast.

The full agreement and noun-phrase choice belongs to
[*någon, något, ingen, inget*](../../pronouns-roles/nagon-nagot-ingen-inget/).

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="No car">
	<p slot="de">Ich habe kein Auto.</p>
	<p slot="en">I do not have a car.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag har ingen bil.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Nothing">
	<p slot="de">Ich habe nichts gesehen.</p>
	<p slot="en">I did not see anything.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag såg ingenting.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

English often combines *not* with an indefinite form such as *anything*.
Swedish can use either <Sv>inte</Sv> + an indefinite expression or one negative
expression, but the choice can shift focus and scope.

<MistakeBox
	title="Avoid ordinary double negation"
	incorrect="Ingen kom inte."
	correct="Ingen kom."
	lang="sv"
>
In ordinary standard Swedish, one expression normally carries the negative
force. If <Sv>ingen</Sv> already means “nobody,” a second <Sv>inte</Sv> normally
changes or obscures the intended meaning instead of strengthening it.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Choose **one carrier of the negative meaning** first: clause-wide <Sv>inte</Sv>,
or a negative person, thing, time, or place expression.
</MemoryTip>
