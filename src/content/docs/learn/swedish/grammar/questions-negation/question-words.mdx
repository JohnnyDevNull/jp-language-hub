<KeyTakeaway>
- In a direct question the question word comes first and the finite verb second:
  <Sv>Var bor du?</Sv>
- An indirect question is a subordinate clause, so the subject comes back before
  the verb: <Sv>Jag vet inte var hon bor.</Sv>
- In an indirect question, <Sv>vem</Sv> and <Sv>vad</Sv> add <Sv>som</Sv> when
  they are the subject of their own clause.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish question words nearly all begin with <Sv>v-</Sv>, which
makes them easy to recognize. The harder part is the word order, which differs
between direct and indirect questions.

## Use this now

| Swedish | Use it to ask about… |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>vem</Sv> | a person |
| <Sv>vems</Sv> | an owner |
| <Sv>vad</Sv> | a thing, event, or identity |
| <Sv>vilken / vilket / vilka</Sv> | a choice from a set |
| <Sv>var</Sv> | a location |
| <Sv>vart</Sv> | a direction |
| <Sv>när</Sv> | a time |
| <Sv>hur</Sv> | a manner or condition |
| <Sv>varför</Sv> | a reason |
| <Sv>hur mycket / hur många</Sv> | an amount or number |

<Sv>Vem</Sv> is invariable — there is no separate object form,
so <Sv>Vem såg du?</Sv> and <Sv>Vem såg dig?</Sv>
differ only in word order, not in the pronoun.

## Core rule: direct questions

When the question word is not the subject, it fills the first field and the
finite verb follows it. Compare that frame with a yes/no question:

<SentenceSchema
	caption="Direct question frames"
	rowHeader="Question"
	columns={['Question phrase', 'Finite verb', 'Subject', 'Rest']}
	rows={[
		{ label: 'Question word', cells: ['Var', 'bor', 'du', ''] },
		{ label: 'Question phrase', cells: ['Vilken bok', 'läser', 'du', ''] },
		{ label: 'Yes/no', cells: ['', 'Bor', 'du', 'här?'] },
	]}
/>

When the question word itself is the subject, it occupies the subject role and
no separate subject follows: <Sv>Vem ringde?</Sv> and <Sv>Vad hände?</Sv>

<RuleBox title="No do-support">
	Swedish has nothing like English *do*. <Sv>Bor du här?</Sv> is
	*Do you live here?*, and <Sv>Vad sa han?</Sv> is *What did he
	say?* The verb itself moves; no helper is added.
</RuleBox>

See [Questions](../questions/) for the shared yes/no frame and
[V2](../../sentence-structure/v2/) for declarative main clauses.

## var or vart

Standard Swedish separates position from direction, as German does:

- <Sv>Var bor du?</Sv> — *Where do you live?*
- <Sv>Vart går du?</Sv> — *Where are you going?*

For *where from*, the preposition goes to the end:
<Sv>Var kommer du ifrån?</Sv>

## vilken agrees with the noun

The <Sv>vilken</Sv> series agrees with the noun's gender and number:

- <Sv>Vilken bok läser du?</Sv> — an en-word
- <Sv>Vilket hus är ditt?</Sv> — an ett-word
- <Sv>Vilka böcker har du läst?</Sv> — plural

For *what kind of*, Swedish uses <Sv>vad för</Sv> or
<Sv>vad för sorts</Sv>:
<Sv>Vad för sorts musik lyssnar du på?</Sv>

See [en / ett](../../nouns-articles/en-ett/) for the gender
behind the choice.

## Indirect questions: no inversion

An indirect question is a subordinate clause. The subject therefore comes
before the finite verb, and a sentence adverb such as <Sv>inte</Sv> comes before
that verb:

<SentenceSchema
	caption="Direct and indirect question-word order"
	rowHeader="Question"
	columns={['Question word', 'Finite verb (direct)', 'Subject', 'Sentence adverb', 'Verb (indirect)', 'Rest']}
	rows={[
		{ label: 'Direct', cells: ['Var', 'bor', 'hon', '', '', ''] },
		{ label: 'Indirect', cells: ['var', '', 'hon', '', 'bor', ''] },
		{ label: 'Indirect, negative', cells: ['varför', '', 'hon', 'inte', 'kommer', ''] },
	]}
/>

- <Sv>Var bor hon?</Sv> → <Sv>Jag vet inte var hon bor.</Sv>
- <Sv>Varför kommer hon?</Sv> → <Sv>Jag förstår inte varför hon kommer.</Sv>

See [Subordinate clauses](../../sentence-structure/subordinate-clauses/) for the
general dependent-clause frame. [Questions](../questions/#indirect-yesno-questions-with-om)
handles indirect yes/no questions with <Sv>om</Sv>.

## Insert som when the question word is the subject

In an indirect question, <Sv>vem</Sv> and
<Sv>vad</Sv> take <Sv>som</Sv> when they are the
subject of their own clause:

- <Sv>Jag vet inte vem som ringde.</Sv> — *I do not know who
  called.*
- <Sv>Berätta vad som hände!</Sv> — *Tell me what happened!*

When they are the object, no <Sv>som</Sv> appears:

- <Sv>Jag vet inte vem hon ringde.</Sv> — *…who she called.*
- <Sv>Berätta vad du gjorde!</Sv> — *…what you did.*

Direct questions do not need it: <Sv>Vem ringde?</Sv>,
<Sv>Vad hände?</Sv>

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag vet inte var bor hon.</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag vet inte var hon bor.</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag vet inte vem ringde.</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag vet inte vem som ringde.</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Vart bor du?</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Var bor du?</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Vilken hus är ditt?</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Vilket hus är ditt?</Sv>
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Contrast one pair until it is automatic:
<Sv><strong>Vem som ringde</strong></Sv> — the caller is the
subject. <Sv><strong>Vem hon ringde</strong></Sv> — the caller is
someone else. The extra <Sv>som</Sv> marks the subject.
</MemoryTip>
