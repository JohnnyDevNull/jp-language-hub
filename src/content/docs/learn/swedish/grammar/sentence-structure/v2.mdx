<KeyTakeaway>
- The <span lang="sv">finit verb</span> is the second **constituent**, not the second word.
- If something other than the <span lang="sv">subjekt</span> fills position one, the
  <span lang="sv">subjekt</span> follows the <span lang="sv">finit verb</span>.
- Only the <span lang="sv">finit verb</span> counts. <span lang="sv">Infinit verb</span> stay later in
  the clause.
</KeyTakeaway>

**V2** means that the <span lang="sv">finit verb</span> normally occupies the second position in a
Swedish declarative main clause. It is one of the most important rules for
producing natural Swedish sentences, and German speakers already know it.

## Core rule

> In a Swedish declarative main clause, the **<span lang="sv">finit verb</span>** normally comes in
> the **second constituent position**.

Read the clause as a row of fields. Position one is a single field no matter how
many words it holds, and the <span lang="sv">finit verb</span> owns position two:

In a neutral sentence the <span lang="sv">subjekt</span> itself fills position one, so
position three stays empty. As soon as something else is fronted, the
<span lang="sv">subjekt</span> moves behind the verb:

<ExampleBox title="Time expression in position one" lang="sv">
	<p><strong>Idag</strong> <strong>köper</strong> jag en bil.</p>
</ExampleBox>

## Position does not mean word

One constituent may contain several words:

<p lang="sv"><strong>Efter jobbet</strong> <strong>köper</strong> jag mat.</p>

<span lang="sv">Efter jobbet</span> is one adverbial constituent. The verb <span lang="sv">köper</span> is
still in the second sentence position even though it is the third written word.

## Compound verb phrases

V2 applies to the **<span lang="sv">finit verb</span>**, not to every verb in the clause:

<p lang="sv">Idag <strong>vill</strong> jag köpa en bil.</p>
<p lang="sv">Idag <strong>har</strong> jag köpt en bil.</p>

The <span lang="sv">infinit verb</span> forms <span lang="sv">köpa</span> and <span lang="sv">köpt</span> stay later in the clause.

## What can occupy position one?

Common choices include:

- the <span lang="sv">subjekt</span>: <span lang="sv">Jag arbetar hemma idag.</span>
- time: <span lang="sv">Idag arbetar jag hemma.</span>
- place: <span lang="sv">Hemma arbetar jag bäst.</span>
- an <span lang="sv">objekt</span>: <span lang="sv">Den boken har jag redan läst.</span>
- a subordinate clause: <span lang="sv">När jag är klar går jag hem.</span>

Changing position one changes the emphasis, not the V2 requirement.

German is a useful bridge because its main clauses also normally follow V2.
English does not generally invert the subject and verb after an initial time
expression.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="På måndag morgon jag börjar mitt nya jobb."
	correct="På måndag morgon börjar jag mitt nya jobb."
	lang="sv"

Do not count individual words and do not leave the <span lang="sv">subjekt</span> before the
verb after fronting another constituent.
</MistakeBox>

<SpecialRule language="Swedish" title="Scope of V2">
	V2 describes declarative **main clauses**. A subordinate clause has a
	different internal pattern; in particular, <span lang="sv">satsadverbial</span> such as
	<span lang="sv">inte</span> normally come before the <span lang="sv">finit verb</span>. See
	[Subordinate clauses](../subordinate-clauses/) for the whole pattern and
	[BIFF](../biff/) for <span lang="sv">satsadverbial</span> placement.
</SpecialRule>

Think in blocks: **first block → <span lang="sv">finit verb</span> → <span lang="sv">subjekt</span> (if it
was not first) → the rest**.
</MemoryTip>
