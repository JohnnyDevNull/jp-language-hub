<KeyTakeaway>
- <Sv>inte</Sv> follows the finite verb in a main clause and precedes it in a
  subordinate clause.
- If a subject follows the finite verb, <Sv>inte</Sv> comes after that subject.
- With two verbs, <Sv>inte</Sv> sits between the finite verb and the non-finite
  verb.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

The position of Swedish <Sv>inte</Sv> depends on the type of clause. The central
contrast is simple: it normally follows the finite verb in a main clause but
precedes the finite verb in a subordinate clause.

## Core rule

One set of fields covers both clause types. <Sv>inte</Sv> always sits in the
same sentence-adverb field; what moves is the finite verb. In the main clause it
holds position two, in the subordinate clause position two is empty and the verb
follows <Sv>inte</Sv>:

way to the end of the subordinate clause, while Swedish moves it only past the
sentence adverb.

## Main clauses

In a main clause, <Sv>inte</Sv> normally comes after the finite verb and after a
subject that follows that verb:

- <Sv>Jag <strong>kommer inte</strong> idag.</Sv>
- <Sv>Idag <strong>kommer jag inte</strong>.</Sv>
- <Sv>Jag <strong>har inte</strong> köpt bilen.</Sv>

With a compound verb phrase, <Sv>inte</Sv> follows the finite auxiliary and
precedes the non-finite verb:

<p lang="sv">Hon <strong>vill inte gå</strong> hem.</p>

## Subordinate clauses

In a subordinate clause, <Sv>inte</Sv> normally comes before the finite verb:

- <Sv>… eftersom jag <strong>inte kommer</strong> idag.</Sv>
- <Sv>… att hon <strong>inte har</strong> köpt bilen.</Sv>
- <Sv>… om vi <strong>inte kan</strong> träffas.</Sv>

This contrast is summarized by [BIFF](../biff/).

## Questions

In a yes/no question, the finite verb comes first, followed by the subject and
then <Sv>inte</Sv>:

<p lang="sv">Kommer du inte imorgon?</p>

With a question word:

<p lang="sv">Varför kommer du inte imorgon?</p>

The intonation and context can make a negative question sound surprised or as
if the speaker expected a positive answer.

## Imperatives and infinitives

With a negative imperative, place <Sv>inte</Sv> after the verb:

<p lang="sv">Glöm inte nycklarna!</p>

When an infinitive itself is negated, <Sv>inte</Sv> normally comes before it:

<p lang="sv">Det är svårt att inte tänka på det.</p>

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Jag vet att hon arbetar inte idag."
	correct="Jag vet att hon inte arbetar idag."
	lang="sv"
>
Do not place <Sv>inte</Sv> after the finite verb merely because the clause
contains an explicit subject. First decide whether it is a main clause or a
subordinate clause.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
**Main clause:** finite first, then <Sv>inte</Sv>.  
**Subordinate clause:** <Sv>inte</Sv> first, then finite.
</MemoryTip>
