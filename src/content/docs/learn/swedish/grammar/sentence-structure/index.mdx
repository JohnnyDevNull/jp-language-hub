<KeyTakeaway>
- Start with the neutral main-clause frame, then use V2 and inversion when a
  different constituent comes first.
- Treat subordinate clauses as their own clause type; use BIFF for the narrower
  placement of sentence adverbs inside them.
- Look up the position of *inte* separately when clause type, questions, or
  multi-verb phrases make the placement uncertain.

## Learn in this order

1. [Basic word order](./basic-word-order/) gives you the neutral main-clause
   frame.
2. [V2 rule](./v2/) explains the finite verb's fixed second position.
3. [Inversion](./inversion/) shows what happens to the subject after a different
   first constituent.
4. [Subordinate clauses](./subordinate-clauses/) introduces the dependent
   clause frame.
5. [BIFF](./biff/) focuses on sentence-adverb placement inside that frame.
6. [Placement of *inte*](./inte-placement/) connects the main patterns and adds
   questions, infinitives, and imperatives.

## Watch for

- **V2 versus inversion:** V2 is the finite-verb-second rule; inversion is its
  visible subject-after-verb result when the subject is not first.
- **Subordinate clauses versus BIFF:** the first page builds the whole clause;
  BIFF answers only where sentence adverbs go.
- **Negation versus placement:** [Negation](../questions-negation/negation/)
  chooses a negative expression; Placement of *inte* positions one particular
  expression.
