<KeyTakeaway>
- The neutral main clause runs subject, finite verb, sentence adverb, remaining
  verb parts, object, adverbial.
- A sentence position holds one **constituent**, which may be several words.
- Swedish verbs do not change according to person; word order shows how the
  clause is organized.

Swedish word order carries grammatical meaning. The most useful starting point
is the neutral subject-first main clause; other pages handle fronting,
questions, and subordinate clauses.

## Core rule

In a neutral declarative main clause, Swedish usually follows this order:

> **subject + finite verb + sentence adverb + remaining verb parts + object + adverbial**

Read the clause as a row of fields. This is the neutral schema behind the
[V2 rule](../v2/):

In a neutral clause the subject fills position one itself, so position three
stays empty. The German row fills the same opening fields, which is why the
neutral pattern feels familiar; German differs later in the clause, where it
sends non-finite verb forms to the very end.

- <Sv>Jag</Sv> — subject
- <Sv>köper</Sv> — finite verb
- <Sv>inte</Sv> — sentence adverb
- <Sv>bilen</Sv> — object
- <Sv>idag</Sv> — time adverbial

The finite verb carries tense and anchors the clause structure. Swedish verbs
do not change according to person:

- <Sv>Jag arbetar.</Sv> — I work.
- <Sv>Vi arbetar.</Sv> — We work.

## A constituent can contain several words

A sentence position is occupied by a **constituent**, not necessarily one word.

<p lang="sv">Min nya kollega börjar idag.</p>

The entire noun phrase <Sv>min nya kollega</Sv> is the subject and occupies the
first position. The finite verb <Sv>börjar</Sv> is therefore second.

## Choose the next pattern

- If something other than the subject comes first, use
  [Inversion](../inversion/).
- If the sentence asks for confirmation or missing information, use
  [Questions](../../questions-negation/questions/).
- If the clause depends on another clause, use
  [Subordinate clauses](../subordinate-clauses/).
- If you specifically need to place <Sv>inte</Sv>, use
  [Placement of *inte*](../inte-placement/).

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Neutral statement">
	<p slot="de">Ich kaufe heute ein Auto.</p>
	<p slot="en">I am buying a car today.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag köper en bil idag.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

The three languages can look similar in a neutral sentence. Swedish keeps one
finite form across persons, while English may use an auxiliary and German may
place later verb parts differently.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Jag inte köper bilen idag."
	correct="Jag köper inte bilen idag."
	lang="sv"
>
Keep the finite verb before the sentence adverb in a neutral main clause.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Start with **who + finite verb**, then add the sentence adverb, remaining verb
parts, object, and other information.
</MemoryTip>
