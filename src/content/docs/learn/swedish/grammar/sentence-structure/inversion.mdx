<KeyTakeaway>
- As soon as something other than the subject opens a main clause, the subject
  moves behind the finite verb.
- Only the finite verb moves; infinitives and supines stay later in the clause.
- German uses the same verb-before-subject pattern in comparable main clauses;
  English normally does not.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Inversion occurs when a Swedish declarative main clause begins with something
other than the subject. The finite verb remains in the second position, so the
subject follows the verb.

## Core rule

> **fronted constituent + finite verb + subject + rest of clause**

The neutral clause and its fronted variants only differ in what fills position
one. The subject then appears in position three instead:

This is the visible consequence of the [V2 rule](../v2/): whatever the opening
field contains, it is one constituent, and the finite verb owns the next field.

## With more than one verb

Only the finite verb moves before the subject:

- <Sv>Imorgon <strong>ska</strong> vi träffa Anna.</Sv>
- <Sv>I går <strong>hade</strong> jag inte ätit frukost.</Sv>

The infinitive <Sv>träffa</Sv> and the supine <Sv>ätit</Sv> remain later in the
clause.

## After an initial subordinate clause

An entire subordinate clause can occupy the first position. The following main
clause then uses inversion:

<p lang="sv"><strong>När mötet är slut</strong> <strong>går</strong> vi hem.</p>

## Inversion and questions

Yes/no questions also place the finite verb before the subject:

<p lang="sv">Kommer du imorgon?</p>

However, it is useful to distinguish the two patterns:

- a statement keeps the verb in position two after a fronted constituent;
- a yes/no question normally starts with the finite verb, leaving position one
  empty.

See [Questions](../../questions-negation/questions/) for the complete yes/no
frame.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Time first">
	<p slot="de">Heute arbeite ich zu Hause.</p>
	<p slot="en">Today I am working at home.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Idag jobbar jag hemma.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Subordinate clause first">
	<p slot="de">Wenn das Meeting vorbei ist, gehen wir nach Hause.</p>
	<p slot="en">When the meeting is over, we go home.</p>
	<p slot="sv">När mötet är slut går vi hem.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

Swedish and German place the main-clause finite verb before the subject here.
English normally does not.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Efter jobbet jag ska handla."
	correct="Efter jobbet ska jag handla."
	lang="sv"
>
Fronting an expression does not create an extra position before the ordinary
sentence. It changes the order of the main clause.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
If the subject is not first, listen for the pair **verb + subject** immediately
after the opening block: <Sv>Idag <strong>kommer jag</strong> …</Sv>
</MemoryTip>
