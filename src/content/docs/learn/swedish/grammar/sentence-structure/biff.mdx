<KeyTakeaway>
- In neutral standard order, <span lang="sv">inte</span> comes **before** the finite verb in a
  subordinate clause; in a main clause it comes after it.
- The subordinate clause leaves position two empty: the finite verb moves behind
  the sentence adverb.
- The same position is used by other sentence adverbs, not only <span lang="sv">inte</span>.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

**BIFF** is a Swedish memory rule for the neutral standard placement of
sentence adverbs in subordinate clauses:

> **B**isats: **I**nte **F**öre **F**init  
> In a subordinate clause, <span lang="sv">inte</span> comes before the finite verb.

## Core rule

> **<span lang="sv">bisatsinledare + subjekt + satsadverbial + finit verb + adverbial</span>**

The same fields make the shift visible. In the main clause the finite verb sits
in position two and <span lang="sv">inte</span> follows it. In the subordinate clause position
two is empty: the finite verb has moved into the verb group behind
<span lang="sv">inte</span>.

The German rows are worth reading closely. German makes the same main-clause
versus subordinate-clause split, and the sentence adverb also ends up before the
verb — but for a different reason: German sends the whole finite verb to the
**end** of the subordinate clause. Swedish only moves it behind the sentence
adverb, and anything else in the clause still follows it.

<p lang="sv">Jag stannar hemma <strong>eftersom jag inte mår bra</strong>.</p>

## Common subordinators

BIFF becomes useful after words such as:

- <span lang="sv">att</span> — that
- <span lang="sv">eftersom</span> — because
- <span lang="sv">om</span> — if / whether
- <span lang="sv">när</span> — when
- <span lang="sv">fast</span> — although / even though
- <span lang="sv">innan</span> — before

Examples:

- <span lang="sv">Jag vet att hon inte arbetar idag.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Vi går ut om det inte regnar.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Han ringde när jag inte var hemma.</span>

## More than *inte*

The same position is used by many sentence adverbs, for example <span lang="sv">alltid</span>,
<span lang="sv">aldrig</span>, <span lang="sv">kanske</span>, and <span lang="sv">redan</span>, although their exact
meaning and preferred placement can depend on context.

<p lang="sv">Hon säger att hon <strong>aldrig äter</strong> kött.</p>

## Direct comparison

	<p slot="de">… weil ich heute nicht komme.</p>

	<p slot="sv">… eftersom jag inte kommer idag.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

English does not show the Swedish finite-verb contrast clearly. German also
changes subordinate-clause order, but its finite verb normally moves to the end;
that is not the Swedish rule.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Jag stannar hemma eftersom jag mår inte bra."
	correct="Jag stannar hemma eftersom jag inte mår bra."
	lang="sv"
>
Do not keep main-clause placement inside a subordinate clause.
</MistakeBox>

:::note[BIFF is a learning rule]
BIFF helps locate sentence adverbs; it is not a complete grammar of Swedish
subordinate clauses. You still need the subordinator, a subject, the appropriate
verb forms, and the rest of the clause. Main-clause-like order also occurs in
some embedded clauses, especially in speech and when the content is presented
as asserted; use BIFF as the reliable default through B2.
:::
