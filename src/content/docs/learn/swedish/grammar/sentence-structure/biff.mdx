<KeyTakeaway>
- In a main clause, <span lang="sv">inte</span> comes after the conjugated verb:
  <span lang="sv">Jag kommer inte.</span>
- In a subordinate clause, <span lang="sv">inte</span> comes before it:
  <span lang="sv">… eftersom jag inte kommer.</span>
- Words like <span lang="sv">aldrig</span>, <span lang="sv">alltid</span> and
  <span lang="sv">kanske</span> follow the same rule.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

A subordinate clause
([<span lang="sv">bisats</span>](../../../glossary/#bisats)) is a part-sentence
that cannot stand alone. It starts with a word like <span lang="sv">att</span>
(that), <span lang="sv">eftersom</span> (because) or <span lang="sv">om</span>
(if). In it, <span lang="sv">inte</span> moves in front of the conjugated verb:
<span lang="sv">Jag stannar hemma eftersom jag <strong>inte mår</strong> bra.</span> You need
this as soon as you give reasons, report what someone said, or join two
sentences.

Swedish learners remember it with **BIFF**:

> **B**isats — **I**nte — **F**öre — **F**init  
> In a subordinate clause, <span lang="sv">inte</span> comes before the
> conjugated verb.

<span lang="sv">Bisats</span> means subordinate clause, <span lang="sv">före</span>
means before, and <span lang="sv">finit</span> stands for the conjugated verb
([<span lang="sv">finit verb</span>](../../../glossary/#finit-verb)).

## Core rule

Main clause: conjugated verb, then <span lang="sv">inte</span>.

/>

Subordinate clause: <span lang="sv">inte</span>, then the conjugated verb.

The two tables have different boxes, because the two kinds of clause are built
differently. A subordinate clause starts with the word that introduces it
([<span lang="sv">bisatsinledare</span>](../../../glossary/#bisatsinledare)),
then comes the subject. Words like <span lang="sv">inte</span> sit in the box
[<span lang="sv">satsadverbial</span>](../../../glossary/#satsadverbial), and in
a subordinate clause that box comes before the conjugated verb. Everything else
follows as usual.

## Words that start a subordinate clause

BIFF applies after words such as:

- <span lang="sv">att</span> — that
- <span lang="sv">eftersom</span> — because
- <span lang="sv">om</span> — if, whether
- <span lang="sv">när</span> — when
- <span lang="sv">fast</span> — although
- <span lang="sv">innan</span> — before

Examples:

- <span lang="sv">Jag vet att hon inte arbetar idag.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Vi går ut om det inte regnar.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Han ringde när jag inte var hemma.</span>

## Not only <span lang="sv">inte</span>

Other small words that comment on the whole sentence take the same place:
<span lang="sv">aldrig</span> (never), <span lang="sv">alltid</span> (always),
<span lang="sv">kanske</span> (maybe), <span lang="sv">redan</span> (already).

<p lang="sv">Hon säger att hon <strong>aldrig äter</strong> kött.</p>

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Main clause">
	<p slot="de">Ich komme heute nicht.</p>
	<p slot="en">I am not coming today.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag kommer inte idag.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Subordinate clause">
	<p slot="de">… weil ich heute nicht komme.</p>
	<p slot="en">… because I am not coming today.</p>
	<p slot="sv">… eftersom jag inte kommer idag.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German also puts <span lang="de">nicht</span> before the verb in a subordinate
clause, but for a different reason: German moves the conjugated verb to the very
end. Swedish only moves it behind <span lang="sv">inte</span>, and the rest of the
sentence still follows: <span lang="sv">eftersom jag inte kommer <strong>idag</strong></span>.
English keeps the same order in both kinds of clause.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Jag stannar hemma eftersom jag mår inte bra."
	correct="Jag stannar hemma eftersom jag inte mår bra."
	lang="sv"
>
After <span lang="sv">eftersom</span>, <span lang="sv">att</span> or
<span lang="sv">om</span>, put <span lang="sv">inte</span> in front of the
verb.
</MistakeBox>

:::note[A safe default]
BIFF tells you where <span lang="sv">inte</span> goes. It is not the whole
grammar of subordinate clauses. In speech, Swedes sometimes keep main-clause
order after <span lang="sv">att</span>, especially when they state something
strongly. Until you reach level B2 (confident everyday use), BIFF is the rule
to follow.
:::
