<KeyTakeaway>
- <span lang="sv">som</span> introduces a clause that describes the noun right before it.
- <span lang="sv">att</span> introduces a clause that states what someone says, thinks, or
  knows.
- <span lang="sv">att</span> before a bare verb form is not a clause at all: it is the
  <span lang="sv">infinitivmärke</span>.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

English hands one word, *that*, to three different jobs. Swedish splits them
between <span lang="sv">att</span> and <span lang="sv">som</span>, and German splits them the
same way into *dass* and *der*/*die*/*das*. The distinction is therefore
familiar; only the labels are new.

## Use this now

| What the clause does | Word | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| describes the noun before it | <span lang="sv">som</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag har en kompis som bor i Malmö.</span> |
| states a thought, a statement, or a fact | <span lang="sv">att</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag tycker att huset är för dyrt.</span> |
| adds a bare verb with no new subject | <span lang="sv">att</span> + infinitive | <span lang="sv">Vi planerar att flytta.</span> |

## Core rule

<span lang="sv">att</span> and <span lang="sv">som</span> compete only in the first two rows,
because only those two open a real clause with its own
<span lang="sv">subjekt</span> and <span lang="sv">finit verb</span>:

<SentenceSchema
	caption="Two clause types, one opening field"
	rowHeader="Clause type"
	columns={['bisatsinledare', 'subjekt', 'satsadverbial', 'finit verb', 'objekt / predikativ', 'adverbial']}
	rows={[
		{ label: 'Content clause', cells: ['att', 'han', 'inte', 'kommer', '', 'idag'] },
		{ label: 'Relative, som = subject', cells: ['som', '', 'inte', 'bor', '', 'här'] },
		{ label: 'Relative, som = object', cells: ['som', 'jag', '', 'läste', '', 'igår'] },
	]}
/>

Read the empty <span lang="sv">subjekt</span> cell in row two: <span lang="sv">som</span> is
the subject of its own clause, so nothing else fills that field.
<span lang="sv">att</span> never takes a role inside the clause, so a subject always
follows it.

The third use has no row here on purpose. <span lang="sv">att göra</span> has no subject
and no <span lang="sv">finit verb</span>, so it is an infinitive phrase, not a clause.

## att before an infinitive

The <span lang="sv">infinitivmärke</span> appears after most ordinary verbs, after
adjectives, after nouns, and after prepositions:

- <span lang="sv">Jag gillar **att spela** gitarr.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Det är roligt **att resa**.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Vi har tid **att äta** först.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Han gick ut utan **att säga** något.</span>

Modal verbs are the systematic exception: they take a bare infinitive.
[Modal verbs](../../verb-patterns-voice/modal-verbs/) owns that pattern, and
[Future forms](../../verbs-time-forms/future-forms/) owns why
<span lang="sv">kommer att</span> keeps the marker while <span lang="sv">ska</span> drops it.

## att before a clause

Here <span lang="sv">att</span> is a <span lang="sv">bisatsinledare</span>. The clause answers
*what* after a verb of saying, thinking, or knowing:

- <span lang="sv">Hon berättade **att läraren var sjuk**.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Jag vet **att tåget är sent**.</span>

[Reported speech](../../mood-politeness-reports/reported-speech/) owns the
reporting frame around it, and [Subordinate clauses](../subordinate-clauses/)
owns the word order inside it.

## som after a noun

<span lang="sv">som</span> is a <span lang="sv">relativpronomen</span>. It points back at the
word in front of it and adds information about that word:

- <span lang="sv">Jag har en kompis **som bor i Malmö**.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Huset **som vi ska köpa** är gammalt.</span>

It is invariable: no gender, no number, no case.
[Relative pronouns](../../pronouns-roles/relative-pronouns/) owns the full set
with <span lang="sv">vilket</span>, <span lang="sv">vars</span>, and preposition placement.

## Where the choice is real

After a noun or an indefinite pronoun such as <span lang="sv">något</span>, both words
fit the slot and only the meaning decides:

- <span lang="sv">Det är något **som** min man verkligen vill göra.</span> — the clause
  describes <span lang="sv">något</span>.
- <span lang="sv">Jag tycker **att** det blir bra.</span> — the clause is the content of
  <span lang="sv">tycker</span>.

Test it by replacement. If *which* or *who* fits the English version, use
<span lang="sv">som</span>. If only *that* fits and you could drop it, use
<span lang="sv">att</span>.

## When the word disappears

| Word | May be dropped? |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">att</span> as <span lang="sv">bisatsinledare</span> | Often, in everyday speech: <span lang="sv">Jag tror (att) han kommer.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">att</span> as <span lang="sv">infinitivmärke</span> | No, apart from a few verbs where it is optional: <span lang="sv">Hon började (att) läsa.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">som</span> | Only as the object of its clause, never as its subject. |

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Relative clause">
	<p slot="de">Ich habe einen Freund, der in Malmö wohnt.</p>
	<p slot="en">I have a friend who lives in Malmö.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag har en kompis som bor i Malmö.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Content clause">
	<p slot="de">Ich glaube, dass das Haus zu teuer ist.</p>
	<p slot="en">I think that the house is too expensive.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag tycker att huset är för dyrt.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Infinitive after a preposition">
	<p slot="de">Ich bin daran interessiert, Schwedisch zu lernen.</p>
	<p slot="en">I am interested in learning Swedish.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag är intresserad av att lära mig svenska.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German marks all three the same way Swedish does. English is the odd one: it
merges the first two under *that* and switches to an *-ing* form in the third.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Jag har en vän att bor i Stockholm."
	correct="Jag har en vän som bor i Stockholm."
	lang="sv"
>
English *that* covers a describing clause too, so it pulls
<span lang="sv">att</span> into a slot that belongs to <span lang="sv">som</span>.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Describe a thing → <span lang="sv">som</span>. Report a thought →
<span lang="sv">att</span>. Bare verb → <span lang="sv">att</span>.
</MemoryTip>
