<KeyTakeaway>
- Learn a preposition inside a whole expression, not as a translation of a
  German or English one.
- A verb and its fixed preposition are one item: <Sv>vänta på</Sv>,
  <Sv>gifta sig med</Sv>.
- <Sv>Om en vecka</Sv> counts forward from now, <Sv>för en vecka sedan</Sv>
  counts back.
</KeyTakeaway>

Swedish prepositions are best learned in complete expressions. A German or
English preposition can be a useful hint, but it is not a reliable one-to-one
translation.

## Place: where something is

| Preposition | Typical idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>i</Sv> | in a country, city, area, or enclosed place | <Sv>Malmö ligger i Skåne.</Sv> |
| <Sv>på</Sv> | on, at, or in many activity/place expressions | <Sv>Hon väntar på caféet.</Sv> |
| <Sv>vid</Sv> | by, at, next to | <Sv>Bänken står vid dörren.</Sv> |
| <Sv>under</Sv> | under | <Sv>Väskan ligger under stolen.</Sv> |
| <Sv>över</Sv> | over, above, across | <Sv>Bron går över älven.</Sv> |
| <Sv>mellan</Sv> | between | <Sv>Caféet ligger mellan biblioteket och torget.</Sv> |
| <Sv>mittemot</Sv> | opposite, across from | <Sv>Apoteket ligger mittemot hotellet.</Sv> |

## Direction: where something goes

| Preposition | Typical idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>från</Sv> | from | <Sv>Bussen kommer från Lund.</Sv> |
| <Sv>till</Sv> | to | <Sv>Vi går till biblioteket.</Sv> |
| <Sv>mot</Sv> | towards, in the direction of | <Sv>Spårvagnen mot centrum stannar här.</Sv> |

Use <Sv>till</Sv> when the destination is the goal of the
movement. Use <Sv>mot</Sv> when the phrase gives a direction,
line, sign, or route.

## Common fixed choices

Some frequent choices must simply be stored as phrases:

	<p slot="en">We are sitting on the train to Kalmar.</p>

Notice that German <span lang="de">in</span> and English <span lang="en">in</span>
do not always map to Swedish <Sv>i</Sv>.

## Verbs with a fixed preposition

Many Swedish verbs require a specific preposition that cannot be predicted from
German or English. Learn the verb and its preposition as one item.

| Swedish | English | German |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>vänta på</Sv> | to wait for | <span lang="de">warten auf</span> |
| <Sv>titta på</Sv> | to look at | <span lang="de">ansehen, schauen auf</span> |
| <Sv>lyssna på</Sv> | to listen to | <span lang="de">hören, zuhören</span> |
| <Sv>tänka på</Sv> | to think about | <span lang="de">denken an</span> |
| <Sv>tro på</Sv> | to believe in | <span lang="de">glauben an</span> |
| <Sv>svara på</Sv> | to answer | <span lang="de">antworten auf</span> |
| <Sv>bero på</Sv> | to depend on | <span lang="de">abhängen von</span> |
| <Sv>leta efter</Sv> | to look for | <span lang="de">suchen nach</span> |
| <Sv>längta efter</Sv> | to long for | <span lang="de">sich sehnen nach</span> |
| <Sv>be om</Sv> | to ask for | <span lang="de">bitten um</span> |
| <Sv>skratta åt</Sv> | to laugh at | <span lang="de">lachen über</span> |
| <Sv>vara intresserad av</Sv> | to be interested in | <span lang="de">interessiert an</span> |
| <Sv>bli kär i</Sv> | to fall in love with | <span lang="de">sich verlieben in</span> |
| <Sv>gifta sig med</Sv> | to marry | <span lang="de">heiraten</span> |

Two patterns are worth noticing. First, <Sv>på</Sv> is by far the
most common preposition in these constructions, so it is the best guess when
you cannot recall the right one. Second, some Swedish verbs need a preposition
where German uses none at all: <Sv>gifta sig med någon</Sv>
against <span lang="de">jemanden heiraten</span>.

### Where the preposition ends up

In a relative clause or a question, the preposition does not move to the front
with its noun. It stays at the end:

- <Sv>Bussen som vi väntar på är sen.</Sv> — *The bus we are
  waiting for is late.*
- <Sv>Var kommer du ifrån?</Sv> — *Where do you come from?*

German does the opposite (<span lang="de">der Bus, auf den wir warten</span>),
so this is worth practising. See
[relative pronouns](../relative-pronouns/).

### Preposition or particle?

<Sv>Titta på</Sv> and <Sv>tycka om</Sv> look alike
but behave differently. In <Sv>titta på</Sv> the preposition is
unstressed and only introduces the object. In <Sv>tycka om</Sv>
the particle is stressed and belongs to the verb, which also changes where
<Sv>inte</Sv> goes:

- <Sv>Jag tittar inte på henne.</Sv> — preposition
- <Sv>Jag tycker inte om henne.</Sv> — particle

See [particle verbs](../../verbs/particle-verbs/) for the
stress test and the word-order consequences.

## Time: when something happens

| Preposition | Typical idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>på</Sv> | a coming weekday, or a part of the day | <Sv>Vi ses på måndag.</Sv> |
| <Sv>i</Sv> | in fixed expressions for today and yesterday | <Sv>Jag kommer i kväll.</Sv> |
| <Sv>om</Sv> | after a period, counted from now | <Sv>Vi ses om en vecka.</Sv> |
| <Sv>för … sedan</Sv> | a period back from now | <Sv>Jag flyttade hit för ett år sedan.</Sv> |
| <Sv>i</Sv> | how long something lasted | <Sv>Jag bodde i Lund i tre år.</Sv> |
| <Sv>under</Sv> | during | <Sv>Vi reser under sommaren.</Sv> |
| <Sv>mellan</Sv> | between two points in time | <Sv>Mötet är mellan två och tre.</Sv> |

The pair <Sv>om</Sv> and <Sv>för … sedan</Sv> is
the one to get right first, because German uses <span lang="de">in</span> and
<span lang="de">vor</span> for both directions while Swedish changes the whole
construction.

<Sv>På måndag</Sv> points forward to the coming Monday, and
<Sv>i måndags</Sv> points back to the previous one.
<Sv>På måndagar</Sv> with a plural describes something habitual.

For clock times, see
[telling time](../../../vocabulary/telling-time/).

## In a sentence

Place and direction phrases often appear near the end of a neutral main clause:

<p lang="sv">Jag bor på Sveavägen.</p>

If a place or time phrase moves to the front, Swedish still keeps the finite
verb in position two:

<p lang="sv">I morgon åker jag till Kalmar.</p>

See [V2](../../sentence-structure/v2/) for the word-order
rule.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- Do not translate German or English prepositions mechanically.
	- Do not use <Sv>i</Sv> as the default for every German
	  <span lang="de">in</span>.
	- After a fronted place or time phrase, keep the finite verb second:
	  <Sv>I morgon åker jag</Sv>, not
	  <Sv>I morgon jag åker</Sv>.
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag gifte mig henne.</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag gifte mig med henne.</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag flyttade hit för ett år.</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag flyttade hit för ett år sedan.</Sv>
	- Do not use <Sv>i</Sv> for a future period.
	  <Sv>Om en vecka</Sv> means *in a week from now*.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Store the whole expression first:
<Sv><strong>bo på en gata</strong></Sv>,
<Sv><strong>vänta på caféet</strong></Sv>,
<Sv><strong>gå till biblioteket</strong></Sv>,
<Sv><strong>vänta på bussen</strong></Sv>. A verb and its
preposition belong together in the same way a noun belongs with its gender.
</MemoryTip>
