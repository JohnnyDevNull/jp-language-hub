<KeyTakeaway>
- Choose <Sv>den</Sv> for an en-word and <Sv>det</Sv> for an ett-word, then add
  <Sv>här</Sv> for near and <Sv>där</Sv> for far.
- The noun after <Sv>den här</Sv> and <Sv>den där</Sv> takes its definite form:
  <Sv>den här nyckeln</Sv>.
- Formal <Sv>denna</Sv>, <Sv>detta</Sv>, and <Sv>dessa</Sv> leave the noun
  indefinite: <Sv>denna bok</Sv>.
</KeyTakeaway>

Swedish often uses <Sv>här</Sv> and <Sv>där</Sv>
with demonstratives to distinguish “this here” from “that there”. The form
before the noun follows the noun's gender.

## Core forms

| Distance | en-word | ett-word |
| --- | --- | --- |
| near the speaker | <Sv>den här jackan</Sv> | <Sv>det här paraplyet</Sv> |
| farther away | <Sv>den där cykeln</Sv> | <Sv>det där biblioteket</Sv> |

Use <Sv>den</Sv> with en-words and <Sv>det</Sv>
with ett-words:

- <Sv>den här nyckeln</Sv> — this key here
- <Sv>det här mejlet</Sv> — this email here
- <Sv>den där cykeln</Sv> — that bike there
- <Sv>det där biblioteket</Sv> — that library there

## The noun is definite

After <Sv>den här</Sv>, <Sv>det här</Sv>,
<Sv>den där</Sv>, and <Sv>det där</Sv>, the noun
normally uses the definite form:

| Indefinite | Demonstrative phrase |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>en nyckel</Sv> | <Sv>den här nyckeln</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett paraply</Sv> | <Sv>det här paraplyet</Sv> |
| <Sv>en cykel</Sv> | <Sv>den där cykeln</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett bibliotek</Sv> | <Sv>det där biblioteket</Sv> |

This is one reason the four noun forms are useful to learn early.

## Standing alone

When the noun is not spoken directly after the demonstrative, Swedish normally
uses <Sv>det här</Sv> or <Sv>det där</Sv> as a
general “this” or “that”:

<LanguageComparison title="This here">
	<p slot="de">Das hier ist mein Platz.</p>
	<p slot="en">This is my seat.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Det här är min plats.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="That there">
	<p slot="de">Das dort ist unser Bus.</p>
	<p slot="en">That is our bus.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Det där är vår buss.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

In <Sv>Det här är min plats</Sv>, the demonstrative is not
attached to the noun phrase <Sv>min plats</Sv>. It points to the
situation as “this”.

## With adjectives

If an adjective appears inside the demonstrative noun phrase, Swedish uses the
definite adjective form and the noun still remains definite:

| Phrase | Pattern |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>den här varma jackan</Sv> | <Sv>den här</Sv> + definite adjective + definite noun |
| <Sv>det där nya biblioteket</Sv> | <Sv>det där</Sv> + definite adjective + definite noun |

This is closely related to [double definiteness](../../adjectives-adverbs/agreement/#definite-noun-phrases-and-double-definiteness).

## The formal alternative: denna, detta, dessa

<Sv>Denna</Sv>, <Sv>detta</Sv>, and
<Sv>dessa</Sv> mean the same as <Sv>den här</Sv>,
<Sv>det här</Sv>, and <Sv>de här</Sv>, but they
belong to written and formal Swedish: reports, notices, contracts, academic
prose.

They behave differently in one important way — the noun after them stays
**indefinite**:

| Everyday | Formal |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>den här boken</Sv> | <Sv>denna bok</Sv> |
| <Sv>det här huset</Sv> | <Sv>detta hus</Sv> |
| <Sv>de här böckerna</Sv> | <Sv>dessa böcker</Sv> |

- <Sv>Denna fråga är viktig.</Sv> — *This question is
  important.*
- <Sv>Detta beslut gäller från januari.</Sv>

An adjective in between takes the definite <Sv>-a</Sv> form, as
always: <Sv>denna viktiga fråga</Sv>.

Referring to a male person, formal Swedish also has
<Sv>denne</Sv>: <Sv>denne författare</Sv>. Like the
masculine <Sv>-e</Sv> on adjectives, this is worth recognizing
rather than using.

<RuleBox title="Which one to use">
	In speech and everyday writing, use <Sv>den här</Sv> with a
	definite noun. Reach for <Sv>denna</Sv> only in formal writing
	— and then remember to leave the noun indefinite.
</RuleBox>

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <Sv>den här nyckel</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>den här nyckeln</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>det här jackan</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>den här jackan</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>den här varm jackan</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>den här varma jackan</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>denna boken</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>denna bok</Sv> — the noun stays indefinite after
	  <Sv>denna</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>detta bok</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>denna bok</Sv> — <Sv>bok</Sv> is an
	  en-word
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
First choose <Sv>den</Sv> or <Sv>det</Sv> from the
noun's gender. Then use the noun's definite form:
<Sv><strong>en nyckel → den här nyckeln</strong></Sv>.
</MemoryTip>
