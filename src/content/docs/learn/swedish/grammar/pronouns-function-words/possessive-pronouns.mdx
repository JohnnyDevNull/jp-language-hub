<KeyTakeaway>
- The possessed noun decides the form: <Sv>min bil</Sv>, <Sv>mitt hus</Sv>,
  <Sv>mina böcker</Sv>.
- <Sv>hans</Sv>, <Sv>hennes</Sv>, and <Sv>deras</Sv> never change form.
- With a third-person subject, <Sv>sin</Sv> means the subject's own thing;
  <Sv>hans</Sv> or <Sv>hennes</Sv> points to somebody else.
</KeyTakeaway>

Swedish possessive pronouns often change form according to what is possessed.
For many forms, the choice is the same pattern learners already know from
<Sv>en</Sv> nouns, <Sv>ett</Sv> nouns, and plural
nouns.

## Core pattern

Choose the possessive form from the noun that follows:

| Possessor | <Sv>en</Sv> noun | <Sv>ett</Sv> noun | Plural noun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | <Sv>min bil</Sv> | <Sv>mitt hus</Sv> | <Sv>mina böcker</Sv> |
| you (singular) | <Sv>din bil</Sv> | <Sv>ditt hus</Sv> | <Sv>dina böcker</Sv> |
| he / she / they, own | <Sv>sin bil</Sv> | <Sv>sitt hus</Sv> | <Sv>sina böcker</Sv> |
| we | <Sv>vår bil</Sv> | <Sv>vårt hus</Sv> | <Sv>våra böcker</Sv> |
| you (plural) | <Sv>er bil</Sv> | <Sv>ert hus</Sv> | <Sv>era böcker</Sv> |

<MemoryTip title="The possessed noun decides">
In <Sv>min bil</Sv>, <Sv>mitt hus</Sv>, and
<Sv>mina böcker</Sv>, the possessor is always “I”. The form
changes because <Sv>bil</Sv>, <Sv>hus</Sv>, and
<Sv>böcker</Sv> are different noun forms.
</MemoryTip>

## Forms that do not change

Some Swedish possessives do not change for <Sv>en</Sv>,
<Sv>ett</Sv>, or plural:

| Possessor | Form | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| he | <Sv>hans</Sv> | <Sv>hans bil</Sv>, <Sv>hans hus</Sv>, <Sv>hans böcker</Sv> |
| she | <Sv>hennes</Sv> | <Sv>hennes bil</Sv>, <Sv>hennes hus</Sv>, <Sv>hennes böcker</Sv> |
| they | <Sv>deras</Sv> | <Sv>deras bil</Sv>, <Sv>deras hus</Sv>, <Sv>deras böcker</Sv> |

These forms point to a possessor, but they do not show whether the possessed
thing is an <Sv>en</Sv> noun, an <Sv>ett</Sv> noun,
or plural.

## Min, din, and sin

<Sv>Min</Sv>, <Sv>din</Sv>, and
<Sv>sin</Sv> follow the same noun pattern:

| Noun type | My | Your | Own |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>en</Sv> noun | <Sv>min jacka</Sv> | <Sv>din jacka</Sv> | <Sv>sin jacka</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett</Sv> noun | <Sv>mitt rum</Sv> | <Sv>ditt rum</Sv> | <Sv>sitt rum</Sv> |
| Plural noun | <Sv>mina skor</Sv> | <Sv>dina skor</Sv> | <Sv>sina skor</Sv> |

The grammar pattern is shared, but <Sv>sin</Sv>,
<Sv>sitt</Sv>, and <Sv>sina</Sv> have an extra
meaning: they refer back to the subject of the same clause.

## Sin, sitt, and sina

Use <Sv>sin</Sv>, <Sv>sitt</Sv>, or
<Sv>sina</Sv> when a third-person subject possesses something
and the possessive means “his/her/their own”.

| Swedish | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>Anna tar sin jacka.</Sv> | Anna takes her own jacket. |
| <Sv>Anna tar hennes jacka.</Sv> | Anna takes another woman's jacket. |
| <Sv>Erik säljer sitt hus.</Sv> | Erik sells his own house. |
| <Sv>Erik säljer hans hus.</Sv> | Erik sells another man's house. |

See the full [sin, sitt, and sina](../sin-sitt-sina/) reference for the
clause-boundary rule and more examples.

## Direct comparison

German and English do not mark the same “own versus somebody else's”
distinction in the possessive form:

<LanguageComparison title="First person">
	<p slot="de">Ich nehme meine Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">I take my jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag tar min jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Second person">
	<p slot="de">Du nimmst deine Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">You take your jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Du tar din jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Third person, own thing">
	<p slot="de">Anna nimmt ihre Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">Anna takes her jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Anna tar sin jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

The German and English third-person examples can be ambiguous without context.
Swedish usually uses <Sv>sin</Sv> when the jacket belongs to
Anna herself.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <Sv>mitt bil</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>min bil</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>min hus</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>mitt hus</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>din böcker</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>dina böcker</Sv>
	- ❌ Choosing <Sv>min</Sv>, <Sv>din</Sv>, or
	  <Sv>sin</Sv> from the owner. Choose the form from the thing
	  that is owned.
</MistakeBox>
