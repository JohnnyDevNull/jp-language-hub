<KeyTakeaway>
- Learn a preposition inside a whole expression, not as a translation of a
  German or English one.
- A verb and its fixed preposition are one item: <span lang="sv">vänta på</span>,
  <span lang="sv">gifta sig med</span>.
- <span lang="sv">Om en vecka</span> counts forward from now, <span lang="sv">för en vecka sedan</span>
  counts back.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish prepositions are best learned in complete expressions. A German or
English preposition can be a useful hint, but it is not a reliable one-to-one
translation.

## Use this now

When you need a preposition, choose by the job the phrase is doing first. Only
translate from German or English after that.

| Need | Start with | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| inside a country, city, area, or room | <span lang="sv">i</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag bor i Malmö.</span> |
| at an activity place, on a street, on transport | <span lang="sv">på</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag bor på Sveavägen.</span> |
| next to or by something | <span lang="sv">vid</span> | <span lang="sv">Vi ses vid stationen.</span> |
| movement to a destination | <span lang="sv">till</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag går till biblioteket.</span> |
| direction, route, or sign | <span lang="sv">mot</span> | <span lang="sv">Tåget mot Göteborg.</span> |
| a future period counted from now | <span lang="sv">om</span> | <span lang="sv">Vi ses om en vecka.</span> |
| a past period counted back from now | <span lang="sv">för ... sedan</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag kom hit för en timme sedan.</span> |
| a verb phrase you have heard before | store the whole phrase | <span lang="sv">vänta på bussen</span>, <span lang="sv">leta efter nycklarna</span> |

For a quick check, ask:

1. Is it place, direction, time, or a fixed verb phrase?
2. If it is place, is the Swedish expression usually <span lang="sv">i</span> or <span lang="sv">på</span>?
3. If a verb is involved, have you learned the verb together with its
   preposition?

## Place: where something is

| Preposition | Typical idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">i</span> | in a country, city, area, or enclosed place | <span lang="sv">Malmö ligger i Skåne.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">på</span> | on, at, or in many activity/place expressions | <span lang="sv">Vi träffas på caféet.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">vid</span> | by, at, next to | <span lang="sv">Bänken står vid dörren.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">under</span> | under | <span lang="sv">Väskan ligger under stolen.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">över</span> | over, above, across | <span lang="sv">Bron går över älven.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">mellan</span> | between | <span lang="sv">Caféet ligger mellan biblioteket och torget.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">mittemot</span> | opposite, across from | <span lang="sv">Apoteket ligger mittemot hotellet.</span> |

## Direction: where something goes

| Preposition | Typical idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">från</span> | from | <span lang="sv">Bussen kommer från Lund.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">till</span> | to | <span lang="sv">Vi går till biblioteket.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">mot</span> | towards, in the direction of | <span lang="sv">Spårvagnen mot centrum stannar här.</span> |

Use <span lang="sv">till</span> when the destination is the goal of the
movement. Use <span lang="sv">mot</span> when the phrase gives a direction,
line, sign, or route.

## Common fixed choices

Some frequent choices must simply be stored as phrases:

	<p slot="en">We are sitting on the train to Kalmar.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Vi sitter på tåget till Kalmar.</p>

Notice that German <span lang="de">in</span> and English <span lang="en">in</span>
do not always map to Swedish <span lang="sv">i</span>.

## Verbs with a fixed preposition

Many Swedish verbs require a specific preposition that cannot be predicted from
German or English. Learn the verb and its preposition as one item.

| Swedish | English | German |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">vänta på</span> | to wait for | <span lang="de">warten auf</span> |
| <span lang="sv">titta på</span> | to look at | <span lang="de">ansehen, schauen auf</span> |
| <span lang="sv">lyssna på</span> | to listen to | <span lang="de">hören, zuhören</span> |
| <span lang="sv">tänka på</span> | to think about | <span lang="de">denken an</span> |
| <span lang="sv">tro på</span> | to believe in | <span lang="de">glauben an</span> |
| <span lang="sv">svara på</span> | to answer | <span lang="de">antworten auf</span> |
| <span lang="sv">bero på</span> | to depend on | <span lang="de">abhängen von</span> |
| <span lang="sv">leta efter</span> | to look for | <span lang="de">suchen nach</span> |
| <span lang="sv">längta efter</span> | to long for | <span lang="de">sich sehnen nach</span> |
| <span lang="sv">be om</span> | to ask for | <span lang="de">bitten um</span> |
| <span lang="sv">skratta åt</span> | to laugh at | <span lang="de">lachen über</span> |
| <span lang="sv">vara intresserad av</span> | to be interested in | <span lang="de">interessiert an</span> |
| <span lang="sv">bli kär i</span> | to fall in love with | <span lang="de">sich verlieben in</span> |
| <span lang="sv">gifta sig med</span> | to marry | <span lang="de">heiraten</span> |

Two patterns are worth noticing. First, <span lang="sv">på</span> is very common in these
constructions, so many high-frequency verb phrases use it. Second, some
Swedish verbs need a preposition where German uses none at all:
<span lang="sv">gifta sig med någon</span> against
<span lang="de">jemanden heiraten</span>.

### Where the preposition ends up

In many everyday relative clauses and questions, the preposition does not move
to the front with its noun. It stays at the end:

- <span lang="sv">Bussen som vi väntar på är sen.</span> — *The bus we are
  waiting for is late.*
- <span lang="sv">Var kommer du ifrån?</span> — *Where do you come from?*

German does the opposite (<span lang="de">der Bus, auf den wir warten</span>),
so this is worth practising. See
[relative pronouns](../../pronouns-roles/relative-pronouns/).

### Preposition or particle?

<span lang="sv">Titta på</span> and <span lang="sv">tycka om</span> look alike
but behave differently. In <span lang="sv">titta på</span>, the preposition is
unstressed and introduces the object. In <span lang="sv">tycka om</span>, the
stressed particle belongs to the verb and changes the verb phrase's meaning:

- <span lang="sv">Jag tittar inte på henne.</span> — preposition
- <span lang="sv">Jag tycker inte om henne.</span> — particle

See [particle verbs](../../verb-patterns-voice/particle-verbs/) for the
stress test and the word-order consequences.

## Time: when something happens

| Preposition | Typical idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">på</span> | a coming weekday, or a part of the day | <span lang="sv">Vi ses på måndag.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">i</span> | in fixed expressions for today and yesterday | <span lang="sv">Jag kommer i kväll.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">om</span> | after a period, counted from now | <span lang="sv">Vi ses om en vecka.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">för … sedan</span> | a period back from now | <span lang="sv">Jag flyttade hit för ett år sedan.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">i</span> | how long something lasted | <span lang="sv">Jag bodde i Lund i tre år.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">under</span> | during | <span lang="sv">Vi reser under sommaren.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">mellan</span> | between two points in time | <span lang="sv">Mötet är mellan två och tre.</span> |

The pair <span lang="sv">om</span> and <span lang="sv">för … sedan</span> is
the one to get right first, because German uses <span lang="de">in</span> and
<span lang="de">vor</span> for both directions while Swedish changes the whole
construction.

<span lang="sv">På måndag</span> points forward to the coming Monday, and
<span lang="sv">i måndags</span> points back to the previous one.
<span lang="sv">På måndagar</span> with a plural describes something habitual.

For clock times, see
[telling time](../../../vocabulary/telling-time/).

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- Do not translate German or English prepositions mechanically.
	- Do not use <span lang="sv">i</span> as the default for every German
	  <span lang="de">in</span>.
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Jag gifte mig henne.</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Jag gifte mig med henne.</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Jag flyttade hit för ett år.</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Jag flyttade hit för ett år sedan.</span>
	- Do not use <span lang="sv">i</span> for a future period.
	  <span lang="sv">Om en vecka</span> means *in a week from now*.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Store the whole expression first:
<span lang="sv"><strong>bo på en gata</strong></span>,
<span lang="sv"><strong>träffas på caféet</strong></span>,
<span lang="sv"><strong>gå till biblioteket</strong></span>,
<span lang="sv"><strong>vänta på bussen</strong></span>. A verb and its
preposition belong together in the same way a noun belongs with its gender.
</MemoryTip>
