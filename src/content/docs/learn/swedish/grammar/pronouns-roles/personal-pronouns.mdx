Swedish personal pronouns change mainly according to subject or object role.
This is a compact form-and-role lookup, not a Swedish version of the productive
German nominative–accusative–dative noun system.

## Use this now

| Role | Ask | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| subject | Who performs or experiences the clause? | <Sv>Hon väntar.</Sv> |
| object | Who or what does the action affect? | <Sv>Jag ser henne.</Sv> |
| after a preposition | Which form follows words such as <Sv>med</Sv> or <Sv>till</Sv>? | <Sv>med henne</Sv> |

## Core forms

| Meaning | Subject | Object / after a preposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I / me | <Sv>jag</Sv> | <Sv>mig</Sv> |
| you (singular) | <Sv>du</Sv> | <Sv>dig</Sv> |
| he / him | <Sv>han</Sv> | <Sv>honom</Sv> |
| she / her | <Sv>hon</Sv> | <Sv>henne</Sv> |
| singular gender-neutral person | <Sv>hen</Sv> | <Sv>hen</Sv> |
| it, en-word | <Sv>den</Sv> | <Sv>den</Sv> |
| it, ett-word | <Sv>det</Sv> | <Sv>det</Sv> |
| people in general | <Sv>man</Sv> | <Sv>en</Sv> |
| we / us | <Sv>vi</Sv> | <Sv>oss</Sv> |
| you (plural) | <Sv>ni</Sv> | <Sv>er</Sv> |
| they / them | <Sv>de</Sv> | <Sv>dem</Sv> |

<Sv>Ni</Sv> is primarily plural. It can address one person in some service
encounters or deliberately formal contexts, but it is not a blanket Swedish
equivalent of polite German <span lang="de">Sie</span>. <Sv>Du</Sv> is the
normal singular default in modern Swedish.

## Subject or object

- <Sv>Hon ringer mig.</Sv> — She calls me.
- <Sv>Jag hjälper honom.</Sv> — I help him.
- <Sv>De väntar på oss.</Sv> — They are waiting for us.
- <Sv>Vi åker med dem.</Sv> — We travel with them.

Prepositions take the object form: <Sv>med mig</Sv>, <Sv>till dig</Sv>,
<Sv>från henne</Sv>.

## Referring to things

Use <Sv>den</Sv> for an en-word and <Sv>det</Sv> for an ett-word when a
personal pronoun refers to a specific thing:

- <Sv>Var är bilen? Den står ute.</Sv>
- <Sv>Var är brevet? Det ligger här.</Sv>

This reference use is separate from demonstrative phrases such as
<Sv>den här bilen</Sv>.

## Spoken *dom* and written *de/dem*

In standard writing, use <Sv>de</Sv> as subject and <Sv>dem</Sv> as object:
<Sv>De såg oss</Sv>; <Sv>vi såg dem</Sv>. In speech, both are normally
pronounced <Sv>dom</Sv>, and <Sv>dom</Sv> also appears in informal writing.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="After a preposition">
	<p slot="de">mit ihr</p>
	<p slot="en">with her</p>
	<p slot="sv">med henne</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German has a broader productive case system across articles, adjectives, and
nouns. Swedish keeps the subject–object contrast mainly in personal pronouns.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
- <Sv>❌ med jag</Sv> → <Sv>✅ med mig</Sv>
- <Sv>❌ henne kommer</Sv> → <Sv>✅ hon kommer</Sv>
- In standard writing, use <Sv>de</Sv> as subject and <Sv>dem</Sv> as object.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Learn each pair as a small rhythm: <Sv><strong>jag–mig, du–dig, hon–henne,
vi–oss, de–dem</strong></Sv>.
</MemoryTip>
