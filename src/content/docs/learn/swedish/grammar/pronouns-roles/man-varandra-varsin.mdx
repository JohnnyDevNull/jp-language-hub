This page collects the pronouns Swedish uses to talk about people in general
and about people in relation to each other.

## man for general statements

<Sv>Man</Sv> works like German <span lang="de">man</span> when both are
pronouns: it makes a statement about people in general without naming anyone.

- <Sv>Man får inte parkera här.</Sv> — *You may not park here.*
- <Sv>Så säger man i Sverige.</Sv> — *That is what people say in
  Sweden.*

English has no single equivalent, which is why English speakers tend to reach
for a passive instead. In Swedish, <Sv>man</Sv> is often the more
natural choice.

<Sv>Man</Sv> has an object form, a genitive form, and a separate reflexive
possessive:

| Function | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| subject | <Sv>man</Sv> | <Sv>Man vet aldrig.</Sv> |
| object | <Sv>en</Sv> | <Sv>Det gör en glad.</Sv> |
| genitive / ordinary possessive | <Sv>ens</Sv> | <Sv>Det påverkar ens hälsa.</Sv> |
| reflexive possessive | <Sv>sin / sitt / sina</Sv> | <Sv>Man måste ta sitt ansvar.</Sv> |

Use <Sv>ens</Sv> for general “one's” possession when the possessor is not the
subject owning something inside that same clause. When <Sv>man</Sv> is the
subject and the possession points back to it, use the reflexive
<Sv>sin / sitt / sina</Sv>: <Sv>Man måste ta sitt ansvar</Sv>. See
[*sin, sitt, sina*](../sin-sitt-sina/) for the clause-boundary rule.

<RuleBox title="Do not confuse man with a noun">
	<Sv>Man</Sv> as a pronoun is unstressed and never takes an
	article. <Sv>En man</Sv> with an article is the noun *a man*.
	Only the article and the stress separate them.
</RuleBox>

## varandra for "each other"

<Sv>Varandra</Sv> is invariable and never acts as a subject. It can be the
object of a verb or the complement after a preposition. Its genitive is
<Sv>varandras</Sv>:

- <Sv>De hjälper varandra.</Sv> — *They help each other.*
- <Sv>De talar med varandra.</Sv> — *They talk to each other.*
- <Sv>Vi läser varandras texter.</Sv> — *We read each other's
  texts.*

Many verbs express the same idea with an <Sv>-s</Sv> form
instead:

- <Sv>De träffas varje vecka.</Sv> = <Sv>De träffar
  varandra varje vecka.</Sv>
- <Sv>Vi ses!</Sv>

See [passive](../../verb-patterns-voice/passive/) for that
<Sv>-s</Sv>.

Because <Sv>varandra</Sv> is an object, it may still be fronted under the V2
rule: <Sv>Varandra hjälper de</Sv> is marked but grammatical (“Each other is
who they help”). Front position does not turn it into the subject.

## var sin: one each

<Sv>Var sin</Sv> and <Sv>var sitt</Sv> — also
written <Sv>varsin</Sv> and <Sv>varsitt</Sv> —
mean that each person has one of their own. The form follows the gender of the
thing possessed:

- <Sv>De fick var sin bok.</Sv> — *They each got a book.*
- <Sv>Barnen har var sitt rum.</Sv> — *The children each have
  their own room.*

## varje, var och en, and alla

| Swedish | Meaning | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>varje</Sv> | each, every | with an indefinite singular noun: <Sv>varje dag</Sv> |
| <Sv>var och en</Sv> | each one | stands alone: <Sv>Var och en fick ett äpple.</Sv> |
| <Sv>alla</Sv> | all, everyone | <Sv>alla barn</Sv>, <Sv>Alla kom.</Sv> |
| <Sv>allt</Sv> | everything | <Sv>Allt är klart.</Sv> |

For <Sv>någon</Sv> and <Sv>ingen</Sv>, see
[någon, något, ingen, and inget](../nagon-nagot-ingen-inget/).

## The som helst phrases

Adding <Sv>som helst</Sv> to a question word gives the
*any-at-all* meaning:

| Swedish | English |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>vem som helst</Sv> | anyone at all |
| <Sv>vad som helst</Sv> | anything at all |
| <Sv>när som helst</Sv> | any time |
| <Sv>var som helst</Sv> | anywhere |
| <Sv>hur som helst</Sv> | anyhow, in any case |

<Sv>Hur som helst</Sv> is also a very common discourse marker
meaning *anyway*.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <Sv>Man måste ta hans ansvar.</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Man måste ta sitt ansvar.</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Varandra hjälper.</Sv> when you mean “They help each other.”
	  ✅ <Sv>De hjälper varandra.</Sv> —
	  <Sv>varandra</Sv> cannot supply the subject
	- ❌ <Sv>varje dagen</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>varje dag</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>Man är trevlig</Sv> meaning *a man is nice*
	  ✅ <Sv>En man är trevlig.</Sv>
	</MistakeBox>

<Sv><strong>varandra</strong></Sv> for people to each other, and
<Sv><strong>var sin</strong></Sv> for one each. The
<Sv>som helst</Sv> phrases are built on question words, so they
come free once those are secure.
</MemoryTip>
