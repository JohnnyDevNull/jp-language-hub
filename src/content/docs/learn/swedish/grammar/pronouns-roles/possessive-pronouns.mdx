<KeyTakeaway>
- The possessed noun decides the form: <span lang="sv">min bil</span>, <span lang="sv">mitt hus</span>,
  <span lang="sv">mina böcker</span>.
- <span lang="sv">hans</span>, <span lang="sv">hennes</span>, <span lang="sv">hens</span>, and <span lang="sv">deras</span> never
  change form.
- The same forms can stand alone: <span lang="sv">Bilen är min</span>,
  <span lang="sv">Huset är mitt</span>, <span lang="sv">Böckerna är mina</span>.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish possessive pronouns often change form according to what is possessed.
For many forms, the choice is the same pattern learners already know from
<span lang="sv">en</span> nouns, <span lang="sv">ett</span> nouns, and plural
nouns.

## Use this now

| What you need | Choose by | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a form before a noun | the gender or number of the possessed noun | <span lang="sv">min bil, mitt hus, mina böcker</span> |
| a form without a following noun | the understood thing's gender or number | <span lang="sv">Bilen är min. Huset är mitt.</span> |
| a third-person subject's own thing | the separate subject-reflexive rule | <span lang="sv">Anna tar sin jacka.</span> |

## Core pattern

Choose the possessive form from the noun that follows:

| Possessor | <span lang="sv">en</span> noun | <span lang="sv">ett</span> noun | Plural noun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | <span lang="sv">min bil</span> | <span lang="sv">mitt hus</span> | <span lang="sv">mina böcker</span> |
| you (singular) | <span lang="sv">din bil</span> | <span lang="sv">ditt hus</span> | <span lang="sv">dina böcker</span> |
| he / she / they, own | <span lang="sv">sin bil</span> | <span lang="sv">sitt hus</span> | <span lang="sv">sina böcker</span> |
| we | <span lang="sv">vår bil</span> | <span lang="sv">vårt hus</span> | <span lang="sv">våra böcker</span> |
| you (plural) | <span lang="sv">er bil</span> | <span lang="sv">ert hus</span> | <span lang="sv">era böcker</span> |

## Forms that do not change

Some Swedish possessives do not change for <span lang="sv">en</span>,
<span lang="sv">ett</span>, or plural:

| Possessor | Form | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| he | <span lang="sv">hans</span> | <span lang="sv">hans bil</span>, <span lang="sv">hans hus</span>, <span lang="sv">hans böcker</span> |
| she | <span lang="sv">hennes</span> | <span lang="sv">hennes bil</span>, <span lang="sv">hennes hus</span>, <span lang="sv">hennes böcker</span> |
| gender-neutral singular | <span lang="sv">hens</span> | <span lang="sv">hens bil</span>, <span lang="sv">hens hus</span>, <span lang="sv">hens böcker</span> |
| they | <span lang="sv">deras</span> | <span lang="sv">deras bil</span>, <span lang="sv">deras hus</span>, <span lang="sv">deras böcker</span> |

These forms point to a possessor, but they do not show whether the possessed
thing is an <span lang="sv">en</span> noun, an <span lang="sv">ett</span> noun,
or plural.

## Forms that change with the possessed noun

The changing possessives follow the same en-word, ett-word, and plural pattern:

| Noun type | My | Your | Our | Your (plural) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">en</span> noun | <span lang="sv">min jacka</span> | <span lang="sv">din jacka</span> | <span lang="sv">vår jacka</span> | <span lang="sv">er jacka</span> |
| <span lang="sv">ett</span> noun | <span lang="sv">mitt rum</span> | <span lang="sv">ditt rum</span> | <span lang="sv">vårt rum</span> | <span lang="sv">ert rum</span> |
| Plural noun | <span lang="sv">mina skor</span> | <span lang="sv">dina skor</span> | <span lang="sv">våra skor</span> | <span lang="sv">era skor</span> |

## Standing alone

The possessive can replace the whole noun phrase. Its form still reflects the
understood noun:

- <span lang="sv">Vems bil är det? — Den är min.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Är huset ert? — Ja, det är vårt.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Vilka böcker är hennes? — De här är hennes.</span>

Unchanging <span lang="sv">hans</span>, <span lang="sv">hennes</span>, <span lang="sv">hens</span>, and <span lang="sv">deras</span>
stay unchanged here too: <span lang="sv">Bilen är hennes</span>, <span lang="sv">Huset är hennes</span>.

## The subject's own thing

With a third-person subject, first decide whether the thing belongs to that
same subject. If it does, Swedish normally uses <span lang="sv">sin, sitt,</span> or
<span lang="sv">sina</span>; otherwise it uses <span lang="sv">hans, hennes, hens,</span> or <span lang="sv">deras</span>.
The full [*sin, sitt, sina*](../sin-sitt-sina/) reference owns that meaning,
the clause boundary, and the detailed contrast.

## Direct comparison

German and English do not mark the same “own versus somebody else's”
distinction in the possessive form:

<LanguageComparison title="First person">
	<p slot="de">Ich nehme meine Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">I take my jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag tar min jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Second person">
	<p slot="de">Du nimmst deine Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">You take your jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Du tar din jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Third person, own thing">
	<p slot="de">Anna nimmt ihre Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">Anna takes her jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Anna tar sin jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

The German and English third-person examples can be ambiguous without context.
Swedish uses the separate subject-reflexive system when the jacket belongs to
Anna herself.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">mitt bil</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">min bil</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">min hus</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">mitt hus</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">din böcker</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">dina böcker</span>
	- ❌ Choosing <span lang="sv">min</span>, <span lang="sv">din</span>, or
	  <span lang="sv">sin</span> from the owner. Choose the form from the thing
	  that is owned.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip title="The possessed noun decides">
In <span lang="sv">min bil</span>, <span lang="sv">mitt hus</span>, and <span lang="sv">mina böcker</span>, the possessor
is always “I”. The form changes because the possessed thing changes.
</MemoryTip>
