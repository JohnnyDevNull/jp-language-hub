<KeyTakeaway>
- The possessed noun decides the form: <Sv>min bil</Sv>, <Sv>mitt hus</Sv>,
  <Sv>mina böcker</Sv>.
- <Sv>hans</Sv>, <Sv>hennes</Sv>, <Sv>hens</Sv>, and <Sv>deras</Sv> never
  change form.
- The same forms can stand alone: <Sv>Bilen är min</Sv>,
  <Sv>Huset är mitt</Sv>, <Sv>Böckerna är mina</Sv>.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish possessive pronouns often change form according to what is possessed.
For many forms, the choice is the same pattern learners already know from
<Sv>en</Sv> nouns, <Sv>ett</Sv> nouns, and plural
nouns.

## Use this now

| What you need | Choose by | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a form before a noun | the gender or number of the possessed noun | <Sv>min bil, mitt hus, mina böcker</Sv> |
| a form without a following noun | the understood thing's gender or number | <Sv>Bilen är min. Huset är mitt.</Sv> |
| a third-person subject's own thing | the separate subject-reflexive rule | <Sv>Anna tar sin jacka.</Sv> |

## Core pattern

Choose the possessive form from the noun that follows:

| Possessor | <Sv>en</Sv> noun | <Sv>ett</Sv> noun | Plural noun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | <Sv>min bil</Sv> | <Sv>mitt hus</Sv> | <Sv>mina böcker</Sv> |
| you (singular) | <Sv>din bil</Sv> | <Sv>ditt hus</Sv> | <Sv>dina böcker</Sv> |
| he / she / they, own | <Sv>sin bil</Sv> | <Sv>sitt hus</Sv> | <Sv>sina böcker</Sv> |
| we | <Sv>vår bil</Sv> | <Sv>vårt hus</Sv> | <Sv>våra böcker</Sv> |
| you (plural) | <Sv>er bil</Sv> | <Sv>ert hus</Sv> | <Sv>era böcker</Sv> |

## Forms that do not change

Some Swedish possessives do not change for <Sv>en</Sv>,
<Sv>ett</Sv>, or plural:

| Possessor | Form | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| he | <Sv>hans</Sv> | <Sv>hans bil</Sv>, <Sv>hans hus</Sv>, <Sv>hans böcker</Sv> |
| she | <Sv>hennes</Sv> | <Sv>hennes bil</Sv>, <Sv>hennes hus</Sv>, <Sv>hennes böcker</Sv> |
| gender-neutral singular | <Sv>hens</Sv> | <Sv>hens bil</Sv>, <Sv>hens hus</Sv>, <Sv>hens böcker</Sv> |
| they | <Sv>deras</Sv> | <Sv>deras bil</Sv>, <Sv>deras hus</Sv>, <Sv>deras böcker</Sv> |

These forms point to a possessor, but they do not show whether the possessed
thing is an <Sv>en</Sv> noun, an <Sv>ett</Sv> noun,
or plural.

## Forms that change with the possessed noun

The changing possessives follow the same en-word, ett-word, and plural pattern:

| Noun type | My | Your | Our | Your (plural) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>en</Sv> noun | <Sv>min jacka</Sv> | <Sv>din jacka</Sv> | <Sv>vår jacka</Sv> | <Sv>er jacka</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett</Sv> noun | <Sv>mitt rum</Sv> | <Sv>ditt rum</Sv> | <Sv>vårt rum</Sv> | <Sv>ert rum</Sv> |
| Plural noun | <Sv>mina skor</Sv> | <Sv>dina skor</Sv> | <Sv>våra skor</Sv> | <Sv>era skor</Sv> |

## Standing alone

The possessive can replace the whole noun phrase. Its form still reflects the
understood noun:

- <Sv>Vems bil är det? — Den är min.</Sv>
- <Sv>Är huset ert? — Ja, det är vårt.</Sv>
- <Sv>Vilka böcker är hennes? — De här är hennes.</Sv>

Unchanging <Sv>hans</Sv>, <Sv>hennes</Sv>, <Sv>hens</Sv>, and <Sv>deras</Sv>
stay unchanged here too: <Sv>Bilen är hennes</Sv>, <Sv>Huset är hennes</Sv>.

## The subject's own thing

With a third-person subject, first decide whether the thing belongs to that
same subject. If it does, Swedish normally uses <Sv>sin, sitt,</Sv> or
<Sv>sina</Sv>; otherwise it uses <Sv>hans, hennes, hens,</Sv> or <Sv>deras</Sv>.
The full [*sin, sitt, sina*](../sin-sitt-sina/) reference owns that meaning,
the clause boundary, and the detailed contrast.

## Direct comparison

German and English do not mark the same “own versus somebody else's”
distinction in the possessive form:

<LanguageComparison title="First person">
	<p slot="de">Ich nehme meine Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">I take my jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag tar min jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Second person">
	<p slot="de">Du nimmst deine Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">You take your jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Du tar din jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Third person, own thing">
	<p slot="de">Anna nimmt ihre Jacke.</p>
	<p slot="en">Anna takes her jacket.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Anna tar sin jacka.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

The German and English third-person examples can be ambiguous without context.
Swedish uses the separate subject-reflexive system when the jacket belongs to
Anna herself.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <Sv>mitt bil</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>min bil</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>min hus</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>mitt hus</Sv>
	- ❌ <Sv>din böcker</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>dina böcker</Sv>
	- ❌ Choosing <Sv>min</Sv>, <Sv>din</Sv>, or
	  <Sv>sin</Sv> from the owner. Choose the form from the thing
	  that is owned.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip title="The possessed noun decides">
In <Sv>min bil</Sv>, <Sv>mitt hus</Sv>, and <Sv>mina böcker</Sv>, the possessor
is always “I”. The form changes because the possessed thing changes.
</MemoryTip>
