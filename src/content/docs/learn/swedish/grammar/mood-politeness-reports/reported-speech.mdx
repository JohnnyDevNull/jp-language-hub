<KeyTakeaway>
- Report a statement with <span lang="sv">att</span>, a yes/no question with <span lang="sv">om</span>, and
  a wh-question with its question word.
- Reported questions use subordinate-clause order, not direct-question
  inversion.
- Change pronouns and time or place words when the reporting viewpoint changes;
  Swedish tense does not require automatic English-style backshift.
</KeyTakeaway>

Reported speech presents the content of somebody's words without repeating the
original sentence as a quotation.

## Use this now

| Original message | Reporting link | Report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| statement | <span lang="sv">att</span> | <span lang="sv">Hon säger att hon kommer.</span> |
| yes/no question | <span lang="sv">om</span> | <span lang="sv">Han frågar om jag kommer.</span> |
| wh-question | same question word | <span lang="sv">Hon frågar när tåget går.</span> |

## Statements with <span lang="sv">att</span>

Use a reporting verb such as <span lang="sv">säga</span>, <span lang="sv">berätta</span>, or
<span lang="sv">förklara</span> followed by an <span lang="sv">att</span> clause:

<span lang="sv">Lisa säger: “Jag är trött.”</span> →
<span lang="sv">Lisa säger att hon är trött.</span>

## Reported questions

A yes/no question becomes an <span lang="sv">om</span> clause. A wh-question keeps its
question word. Both use subordinate-clause order:

<SentenceSchema
	caption="Word order in reported questions"
	columns={['bisatsinledare', 'subjekt', 'satsadverbial', 'finit verb', 'infinit verb', 'adverbial']}
	rows={[
		{ label: 'Yes/no report', lang: 'sv', cells: ['om', 'du', 'inte', 'kan', 'komma', ''] },
		{ label: 'Wh-report', lang: 'sv', cells: ['varför', 'hon', '', 'gick', '', 'hem'] },
	]}
/>

- <span lang="sv">Han frågar om du inte kan komma.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Jag undrar varför hon gick hem.</span>

Do not keep direct-question inversion: <span lang="sv">varför hon gick</span>, not
<span lang="sv">varför gick hon</span> inside the report.

## Viewpoint changes

Adjust forms only when the reporting situation requires it:

- person: <span lang="sv">jag</span> may become <span lang="sv">hon</span> or <span lang="sv">han</span>
- possession: <span lang="sv">min</span> may become <span lang="sv">hennes</span>, <span lang="sv">hans</span>, or a
  reflexive possessive in the relevant clause
- time: <span lang="sv">i dag</span> may become <span lang="sv">den dagen</span>
- place: <span lang="sv">här</span> may become <span lang="sv">där</span>

If the original viewpoint is still shared, a change may be unnecessary.

## Tense behavior

Swedish often keeps a tense that is still true or still relevant:
<span lang="sv">Hon sa att hon bor i Malmö.</span> can report a current fact. A past form is
also natural when the report belongs to a past frame or the situation no longer
holds: <span lang="sv">Hon sa att hon bodde i Malmö.</span>

Do not apply a mechanical English backshift rule. Choose the tense from the
speaker's current time perspective and the intended meaning.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Han frågar att jag kommer.</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Han frågar om jag kommer.</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Hon frågar var bor du.</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Hon frågar var du bor.</span>
	- Do not change every present to a past merely because the reporting verb is
	  past.
</MistakeBox>
