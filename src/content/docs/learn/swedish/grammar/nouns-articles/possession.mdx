<KeyTakeaway>
- Add <Sv>-s</Sv> to a noun or name to make it the possessor:
  <Sv>Annas bok</Sv>, <Sv>hundens namn</Sv>.
- The possessed noun normally has no article or definite suffix after a
  possessor: <Sv>Annas nya bil</Sv>, not <Sv>den Annas bilen</Sv>.
- Use a possessive pronoun when the owner is expressed as *my, your, his, her,
  our,* or *their*.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish nominal possession puts the owner first and marks it with
<Sv>-s</Sv>. This page covers possession built from nouns and names; pronoun
possessors have their own lookup.

## Use this now

| What names the owner? | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a name | name + <Sv>-s</Sv> | <Sv>Annas bok</Sv> |
| a noun phrase | noun phrase + <Sv>-s</Sv> | <Sv>min väns bil</Sv> |
| *my, your, his, her, our, their* | a possessive pronoun | <Sv>min bok</Sv> |
| the third-person subject's own thing | <Sv>sin / sitt / sina</Sv> | <Sv>Anna tar sin bok.</Sv> |

## Core rule

The possessor comes before the possessed noun:

| Owner | Possessive phrase |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>Anna</Sv> | <Sv>Annas cykel</Sv> |
| <Sv>hunden</Sv> | <Sv>hundens namn</Sv> |
| <Sv>mina vänner</Sv> | <Sv>mina vänners hus</Sv> |

The <Sv>-s</Sv> belongs to the complete possessor expression. Swedish writes
it without an apostrophe: <Sv>Eriks rum</Sv>, not <Sv>Erik's rum</Sv>.

Names already ending in <Sv>-s</Sv>, <Sv>-x</Sv>, or <Sv>-z</Sv> normally do
not take another written <Sv>s</Sv>: <Sv>Lars bok</Sv>, <Sv>Max cykel</Sv>.

## The possessed noun stays indefinite in form

After a possessor, the noun normally appears without an article and without
the simple definite suffix:

- <Sv>Annas bil</Sv> — Anna's car
- <Sv>Annas nya bil</Sv> — Anna's new car
- <Sv>hundens namn</Sv> — the dog's name

An adjective takes its definite-looking <Sv>-a</Sv> form, but Swedish still
does not add <Sv>den</Sv> or make the possessed noun definite:
<Sv>Annas nya bil</Sv>.

## Other natural possession statements

The s-genitive builds a noun phrase. When the point is to state who owns
something, a full clause is often clearer:

- <Sv>Boken tillhör Anna.</Sv> — The book belongs to Anna.
- <Sv>Det är Annas bok.</Sv> — It is Anna's book.
- <Sv>Anna har en bok.</Sv> — Anna has a book.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Anna's new car">
	<p slot="de">Annas neues Auto</p>
	<p slot="en">Anna's new car</p>
	<p slot="sv">Annas nya bil</p>
</LanguageComparison>

English uses an apostrophe in this construction; Swedish and German do not.
Swedish also leaves the possessed noun without a separate article.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
- <Sv>❌ Anna's bok</Sv> → <Sv>✅ Annas bok</Sv>
- <Sv>❌ Annas bilen</Sv> → <Sv>✅ Annas bil</Sv>
- <Sv>❌ Annas ny bil</Sv> → <Sv>✅ Annas nya bil</Sv>
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Put the owner first, add <Sv>-s</Sv>, and leave the owned noun bare:
<Sv><strong>Anna + s + bok → Annas bok</strong></Sv>.
</MemoryTip>
