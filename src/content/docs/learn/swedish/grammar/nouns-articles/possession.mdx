<KeyTakeaway>
- Add <span lang="sv">-s</span> to a noun or name to make it the possessor:
  <span lang="sv">Annas bok</span>, <span lang="sv">hundens namn</span>.
- The possessed noun normally has no article or definite suffix after a
  possessor: <span lang="sv">Annas nya bil</span>, not <span lang="sv">den Annas bilen</span>.
- Use a possessive pronoun when the owner is expressed as *my, your, his, her,
  our,* or *their*.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish nominal possession puts the owner first and marks it with
<span lang="sv">-s</span>. This page covers possession built from nouns and names; pronoun
possessors have their own lookup.

## Use this now

| What names the owner? | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a name | name + <span lang="sv">-s</span> | <span lang="sv">Annas bok</span> |
| a noun phrase | noun phrase + <span lang="sv">-s</span> | <span lang="sv">min väns bil</span> |
| *my, your, his, her, our, their* | a possessive pronoun | <span lang="sv">min bok</span> |
| the third-person subject's own thing | <span lang="sv">sin / sitt / sina</span> | <span lang="sv">Anna tar sin bok.</span> |

## Core rule

The possessor comes before the possessed noun:

| Owner | Possessive phrase |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">Anna</span> | <span lang="sv">Annas cykel</span> |
| <span lang="sv">hunden</span> | <span lang="sv">hundens namn</span> |
| <span lang="sv">mina vänner</span> | <span lang="sv">mina vänners hus</span> |

The <span lang="sv">-s</span> belongs to the complete possessor expression. Swedish writes
it without an apostrophe: <span lang="sv">Eriks rum</span>, not <span lang="sv">Erik's rum</span>.

Names already ending in <span lang="sv">-s</span>, <span lang="sv">-x</span>, or <span lang="sv">-z</span> normally do
not take another written <span lang="sv">s</span>: <span lang="sv">Lars bok</span>, <span lang="sv">Max cykel</span>.

## The possessed noun stays indefinite in form

After a possessor, the noun normally appears without an article and without
the simple definite suffix:

- <span lang="sv">Annas bil</span> — Anna's car
- <span lang="sv">Annas nya bil</span> — Anna's new car
- <span lang="sv">hundens namn</span> — the dog's name

An adjective takes its definite-looking <span lang="sv">-a</span> form, but Swedish still
does not add <span lang="sv">den</span> or make the possessed noun definite:
<span lang="sv">Annas nya bil</span>.

## Other natural possession statements

The s-genitive builds a noun phrase. When the point is to state who owns
something, a full clause is often clearer:

- <span lang="sv">Boken tillhör Anna.</span> — The book belongs to Anna.
- <span lang="sv">Det är Annas bok.</span> — It is Anna's book.
- <span lang="sv">Anna har en bok.</span> — Anna has a book.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Anna's new car">
	<p slot="de">Annas neues Auto</p>
	<p slot="en">Anna's new car</p>
	<p slot="sv">Annas nya bil</p>
</LanguageComparison>

English uses an apostrophe in this construction; Swedish and German do not.
Swedish also leaves the possessed noun without a separate article.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
- <span lang="sv">❌ Anna's bok</span> → <span lang="sv">✅ Annas bok</span>
- <span lang="sv">❌ Annas bilen</span> → <span lang="sv">✅ Annas bil</span>
- <span lang="sv">❌ Annas ny bil</span> → <span lang="sv">✅ Annas nya bil</span>
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Put the owner first, add <span lang="sv">-s</span>, and leave the owned noun bare:
<span lang="sv"><strong>Anna + s + bok → Annas bok</strong></span>.
</MemoryTip>
