<KeyTakeaway>
- The indefinite plural uses one of five patterns: <Sv>-or</Sv>,
  <Sv>-ar</Sv>, <Sv>-er</Sv>, <Sv>-n</Sv>, or no ending at all.
- Gender and word ending only narrow the guess down; the plural has to be
  learned with the noun.
- A missing ending does not mean singular: <Sv>tre hus</Sv> is plural.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish nouns form the indefinite plural in several ways. Gender and word
ending provide useful clues, but they do not predict every noun. For reliable
use, learn a noun with its gender and plural.

## Main plural patterns

Traditional descriptions group Swedish nouns by their plural endings:

| Pattern | Typical example | Indefinite plural | Definite plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>-or</Sv> | <Sv>en flicka</Sv> | <Sv>flickor</Sv> | <Sv>flickorna</Sv> |
| <Sv>-ar</Sv> | <Sv>en bil</Sv> | <Sv>bilar</Sv> | <Sv>bilarna</Sv> |
| <Sv>-er</Sv> | <Sv>en telefon</Sv> | <Sv>telefoner</Sv> | <Sv>telefonerna</Sv> |
| <Sv>-n</Sv> | <Sv>ett äpple</Sv> | <Sv>äpplen</Sv> | <Sv>äpplena</Sv> |
| no ending | <Sv>ett hus</Sv> | <Sv>hus</Sv> | <Sv>husen</Sv> |

These are learning patterns, not rules that remove the need to learn individual
noun forms.

## Useful clues

- Many en-words ending in unstressed <Sv>-a</Sv> use
  <Sv>-or</Sv>: <Sv>en vecka → veckor</Sv>.
- Many common en-words use <Sv>-ar</Sv>:
  <Sv>en stol → stolar</Sv>.
- Many international words use <Sv>-er</Sv>:
  <Sv>en station → stationer</Sv>.
- Many ett-words ending in a vowel use <Sv>-n</Sv>:
  <Sv>ett äpple → äpplen</Sv>.
- Many ett-words ending in a consonant have no indefinite plural ending:
  <Sv>ett barn → barn</Sv>.

## Stem changes and irregular forms

Some frequent nouns change internally or use an irregular plural:

- <Sv>en bok → böcker → böckerna</Sv>
- <Sv>en man → män → männen</Sv>
- <Sv>en hand → händer → händerna</Sv>
- <Sv>ett öga → ögon → ögonen</Sv>

These forms should be learned as complete noun paradigms rather than forced into
a regular ending pattern.

## Latin and Greek loan words

A group of ett-words ending in <Sv>-um</Sv> replaces that ending
in the plural. Several of them are everyday words, so the pattern is worth
recognizing:

| Singular | Plural | Definite plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>ett museum</Sv> | <Sv>museer</Sv> | <Sv>museerna</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett gymnasium</Sv> | <Sv>gymnasier</Sv> | <Sv>gymnasierna</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett laboratorium</Sv> | <Sv>laboratorier</Sv> | <Sv>laboratorierna</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett akvarium</Sv> | <Sv>akvarier</Sv> | <Sv>akvarierna</Sv> |

Two frequent words behave differently:

- <Sv>ett faktum → fakta</Sv> — the plural is
  <Sv>fakta</Sv>, which is also widely treated as a mass noun.
- <Sv>ett datum → datum</Sv> — unchanged in the plural.

Do not extend this to every <Sv>-um</Sv> word: everyday
<Sv>ett centrum</Sv> is usually
<Sv>centrum</Sv> in the plural, with the Latin
<Sv>centra</Sv> restricted to formal writing.

## Number after numerals

Use the indefinite plural form after numbers greater than one:

- <Sv>en bil</Sv>
- <Sv>två bilar</Sv>
- <Sv>tre hus</Sv>

The absence of a visible ending in <Sv>tre hus</Sv> does not make
the noun singular; number and context show that it is plural.

Like German, Swedish has several plural patterns and stem changes. English
<span lang="en">-s</span> is therefore not a safe model for Swedish.

<MistakeBox>
Do not infer that all en-words take one ending and all ett-words another:

<Sv>en bil → bilar</Sv>, but
<Sv>en telefon → telefoner</Sv>;  
<Sv>ett äpple → äpplen</Sv>, but
<Sv>ett hus → hus</Sv>.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Learn the four forms, not a guessed group number:
<Sv><strong>ett äpple — äpplet — äpplen — äpplena</strong></Sv>.
The pattern will become familiar through repeated examples.
</MemoryTip>
