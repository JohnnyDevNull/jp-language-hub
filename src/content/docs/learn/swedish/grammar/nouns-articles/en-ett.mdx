<KeyTakeaway>
- Every noun carries a gender, so learn it as <Sv>en bil</Sv> or
  <Sv>ett hus</Sv>, never as the bare noun.
- <Sv>en</Sv> is the better guess: en-words are the clear majority, and several
  derivational endings make the guess safer.
- Productive compounds normally take the gender of their **last** element.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Most Swedish nouns belong to one of two grammatical genders. An **en-word** uses
<Sv>en</Sv>; an **ett-word** uses <Sv>ett</Sv>.
The noun's gender affects articles, definite forms, and adjective endings.

## Use this now

When you need to use a new noun, choose the safest path in this order:

| Situation | What to do | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| You are learning a new noun | Store it with its article, not alone. | <Sv>en bil</Sv>, <Sv>ett hus</Sv> |
| You have no clue | Guess <Sv>en</Sv>, then check later. | <Sv>en dag</Sv>, <Sv>en fråga</Sv> |
| The noun has a strong ending signal | Let the ending guide you, but still learn the full noun phrase. | <Sv>en tidning</Sv>, <Sv>ett bibliotek</Sv> |
| The noun looks like an ordinary productive compound | Start with the final element's gender, then check unknown words. | <Sv>ett sommarhus</Sv> from <Sv>ett hus</Sv> |
| You add an adjective | Match the adjective to the noun's gender and number. | <Sv>en stor bil</Sv>, <Sv>ett stort hus</Sv> |

For everyday speech, the most useful habit is simple: say the noun aloud with
its article whenever you learn it. That one habit gives you the article,
definite form, and adjective pattern a sentence will need later.

## Core rule

| Gender | Indefinite singular | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| en-word | <Sv>en bil</Sv> | a car |
| ett-word | <Sv>ett hus</Sv> | a house |

There is no completely reliable rule that predicts the gender of every Swedish
noun. Learn the article together with the noun: not merely
<Sv>bil</Sv>, but <Sv>en bil</Sv>. Some word
endings are regular enough to narrow the guess down, and productive compounds
normally follow the final element.

<RuleBox>
	Learn a Swedish noun together with its article. The article is part of the
	word's grammatical information: it records the noun's gender and helps you
	choose compatible forms elsewhere in the noun phrase.
</RuleBox>

## Endings that point to a gender

en-words are the clear majority in Swedish, so <Sv>en</Sv> is the
better guess when you have nothing else to go on. Many derivational endings
narrow it down further.

These endings are normally en-words:

| Ending | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>-a</Sv> | <Sv>en flicka</Sv>, <Sv>en gata</Sv>, <Sv>en vecka</Sv> |
| <Sv>-are</Sv> | <Sv>en lärare</Sv>, <Sv>en bagare</Sv> |
| <Sv>-else</Sv> | <Sv>en händelse</Sv>, <Sv>en rörelse</Sv> |
| <Sv>-het</Sv> | <Sv>en möjlighet</Sv>, <Sv>en nyhet</Sv> |
| <Sv>-ning</Sv>, <Sv>-ing</Sv> | <Sv>en tidning</Sv>, <Sv>en övning</Sv> |
| <Sv>-dom</Sv> | <Sv>en sjukdom</Sv>, <Sv>en visdom</Sv> |
| <Sv>-lek</Sv> | <Sv>en kärlek</Sv>, <Sv>en storlek</Sv> |
| <Sv>-nad</Sv> | <Sv>en byggnad</Sv>, <Sv>en skillnad</Sv> |
| <Sv>-tion</Sv>, <Sv>-sion</Sv> | <Sv>en station</Sv>, <Sv>en diskussion</Sv> |
| <Sv>-ist</Sv>, <Sv>-ent</Sv>, <Sv>-ör</Sv> | <Sv>en turist</Sv>, <Sv>en student</Sv>, <Sv>en frisör</Sv> |

These endings are normally ett-words:

| Ending | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>-ande</Sv>, <Sv>-ende</Sv> | <Sv>ett meddelande</Sv>, <Sv>ett leende</Sv> |
| <Sv>-eri</Sv> | <Sv>ett bageri</Sv>, <Sv>ett tryckeri</Sv> |
| <Sv>-um</Sv> | <Sv>ett museum</Sv>, <Sv>ett centrum</Sv>, <Sv>ett faktum</Sv> |
| <Sv>-ment</Sv> | <Sv>ett dokument</Sv>, <Sv>ett argument</Sv> |
| <Sv>-tek</Sv> | <Sv>ett bibliotek</Sv>, <Sv>ett apotek</Sv> |
| <Sv>-gram</Sv> | <Sv>ett program</Sv>, <Sv>ett diagram</Sv> |

Three cases need care:

- <Sv>-a</Sv> has a small group of ett-words:
  <Sv>ett öga</Sv>, <Sv>ett öra</Sv>,
  <Sv>ett hjärta</Sv>.
- <Sv>-ande</Sv> and <Sv>-ende</Sv> are ett-words
  when they name an action, but en-words when they name a person:
  <Sv>ett meddelande</Sv> against
  <Sv>en studerande</Sv>.
- <Sv>-skap</Sv> is genuinely split. Concrete and collective
  nouns tend to be ett-words (<Sv>ett landskap</Sv>,
  <Sv>ett sällskap</Sv>), while relations and qualities tend to
  be en-words (<Sv>en vänskap</Sv>,
  <Sv>en kunskap</Sv>). Check this ending rather than guessing.

An ending such as <Sv>-e</Sv> gives no useful signal at all:
compare <Sv>en pojke</Sv> with
<Sv>ett äpple</Sv>.

Nouns for people and animals are usually en-words —
<Sv>en man</Sv>, <Sv>en kvinna</Sv>,
<Sv>en hund</Sv> — but not always:
<Sv>ett barn</Sv>, <Sv>ett djur</Sv>,
<Sv>ett lejon</Sv>.

## Productive compounds normally follow their last element

This is the most useful gender rule because Swedish builds compounds
constantly. In productive compounds, the final element is the head and
normally supplies the compound's gender; the earlier elements do not matter:

| Parts | Compound |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>en cykel</Sv> + <Sv>ett hjul</Sv> | <Sv>ett cykelhjul</Sv> |
| <Sv>ett tåg</Sv> + <Sv>en station</Sv> | <Sv>en tågstation</Sv> |
| <Sv>en sommar</Sv> + <Sv>ett hus</Sv> | <Sv>ett sommarhus</Sv> |
| <Sv>en post</Sv> + <Sv>ett kontor</Sv> | <Sv>ett postkontor</Sv> |

For ordinary productive compounds, the definite and plural forms normally follow
the last element too:
<Sv>ett cykelhjul → cykelhjulet</Sv>,
<Sv>en tågstation → tågstationen</Sv>.

<RuleBox title="Reliable shortcut">
	For an ordinary productive compound, start with the last element. Its gender,
	definite suffix, and plural pattern normally carry over to the whole word.
</RuleBox>

## Indefinite and definite

Swedish normally uses a separate article for the indefinite singular but adds a
suffix to the noun for the simple definite form:

| Gender | Indefinite | Definite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| en-word | <Sv>en bil</Sv> | <Sv>bilen</Sv> |
| ett-word | <Sv>ett hus</Sv> | <Sv>huset</Sv> |

## Adjective agreement

The noun phrase's gender and number determine the basic adjective form. The
article makes that grammatical information visible:

- <Sv>en <strong>stor</strong> bil</Sv> — a big car
- <Sv>ett <strong>stort</strong> hus</Sv> — a big house
- <Sv><strong>stora</strong> bilar</Sv> — big cars
- <Sv><strong>stora</strong> hus</Sv> — big houses

The basic pattern is **base form** with an en-word, often **-t** with an ett-word,
and often **-a** in the plural. Adjectives also have irregular and spelling-
dependent forms; the canonical explanation is [adjective agreement](../../adjectives-adverbs/agreement/).

## Definite adjective constructions

When an adjective modifies a definite noun, standard Swedish normally marks
definiteness with both a separate article and the noun's definite suffix:

<p lang="sv">den stora bilen</p>

This is called **double definiteness**. See
[adjective agreement](../../adjectives-adverbs/agreement/#definite-noun-phrases-and-double-definiteness)
for the complete pattern, gender and number forms, and the possessive exception.

## Plural

The plural ending cannot be derived from en or ett alone:

- <Sv>en bil → bilar</Sv>
- <Sv>ett hus → hus</Sv>
- <Sv>ett äpple → äpplen</Sv>

Learn the important plural forms with the noun. See the
[plural overview](../plural-overview/).

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
Do not assume that an object's natural properties determine en or ett, and do
not use <Sv>en</Sv> as a universal article. Do not take the
gender of a compound from its first element either:
<Sv>❌ en cykelhjul</Sv>, <Sv>✅ ett cykelhjul</Sv>.

<Sv>❌ en hus</Sv>  
<Sv>✅ ett hus</Sv>

Also keep the adjective consistent:

<Sv>❌ ett stor hus</Sv>  
<Sv>✅ ett stort hus</Sv>
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
	Treat the article as part of the word: learn
	<Sv><strong>en bil</strong></Sv> and
	<Sv><strong>ett hus</strong></Sv>. Add the four noun forms when
	you can: <Sv>en bil, bilen, bilar, bilarna</Sv>.
</MemoryTip>
