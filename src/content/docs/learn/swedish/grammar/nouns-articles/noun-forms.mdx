<KeyTakeaway>
- Every noun has four central forms: indefinite and definite, singular and
  plural — <span lang="sv">en bil — bilen — bilar — bilarna</span>.
- Simple definiteness is a **suffix** on the noun, not a separate article.
- The definite plural is built on the indefinite plural, so that form has to be
  known first.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

A Swedish noun is commonly learned through four forms: indefinite singular,
definite singular, indefinite plural, and definite plural. Seeing these forms
together makes both meaning and morphology easier to recognize.

## The four-form pattern

| Number | Indefinite | Definite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular | <span lang="sv">en bil</span> | <span lang="sv">bilen</span> |
| Plural | <span lang="sv">bilar</span> | <span lang="sv">bilarna</span> |

For an ett-word with no separate indefinite plural ending:

| Number | Indefinite | Definite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Singular | <span lang="sv">ett hus</span> | <span lang="sv">huset</span> |
| Plural | <span lang="sv">hus</span> | <span lang="sv">husen</span> |

## Singular definiteness

The indefinite singular uses <span lang="sv">en</span> or
<span lang="sv">ett</span>. The simple definite singular normally adds a suffix:

- en-word: often <span lang="sv">-en</span> or <span lang="sv">-n</span>  
  <span lang="sv">en bil → bilen</span>,
  <span lang="sv">en flicka → flickan</span>
- ett-word: often <span lang="sv">-et</span> or <span lang="sv">-t</span>  
  <span lang="sv">ett hus → huset</span>,
  <span lang="sv">ett äpple → äpplet</span>

The shorter suffix often appears when the noun already ends in a vowel.

## Plural definiteness

The indefinite plural varies by noun. The definite plural then adds a definite
ending to that plural form:

- <span lang="sv">bilar → bilarna</span>
- <span lang="sv">flickor → flickorna</span>
- <span lang="sv">äpplen → äpplena</span>
- <span lang="sv">hus → husen</span>

The [plural overview](../plural-overview/) groups the most useful
patterns and exceptions.

## Definite nouns with adjectives

A simple noun needs only its suffix:

<p lang="sv">Bilen är ny.</p>

With an attributive adjective, standard Swedish normally adds a separate
definite article while keeping the noun's definite suffix. This is called
**double definiteness**:

<p lang="sv">Den nya bilen är dyr.</p>

See
[adjective agreement](../../adjectives-adverbs/agreement/#definite-noun-phrases-and-double-definiteness)
for the complete pattern, gender and number forms, and the possessive exception.

Demonstratives such as <span lang="sv">den här</span> and
<span lang="sv">det där</span> also normally use a definite noun:
<span lang="sv">den här bilen</span>, <span lang="sv">det där huset</span>.
See [demonstratives with här and där](../../pronouns-roles/demonstratives-har-dar/).

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Indefinite singular">
	<p slot="de">ein Auto</p>
	<p slot="en">a car</p>
	<p slot="sv">en bil</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Definite singular">
	<p slot="de">das Auto</p>
	<p slot="en">the car</p>
	<p slot="sv">bilen</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Definite plural">
	<p slot="de">die Autos</p>
	<p slot="en">the cars</p>
	<p slot="sv">bilarna</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German and English mainly mark definiteness before the noun. Swedish usually
marks simple definiteness on the noun itself.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
Do not normally combine the indefinite article with a definite noun:

<span lang="sv">❌ en bilen</span>  
<span lang="sv">✅ en bil</span> / <span lang="sv">✅ bilen</span>

Do not omit the noun's definite suffix in the standard double-definite
construction:

<span lang="sv">❌ den stora bil</span>  
<span lang="sv">✅ den stora bilen</span>
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Store nouns as a four-part rhythm:
<span lang="sv"><strong>en bil — bilen — bilar — bilarna</strong></span>.
This captures gender, plural, and definiteness at once.
</MemoryTip>
