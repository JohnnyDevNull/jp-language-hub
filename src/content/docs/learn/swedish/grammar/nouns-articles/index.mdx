This section separates four closely connected decisions: noun gender, noun
form, plural pattern, and nominal possession.

## Choose by

| If you need to… | Start with… |
| --- | --- |
| choose <Sv>en</Sv> or <Sv>ett</Sv> | [<Sv>en</Sv> / <Sv>ett</Sv>](./en-ett/) |
| make a noun indefinite or definite, singular or plural | [Noun forms](./noun-forms/) |
| find the likely plural ending or recognize an irregular plural | [Plural overview](./plural-overview/) |
| say “Anna's book” or “the dog's name” | [Possession](./possession/) |
| choose <em>my, your, his, her, our,</em> or <em>their</em> | [Possessive pronouns](../pronouns-roles/possessive-pronouns/) |

## Learn in this order

1. [<Sv>en</Sv> / <Sv>ett</Sv>](./en-ett/) establishes noun gender and the safest learning
   habit.
2. [Noun forms](./noun-forms/) builds the four central forms.
3. [Plural overview](./plural-overview/) adds the main plural patterns and
   frequent irregular forms.
4. [Possession](./possession/) shows how a possessor noun phrase modifies
   another noun.

## Watch for

- **Noun forms versus plural overview:** the first page maps the whole
  singular/plural and indefinite/definite system; the second focuses on how
  the plural itself is formed.
- **Possession versus possessive pronouns:** [Possession](./possession/) owns
  noun possessors such as *Annas*; [Possessive pronouns](../pronouns-roles/possessive-pronouns/)
  owns forms such as *min*, *mitt*, and *mina*.
