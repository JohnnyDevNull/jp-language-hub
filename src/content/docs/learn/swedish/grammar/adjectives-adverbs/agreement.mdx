<KeyTakeaway>
- The indefinite pattern is base form, <Sv>-t</Sv>, <Sv>-a</Sv>:
  <Sv>en stor bil</Sv>, <Sv>ett stort hus</Sv>, <Sv>stora bilar</Sv>.
- The plural form is the same for en-words and ett-words.
- A definite phrase marks definiteness twice: article plus definite adjective
  plus definite noun — <Sv>den stora bilen</Sv>.
</KeyTakeaway>

Swedish adjectives agree with the gender and number of the noun they describe.
Attributive adjectives inside noun phrases also reflect whether the phrase is
indefinite or definite. The central beginner pattern is **base form**, **-t**,
and **-a**.

## Core pattern

| Noun phrase | Adjective form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| indefinite en singular | base form | <Sv>en <strong>{stor.morphology.en}</strong> bil</Sv> |
| indefinite ett singular | often <Sv>-t</Sv> | <Sv>ett <strong>{stor.morphology.ett}</strong> hus</Sv> |
| indefinite plural | often <Sv>-a</Sv> | <Sv><strong>{stor.morphology.plural}</strong> bilar</Sv> |
| definite | often <Sv>-a</Sv> | <Sv>den <strong>{stor.morphology.definite}</strong> bilen</Sv> |

The same plural adjective form is used with en-words and ett-words:

- <Sv>stora bilar</Sv>
- <Sv>stora hus</Sv>

## Indefinite forms

Start from the dictionary or en-form:

<p lang="sv">stor</p>

Add <Sv>-t</Sv> for the regular ett-form and
<Sv>-a</Sv> for the regular plural:

<p lang="sv">stor → stort → stora</p>

Another regular example:

<p lang="sv">{gul.morphology.en} → {gul.morphology.ett} → {gul.morphology.plural}</p>

Spelling can change when endings are added, and some adjectives are irregular.
For example:

- <Sv>{glad.morphology.en} → {glad.morphology.ett} → {glad.morphology.plural}</Sv>
- <Sv>liten → litet → små</Sv>
- <Sv>bra → bra → bra</Sv>

Learn frequent exceptions through complete phrases.

## Spelling patterns

The <Sv>-t</Sv> and <Sv>-a</Sv> endings are
regular, but the stem often changes when they are attached. These patterns
cover most adjectives:

| Stem pattern | en-form | ett-form | plural / definite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| stressed vowel | <Sv>ny</Sv>, <Sv>blå</Sv> | <Sv>nytt</Sv>, <Sv>blått</Sv> | <Sv>nya</Sv>, <Sv>blåa</Sv> |
| vowel + <Sv>-d</Sv> | <Sv>röd</Sv>, <Sv>god</Sv> | <Sv>rött</Sv>, <Sv>gott</Sv> | <Sv>röda</Sv>, <Sv>goda</Sv> |
| consonant + <Sv>-d</Sv> | <Sv>hård</Sv> | <Sv>hårt</Sv> | <Sv>hårda</Sv> |
| vowel + <Sv>-t</Sv> | <Sv>vit</Sv>, <Sv>söt</Sv> | <Sv>vitt</Sv>, <Sv>sött</Sv> | <Sv>vita</Sv>, <Sv>söta</Sv> |
| consonant + <Sv>-t</Sv> | <Sv>kort</Sv>, <Sv>svart</Sv> | <Sv>kort</Sv>, <Sv>svart</Sv> | <Sv>korta</Sv>, <Sv>svarta</Sv> |
| <Sv>-nn</Sv> | <Sv>sann</Sv>, <Sv>tunn</Sv> | <Sv>sant</Sv>, <Sv>tunt</Sv> | <Sv>sanna</Sv>, <Sv>tunna</Sv> |
| <Sv>-en</Sv> | <Sv>öppen</Sv>, <Sv>mogen</Sv> | <Sv>öppet</Sv>, <Sv>moget</Sv> | <Sv>öppna</Sv>, <Sv>mogna</Sv> |
| <Sv>-er</Sv>, <Sv>-el</Sv> | <Sv>vacker</Sv>, <Sv>enkel</Sv> | <Sv>vackert</Sv>, <Sv>enkelt</Sv> | <Sv>vackra</Sv>, <Sv>enkla</Sv> |

Two frequent adjectives are irregular beyond these patterns:

- <Sv>gammal → gammalt → gamla</Sv>
- <Sv>liten → litet → små</Sv>, with
  <Sv>lilla</Sv> in the definite singular:
  <Sv>den lilla bilen</Sv>

The pattern worth internalizing first is the
<Sv>-en</Sv> group, because the unstressed
<Sv>e</Sv> disappears in the plural:
<Sv>ett öppet fönster</Sv> but
<Sv>öppna fönster</Sv>.

## Adjectives that never change

A small group takes no endings at all. Using them is easy once you know which
ones they are:

<Sv>bra</Sv>, <Sv>kul</Sv>,
<Sv>äkta</Sv>, <Sv>sakta</Sv>,
<Sv>främmande</Sv>, and colour words such as
<Sv>rosa</Sv>, <Sv>lila</Sv>,
<Sv>beige</Sv>.

- <Sv>ett bra hus</Sv>, <Sv>bra bilar</Sv>
- <Sv>ett rosa hus</Sv>, <Sv>rosa bilar</Sv>

## Participles as adjectives

Past participles inflect like ordinary adjectives:

| Noun phrase | Example |
| --- | --- |
| indefinite en | <Sv>en stängd dörr</Sv> |
| indefinite ett | <Sv>ett stängt fönster</Sv> |
| plural | <Sv>stängda dörrar</Sv> |

The same applies to participles from other verb groups:
<Sv>en målad vägg</Sv>,
<Sv>ett målat hus</Sv>,
<Sv>målade väggar</Sv>; and
<Sv>en skriven text</Sv>,
<Sv>ett skrivet brev</Sv>,
<Sv>skrivna brev</Sv>.

Present participles in <Sv>-ande</Sv> and
<Sv>-ende</Sv> never inflect:

<p lang="sv">ett leende barn · en leende flicka · leende barn</p>

Past participles are also what the bli-passive is built from. See
[passive](../../verbs/passive/).

## The optional masculine -e

Referring to a male person, the definite form may take
<Sv>-e</Sv> instead of <Sv>-a</Sv>:

- <Sv>den gamle mannen</Sv> beside
  <Sv>den gamla mannen</Sv>
- <Sv>min gode vän</Sv>

This is optional and belongs to a slightly formal or traditional register; it
also survives in fixed names such as
<Sv>Karl den store</Sv>. The
<Sv>-a</Sv> form is always acceptable, so treat
<Sv>-e</Sv> as something to recognize rather than a rule to
apply.

## Definite noun phrases and double definiteness

A noun without an attributive adjective normally marks definiteness only with
its suffix:

<p lang="sv">bilen · huset · bilarna</p>

When an attributive adjective is added, standard Swedish normally marks
definiteness twice: with a separate definite article before the adjective and
with the definite suffix on the noun. This construction is called **double
definiteness**.

| Number and gender | Definite article | Adjective | Definite noun | Complete phrase |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| en singular | <Sv>den</Sv> | <Sv>{stor.morphology.definite}</Sv> | <Sv>bilen</Sv> | <Sv>den {stor.morphology.definite} bilen</Sv> |
| ett singular | <Sv>det</Sv> | <Sv>{stor.morphology.definite}</Sv> | <Sv>huset</Sv> | <Sv>det {stor.morphology.definite} huset</Sv> |
| plural | <Sv>de</Sv> | <Sv>{stor.morphology.definite}</Sv> | <Sv>bilarna</Sv> | <Sv>de {stor.morphology.definite} bilarna</Sv> |

The three-part pattern is therefore:

<p lang="sv"><strong>den / det / de + definite adjective + definite noun</strong></p>

The article marks gender or number, the adjective commonly uses its
<Sv>-a</Sv> form, and the noun keeps its definite suffix. English
usually marks the same phrase only once in <span lang="en">the big car</span>;
Swedish uses both <Sv>den</Sv> and <Sv>-en</Sv> in
<Sv>den stora bilen</Sv>.

Possessives are an important exception to the standard pattern. The adjective
uses the definite form, but there is no separate definite article and the noun
does not take its definite suffix:

<p lang="sv">min stora bil</p>

Demonstratives such as <Sv>den här</Sv> and
<Sv>det där</Sv> follow the definite-noun rhythm:
<Sv>den här stora bilen</Sv>,
<Sv>det där stora huset</Sv>.

See [noun forms](../../nouns-articles/noun-forms/) for the
noun suffixes themselves.

## After verbs such as *vara* and *bli*

Predicative adjectives agree with the subject but do not use double
definiteness:

- <Sv>Bilen är stor.</Sv>
- <Sv>Huset är stort.</Sv>
- <Sv>Bilarna är stora.</Sv>

English adjectives do not agree with the noun. German adjectives agree too,
but German endings depend on case and article type as well as gender and number.
Do not transfer the German ending system directly to Swedish.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
Keep article, adjective, and noun compatible:

<Sv>❌ ett stor hus</Sv>
<Sv>✅ ett stort hus</Sv>

In a standard definite phrase, do not omit either part of double definiteness:

<Sv>❌ det stora hus</Sv>
<Sv>✅ det stora huset</Sv>
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Remember the three-part rhythm:
<Sv><strong>en stor — ett stort — flera stora</strong></Sv>.
For a definite phrase, use the familiar <Sv>-a</Sv> form:
<Sv>den stora bilen</Sv>.
</MemoryTip>
