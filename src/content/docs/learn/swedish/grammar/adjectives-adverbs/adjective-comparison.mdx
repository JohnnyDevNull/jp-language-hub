Swedish adjectives can express three degrees: positive, comparative, and
superlative. Many short adjectives use the endings
<Sv>-are</Sv> and <Sv>-ast</Sv>; other adjectives
change irregularly or use <Sv>mer</Sv> and
<Sv>mest</Sv>.

## Regular comparison

The positive is the base form, the comparative normally adds
<Sv>-are</Sv>, and the superlative normally adds <Sv>-ast</Sv>:

In a comparison, <Sv>än</Sv> means “than”:

<p lang="sv">Tåget är snabbare än bussen.</p>

The basic predicative superlative does not need an ending for gender or number:

<p lang="sv">Det här tåget är snabbast.</p>

## Frequent irregular forms

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>stor</Sv> | <Sv>större</Sv> | <Sv>störst</Sv> | big |
| <Sv>liten</Sv> | <Sv>mindre</Sv> | <Sv>minst</Sv> | small |
| <Sv>gammal</Sv> | <Sv>äldre</Sv> | <Sv>äldst</Sv> | old |
| <Sv>bra</Sv> | <Sv>bättre</Sv> | <Sv>bäst</Sv> | good |
| <Sv>dålig</Sv> | <Sv>sämre</Sv> | <Sv>sämst</Sv> | bad |

These common forms are best learned as sets.

## Comparison with *mer* and *mest*

Some adjectives, especially longer adjectives and many participial adjectives,
are commonly compared with <Sv>mer</Sv> and
<Sv>mest</Sv>:

<p lang="sv">komplicerad → mer komplicerad → mest komplicerad</p>

<p lang="sv">överraskande → mer överraskande → mest överraskande</p>

Usage can vary for some adjectives. When both a suffixed form and a
<Sv>mer</Sv> construction occur, follow established usage rather
than mechanically applying one rule to every word.

## Attributive superlatives

Before a definite noun, use the adjective's definite superlative form. Its
ending depends on the comparison pattern:

- <Sv>den snabbaste bilen</Sv>
- <Sv>det största huset</Sv>
- <Sv>de bästa resultaten</Sv>

This construction also uses the definite article and definite noun expected by
[adjective agreement](../agreement/). A masculine
singular form in <Sv>-e</Sv>, such as
<Sv>den bäste spelaren</Sv>, also occurs, especially when a male
person is explicitly meant. The non-masculine-specific form, for example
<Sv>den bästa spelaren</Sv>, is also widely used.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Analytic degrees">
	<p slot="de">kompliziert → komplizierter → am kompliziertesten</p>
	<p slot="en">complicated → more complicated → most complicated</p>
	<p slot="sv">komplicerad → mer komplicerad → mest komplicerad</p>
</LanguageComparison>

All three languages have regular and irregular patterns. English
<span lang="en">more / most</span> can be a useful bridge to Swedish
<Sv>mer / mest</Sv>, but the languages do not choose the analytic
pattern for exactly the same set of adjectives.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
Do not combine both comparison strategies:

<Sv>❌ mer snabbare</Sv>  
<Sv>✅ snabbare</Sv>

Learn irregular sets instead of adding regular endings:

<Sv>❌ braare / braast</Sv>  
<Sv>✅ bättre / bäst</Sv>
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Store each frequent adjective as a three-step ladder:
<Sv><strong>stor — större — störst</strong></Sv>. If the adjective
is long or participial, check whether <Sv>mer — mest</Sv> is the
natural pattern.
</MemoryTip>
