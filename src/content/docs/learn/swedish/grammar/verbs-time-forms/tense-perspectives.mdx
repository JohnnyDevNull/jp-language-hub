<KeyTakeaway>
- Choose a tense by asking where the action sits relative to **now** or to a
  **past reference point**, not by translating the German tense name.
- The now perspective is perfect — present — future time, with several
  possible future constructions.
- The past perspective is pluperfect — preterite — <Sv>skulle</Sv> +
  infinitive.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish tense choices become easier when they are viewed from two perspectives:
what is anchored around **now** and what is anchored around a point in the
**past**.

## The now perspective

| Position | Swedish tense | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| before now | perfect | <Sv>Jag har skrivit mejlet.</Sv> | I have written the email. |
| now | present | <Sv>Jag läser svaret.</Sv> | I am reading the answer. |
| after now | future time | <Sv>Jag ringer / ska ringa Anna.</Sv> | I am calling / going to call Anna. |

The present is the basic finite form for present-time statements. The perfect
uses <Sv>har</Sv> + supine to connect an earlier action with the
present. Future time can use the contextual present, <Sv>ska</Sv>,
<Sv>tänker</Sv>, or <Sv>kommer att</Sv>; the
[Future forms](../future-forms/) page owns that choice.

## The past perspective

| Position | Swedish tense | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| before then | pluperfect | <Sv>Jag hade packat väskan.</Sv> | I had packed the bag. |
| then | preterite | <Sv>Resan började.</Sv> | The trip began. |
| after then | future in the past | <Sv>Jag skulle köpa biljetten.</Sv> | I was going to buy the ticket. |

The past perspective is useful when telling a story. First establish a past
reference point, then choose whether another action happened before, at, or
after that point.

## What is finite?

For word order, identify the finite verb:

- <Sv>Jag skriver mejlet.</Sv> — <Sv>skriver</Sv> is finite.
- <Sv>Jag har skrivit mejlet.</Sv> — <Sv>har</Sv> is finite.
- <Sv>Jag hade skrivit mejlet.</Sv> — <Sv>hade</Sv> is finite.
- <Sv>Jag ska skriva mejlet.</Sv> — <Sv>ska</Sv> is finite.

This matters for [V2](../../sentence-structure/v2/) and for the
placement of <Sv>inte</Sv>.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- Do not use the infinitive after <Sv>har</Sv> or
	  <Sv>hade</Sv>; use the supine:
	  <Sv>har skrivit</Sv>, <Sv>hade skrivit</Sv>.
	- Do not treat English progressive forms as separate Swedish tense forms.
	  Swedish usually uses the ordinary present.
	- Do not choose a tense only by translating German names. First ask where
	  the action sits relative to now or a past reference point.
</MistakeBox>
