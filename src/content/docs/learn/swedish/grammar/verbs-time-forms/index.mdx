<KeyTakeaway>
- Start with Verb Forms when you need the shape of a verb, and Tense
  Perspectives when you need the time map.
- Present, Preterite, Perfect, and Future Forms each own their formation and
  central uses.
- Swedish future time is a choice among several constructions, not one future
  tense that replaces all the others.
</KeyTakeaway>

This shelf separates two questions: **which form does the verb have?** and
**which time perspective fits the message?**

## Choose by

| If you need to… | Start with… |
| --- | --- |
| recognize the infinitive, imperative, present, preterite, or supine | [Verb forms](./verb-forms/) |
| see events before, at, or after a reference point | [Tense perspectives](./tense-perspectives/) |
| describe what happens now, regularly, or in a scheduled future | [Present tense](./present/) |
| tell about a completed event in a finished past frame | [Preterite](./preterite/) |
| connect a past event or experience with now | [Perfect](./perfect/) |
| choose among contextual present, <Sv>ska</Sv>, <Sv>tänker</Sv>, and <Sv>kommer att</Sv> | [Future forms](./future-forms/) |

## Learn in this order

1. [Verb forms](./verb-forms/) gives you the reusable form chain.
2. [Present tense](./present/) builds the first productive finite form.
3. [Preterite](./preterite/) and [Perfect](./perfect/) separate the main past
   choices.
4. [Tense perspectives](./tense-perspectives/) connects those forms in a time
   map.
5. [Future forms](./future-forms/) adds the choice among future constructions.

## Watch for

- [Verb groups](../verb-patterns-voice/verb-groups/) helps you recognize
  recurring morphology; it does not decide which tense a situation needs.
- [Modal verbs](../verb-patterns-voice/modal-verbs/) includes <Sv>ska</Sv> as a
  modal pattern; Future Forms owns the choice between <Sv>ska</Sv> and other
  future expressions.
