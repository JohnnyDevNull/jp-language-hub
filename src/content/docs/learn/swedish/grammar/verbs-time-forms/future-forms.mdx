<KeyTakeaway>
- Use the present when a time expression or the situation already makes the
  future clear.
- Use <Sv>ska</Sv> for decisions, promises, arrangements, or expected events;
  use <Sv>tänker</Sv> for a personal intention.
- Use <Sv>kommer att</Sv> mainly for a prediction or development not presented
  as someone's plan.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish expresses future time with several ordinary constructions. Choose by
meaning instead of translating English *will* or German *werden* mechanically.

## Use this now

| Meaning | Useful choice | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| scheduled or clear from context | present | <Sv>Tåget går klockan åtta.</Sv> |
| decision, promise, arrangement, expectation | <Sv>ska</Sv> + infinitive | <Sv>Jag ska ringa i kväll.</Sv> |
| personal intention | <Sv>tänker</Sv> + infinitive | <Sv>Jag tänker söka jobbet.</Sv> |
| prediction or development | <Sv>kommer att</Sv> + infinitive | <Sv>Det kommer att regna.</Sv> |

These are tendencies, not four airtight boxes. Context and speaker perspective
can make more than one choice natural.

## Core rule

The contextual present needs a clear future anchor such as <Sv>i morgon</Sv>,
<Sv>nästa vecka</Sv>, or a scheduled event. <Sv>Ska</Sv> and intention
<Sv>tänker</Sv> take a bare infinitive, while <Sv>kommer att</Sv> includes the
infinitive marker <Sv>att</Sv>:

- <Sv>Jag ska resa.</Sv>
- <Sv>Jag tänker resa.</Sv>
- <Sv>Det kommer att regna.</Sv>

The <Sv>att</Sv> in <Sv>kommer att</Sv> belongs to that construction; it is not
part of the bare-infinitive pattern used after <Sv>ska</Sv> and intention
<Sv>tänker</Sv>.

## Direct comparison

</LanguageComparison>

</LanguageComparison>

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- Do not translate every English <em>will</em> or German
	  <span lang="de">werden</span> with <Sv>ska</Sv>.
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag ska att ringa.</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag ska ringa.</Sv>
	- A present form can refer to the future when the time is already clear:
	  <Sv>Vi ses i morgon.</Sv>
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Ask what makes the event future: the context, a plan, an intention, or a
prediction. Then choose the construction.
</MemoryTip>
