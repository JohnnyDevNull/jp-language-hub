<KeyTakeaway>
- The perfect is <span lang="sv">har</span> + supine, and <span lang="sv">har</span> is the same for every
  subject.
- Use it when the present result, the experience or an unfinished time period

- The supine is a form of its own: <span lang="sv">har köpt</span>, never
  <span lang="sv">har köpa</span> or <span lang="sv">har köpte</span>.

The Swedish perfect connects a past event or state to the present. It is formed
with present-tense <span lang="sv">har</span> plus the verb's supine.

## Core rule

**Pattern:** <span lang="sv">har</span> + supine

- <span lang="sv">Jag har köpt en bok.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Hon har skrivit ett mejl.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Vi har bott här länge.</span>

The auxiliary <span lang="sv">har</span> is identical for every subject. The
supine is a dedicated verb form:

<MistakeBox title="Supine, not infinitive">
After <span lang="sv">har</span>, use <span lang="sv">köpt</span>, not
<span lang="sv">köpa</span> or <span lang="sv">köpte</span>.
</MistakeBox>

## Main uses

### Present result

<span lang="sv">Jag har tappat nyckeln.</span>

The key is missing now; the current result matters.

### Experience up to now

<span lang="sv">Har du varit i Kiruna?</span>

The question concerns the person's experience, not one specified past visit.

### A situation continuing until now

<span lang="sv">Vi har bott här i tre år.</span>

Without further context, this normally means that we still live here.

### An unfinished time period

<span lang="sv">Jag har läst två kapitel idag.</span>

Today is treated as still current. If the speaker instead narrates a completed
past occasion, the preterite may be the appropriate perspective.

## Perfect or preterite?

| Focus | Swedish example |
| --- | --- |
| Present relevance or experience | <span lang="sv">Jag har sett filmen.</span> |
| Completed past time | <span lang="sv">Jag såg filmen i lördags.</span> |

## Direct comparison

	<p slot="sv">Jag har sett filmen.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Completed past time">
	<p slot="de">Ich habe den Film am Samstag gesehen.</p>
	<p slot="en">I saw the film on Saturday.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag såg filmen i lördags.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

English provides a useful structural bridge—<span lang="en">have + past
participle</span>—but English and Swedish tense choices do not align in every
context. German also forms a perfect with an auxiliary and a participle, yet
spoken German uses it more broadly for ordinary past narration: the second
block above keeps the German perfect where Swedish takes the preterite.

## Negation and word order

In a main clause, <span lang="sv">inte</span> follows the finite auxiliary:

<span lang="sv">Jag har inte läst boken.</span>

In a subordinate clause, it normally precedes the finite auxiliary:

<span lang="sv">… eftersom jag inte har läst boken.</span>

Only <span lang="sv">har</span> is finite; <span lang="sv">läst</span> is the
supine. This distinction helps when applying V2 and BIFF.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Jag har köpa boken.</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Jag har köpt boken.</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Jag har köpte boken.</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Jag har köpt boken.</span>
	- Avoid mechanically copying the German perfect whenever Swedish places the
	  event in a finished past time frame.
</MistakeBox>
