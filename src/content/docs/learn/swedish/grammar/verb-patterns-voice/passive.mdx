<KeyTakeaway>
- The s-passive uses an <span lang="sv">-s</span> form across common infinitive, present,
  past, and compound patterns; the exact form follows the verb's morphology.
- <span lang="sv">Bli</span> + participle presents an event or change, while <span lang="sv">vara</span> +
  participle presents a state; the participle agrees with the subject.
- An <span lang="sv">-s</span> form is not always a passive: <span lang="sv">hoppas</span> and
  <span lang="sv">Vi ses!</span> are not.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish has an **s-passive** built into the verb form and participial passive
constructions with <span lang="sv">bli</span> or <span lang="sv">vara</span>. German speakers often notice
the familiar shape of <span lang="sv">bli</span> + participle, but the constructions do not
map one-to-one across the languages.

This page owns formation and recognition. For information focus, agent
omission, and the choice between active and passive wording, see
[Active vs passive](../active-vs-passive/).

## The s-passive

The <span lang="sv">-s</span> pattern appears in the infinitive, present, preterite, and
supine. The exact present form depends on the verb pattern: group 1 commonly
has <span lang="sv">-as</span>, many other verbs use the stem plus <span lang="sv">-s</span>, and stems
already ending in an s-sound may use <span lang="sv">-es</span>, as in <span lang="sv">läsa → läses</span>.
Preterite and supine forms commonly add <span lang="sv">-s</span> to the corresponding active
form. Use the form chain rather than one exceptionless spelling rule:

| Form | Active | Passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | <span lang="sv">öppna</span> | <span lang="sv">öppnas</span> |
| Present | <span lang="sv">öppnar</span> | <span lang="sv">öppnas</span> |
| Preterite | <span lang="sv">öppnade</span> | <span lang="sv">öppnades</span> |
| Supine | <span lang="sv">öppnat</span> | <span lang="sv">öppnats</span> |

Other verb groups produce forms such as these:

| Verb | Present | Preterite | Supine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">köpa</span> | <span lang="sv">köps</span> | <span lang="sv">köptes</span> | <span lang="sv">köpts</span> |
| <span lang="sv">skriva</span> | <span lang="sv">skrivs</span> | <span lang="sv">skrevs</span> | <span lang="sv">skrivits</span> |

Examples in use:

- <span lang="sv">Rummet städas varje dag.</span> — *The room is cleaned every
  day.*
- <span lang="sv">Cykeln stals i går.</span> — *The bicycle was stolen
  yesterday.*
- <span lang="sv">Boken skrevs av Astrid Lindgren.</span> — *The book was
  written by Astrid Lindgren.*

The agent, when mentioned at all, is introduced by
<span lang="sv">av</span>.

<RuleBox title="Recognizing the s-passive">
	Identify the underlying verb and check whether the subject is the affected
	participant: <span lang="sv">Dörrarna stängs automatiskt.</span> An <span lang="sv">-s</span> ending by
	itself is not proof of passive meaning.
</RuleBox>

## Participial passives with <span lang="sv">bli</span> and <span lang="sv">vara</span>

<span lang="sv">Bli</span> plus the past participle commonly presents an event or change of
state:

- <span lang="sv">Dörren blev öppnad.</span> — *The door was opened.*
- <span lang="sv">Han blev vald till ordförande.</span> — *He was elected
  chairman.*

The participle behaves like an adjective and agrees with the subject:

| Subject | Example |
| --- | --- |
| en-word | <span lang="sv">Bilen blev såld.</span> |
| ett-word | <span lang="sv">Huset blev sålt.</span> |
| plural | <span lang="sv">Böckerna blev sålda.</span> |

See [adjective agreement](../../adjectives-adverbs/agreement/)
for the endings.

With <span lang="sv">vara</span> instead of <span lang="sv">bli</span>, the
same participle describes a resulting state rather than the event:
<span lang="sv">Huset är sålt.</span>

## Formation map

| Construction | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| s-passive, present | present stem/form + <span lang="sv">-s</span> | <span lang="sv">Biljetter köps i appen.</span> |
| s-passive, preterite | preterite + <span lang="sv">-s</span> | <span lang="sv">Boken skrevs 1950.</span> |
| event/change passive | finite <span lang="sv">bli</span> + agreeing participle | <span lang="sv">Han blev bjuden.</span> |
| resulting state | finite <span lang="sv">vara</span> + agreeing participle | <span lang="sv">Dörren är stängd.</span> |

The s-passive can also describe a specific event, as in <span lang="sv">Cykeln stals i
går</span>. <span lang="sv">Bli</span> highlights the event or transition more explicitly; it
does not own every single-event passive. See [Active vs passive](../active-vs-passive/)
for the communicative choice and the active alternative with <span lang="sv">man</span>.

## s-verbs that are not passive

Some verbs simply end in <span lang="sv">-s</span> and have no active form.
They are not passives, and translating them as one leads nowhere:

| Verb | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">finns</span> | there is, exists |
| <span lang="sv">hoppas</span> | to hope |
| <span lang="sv">trivs</span> | to feel at home, to enjoy being somewhere |
| <span lang="sv">minns</span> | to remember |
| <span lang="sv">lyckas</span> | to succeed |
| <span lang="sv">andas</span> | to breathe |

- <span lang="sv">Jag hoppas att du mår bra.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Jag trivs i Malmö.</span>

## The reciprocal -s

An <span lang="sv">-s</span> form can also mean that two or more people do
something *to each other*:

- <span lang="sv">Vi ses!</span> — *See you!*
- <span lang="sv">De träffas varje vecka.</span> — *They meet every week.*
- <span lang="sv">De kramades.</span> — *They hugged.*

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- <span lang="sv">Rummet städas varje dag</span> is the compact routine pattern; do not
	  infer that <span lang="sv">bli</span> is reserved for every single event.
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Huset blev sålld</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Huset blev sålt.</span> — the participle agrees with the
	  subject
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Jag hoppar att du mår bra</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Jag hoppas att du mår bra.</span> —
	  <span lang="sv">hoppas</span> has no active form
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">Boken skrevs från Astrid Lindgren</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">Boken skrevs av Astrid Lindgren.</span>
	- Do not reach for <span lang="sv">bli</span> just because German uses
	  <span lang="de">werden</span>.
	</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Three short sentences separate the central patterns:
<span lang="sv"><strong>Rummet städas varje dag</strong></span> — routine.
<span lang="sv"><strong>Dörren blev öppnad</strong></span> — event or change.
<span lang="sv"><strong>Vi ses!</strong></span> — each other, not passive at
all.
</MemoryTip>
