<KeyTakeaway>
- The s-passive uses an <Sv>-s</Sv> form across common infinitive, present,
  past, and compound patterns; the exact form follows the verb's morphology.
- <Sv>Bli</Sv> + participle presents an event or change, while <Sv>vara</Sv> +
  participle presents a state; the participle agrees with the subject.
- An <Sv>-s</Sv> form is not always a passive: <Sv>hoppas</Sv> and
  <Sv>Vi ses!</Sv> are not.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Swedish has an **s-passive** built into the verb form and participial passive
constructions with <Sv>bli</Sv> or <Sv>vara</Sv>. German speakers often notice
the familiar shape of <Sv>bli</Sv> + participle, but the constructions do not
map one-to-one across the languages.

This page owns formation and recognition. For information focus, agent
omission, and the choice between active and passive wording, see
[Active vs passive](../active-vs-passive/).

## The s-passive

The <Sv>-s</Sv> pattern appears in the infinitive, present, preterite, and
supine. The exact present form depends on the verb pattern: group 1 commonly
has <Sv>-as</Sv>, many other verbs use the stem plus <Sv>-s</Sv>, and stems
already ending in an s-sound may use <Sv>-es</Sv>, as in <Sv>läsa → läses</Sv>.
Preterite and supine forms commonly add <Sv>-s</Sv> to the corresponding active
form. Use the form chain rather than one exceptionless spelling rule:

| Form | Active | Passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | <Sv>öppna</Sv> | <Sv>öppnas</Sv> |
| Present | <Sv>öppnar</Sv> | <Sv>öppnas</Sv> |
| Preterite | <Sv>öppnade</Sv> | <Sv>öppnades</Sv> |
| Supine | <Sv>öppnat</Sv> | <Sv>öppnats</Sv> |

Other verb groups produce forms such as these:

| Verb | Present | Preterite | Supine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <Sv>köpa</Sv> | <Sv>köps</Sv> | <Sv>köptes</Sv> | <Sv>köpts</Sv> |
| <Sv>skriva</Sv> | <Sv>skrivs</Sv> | <Sv>skrevs</Sv> | <Sv>skrivits</Sv> |

Examples in use:

- <Sv>Rummet städas varje dag.</Sv> — *The room is cleaned every
  day.*
- <Sv>Cykeln stals i går.</Sv> — *The bicycle was stolen
  yesterday.*
- <Sv>Boken skrevs av Astrid Lindgren.</Sv> — *The book was
  written by Astrid Lindgren.*

The agent, when mentioned at all, is introduced by
<Sv>av</Sv>.

<RuleBox title="Recognizing the s-passive">
	Identify the underlying verb and check whether the subject is the affected
	participant: <Sv>Dörrarna stängs automatiskt.</Sv> An <Sv>-s</Sv> ending by
	itself is not proof of passive meaning.
</RuleBox>

## Participial passives with <Sv>bli</Sv> and <Sv>vara</Sv>

<Sv>Bli</Sv> plus the past participle commonly presents an event or change of
state:

- <Sv>Dörren blev öppnad.</Sv> — *The door was opened.*
- <Sv>Han blev vald till ordförande.</Sv> — *He was elected
  chairman.*

The participle behaves like an adjective and agrees with the subject:

| Subject | Example |
| --- | --- |
| en-word | <Sv>Bilen blev såld.</Sv> |
| ett-word | <Sv>Huset blev sålt.</Sv> |
| plural | <Sv>Böckerna blev sålda.</Sv> |

See [adjective agreement](../../adjectives-adverbs/agreement/)
for the endings.

With <Sv>vara</Sv> instead of <Sv>bli</Sv>, the
same participle describes a resulting state rather than the event:
<Sv>Huset är sålt.</Sv>

## Formation map

| Construction | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| s-passive, present | present stem/form + <Sv>-s</Sv> | <Sv>Biljetter köps i appen.</Sv> |
| s-passive, preterite | preterite + <Sv>-s</Sv> | <Sv>Boken skrevs 1950.</Sv> |
| event/change passive | finite <Sv>bli</Sv> + agreeing participle | <Sv>Han blev bjuden.</Sv> |
| resulting state | finite <Sv>vara</Sv> + agreeing participle | <Sv>Dörren är stängd.</Sv> |

The s-passive can also describe a specific event, as in <Sv>Cykeln stals i
går</Sv>. <Sv>Bli</Sv> highlights the event or transition more explicitly; it
does not own every single-event passive. See [Active vs passive](../active-vs-passive/)
for the communicative choice and the active alternative with <Sv>man</Sv>.

## s-verbs that are not passive

Some verbs simply end in <Sv>-s</Sv> and have no active form.
They are not passives, and translating them as one leads nowhere:

| Verb | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <Sv>finns</Sv> | there is, exists |
| <Sv>hoppas</Sv> | to hope |
| <Sv>trivs</Sv> | to feel at home, to enjoy being somewhere |
| <Sv>minns</Sv> | to remember |
| <Sv>lyckas</Sv> | to succeed |
| <Sv>andas</Sv> | to breathe |

- <Sv>Jag hoppas att du mår bra.</Sv>
- <Sv>Jag trivs i Malmö.</Sv>

## The reciprocal -s

An <Sv>-s</Sv> form can also mean that two or more people do
something *to each other*:

- <Sv>Vi ses!</Sv> — *See you!*
- <Sv>De träffas varje vecka.</Sv> — *They meet every week.*
- <Sv>De kramades.</Sv> — *They hugged.*

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- <Sv>Rummet städas varje dag</Sv> is the compact routine pattern; do not
	  infer that <Sv>bli</Sv> is reserved for every single event.
	- ❌ <Sv>Huset blev sålld</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Huset blev sålt.</Sv> — the participle agrees with the
	  subject
	- ❌ <Sv>Jag hoppar att du mår bra</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Jag hoppas att du mår bra.</Sv> —
	  <Sv>hoppas</Sv> has no active form
	- ❌ <Sv>Boken skrevs från Astrid Lindgren</Sv>
	  ✅ <Sv>Boken skrevs av Astrid Lindgren.</Sv>
	- Do not reach for <Sv>bli</Sv> just because German uses
	  <span lang="de">werden</span>.
	</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Three short sentences separate the central patterns:
<Sv><strong>Rummet städas varje dag</strong></Sv> — routine.
<Sv><strong>Dörren blev öppnad</strong></Sv> — event or change.
<Sv><strong>Vi ses!</strong></Sv> — each other, not passive at
all.
</MemoryTip>
