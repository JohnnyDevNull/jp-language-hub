<KeyTakeaway>
- Choose active voice when the actor is known and relevant; choose passive
  when the event, result, or affected participant matters more.
- Omit the agent when it is unknown, obvious, generic, or intentionally
  backgrounded.
- Everyday Swedish often uses active <span lang="sv">man</span> when English or German uses
  an impersonal passive.
</KeyTakeaway>

This page is about communicative focus. For the formation and recognition of
the s-passive, <span lang="sv">bli</span> + participle, and <span lang="sv">vara</span> + participle, use
[Passive](../passive/).

## Use this now

| What you want to foreground | Useful choice | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the actor and what they do | active | <span lang="sv">Anna öppnade dörren.</span> |
| the event or affected participant | passive | <span lang="sv">Dörren öppnades.</span> |
| people in general in everyday speech | active with <span lang="sv">man</span> | <span lang="sv">Man betalar i kassan.</span> |
| the actor despite passive focus | passive + <span lang="sv">av</span> | <span lang="sv">Boken skrevs av Selma Lagerlöf.</span> |

## Core choice

Active voice gives the actor the subject role. Passive voice promotes the
affected participant or presents the event without naming an actor. The
passive agent is optional and introduced with <span lang="sv">av</span> when it is useful.

Agent omission is natural when the actor is:

- unknown: <span lang="sv">Min cykel blev stulen.</span>
- obvious from the situation: <span lang="sv">Ansökan behandlas nu.</span>
- generic: <span lang="sv">Här säljs biljetter.</span>
- less important than the result: <span lang="sv">Vägen är stängd.</span>

If the actor is the new or important information, an active sentence is often
clearer: <span lang="sv">Kommunen stängde vägen.</span>

## Generic <span lang="sv">man</span>

<span lang="sv">Man</span> keeps the clause active while leaving the actor generic:
<span lang="sv">I Sverige dricker man mycket kaffe.</span> It is especially useful in spoken,
personal, or instructional contexts. A passive can sound more compact or
institutional: <span lang="sv">Här serveras kaffe.</span>

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- Do not keep a passive merely because the German or English source has one.
	  Ask whether Swedish should foreground the actor, the event, or the result.
	- Do not add an <span lang="sv">av</span> phrase when the agent is obvious or irrelevant.
	- Use [Passive](../passive/) for form choice; this page does not replace its
	  formation tables.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Choose the subject by focus: actor first means active; event or affected thing
first often means passive. For generic people, test active <span lang="sv">man</span>.
</MemoryTip>
