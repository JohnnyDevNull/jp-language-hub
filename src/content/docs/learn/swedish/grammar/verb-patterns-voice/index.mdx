<KeyTakeaway>
- Use Verb Groups for recurring form patterns and Modal Verbs for meaning plus
  the infinitive pattern that follows.
- Particle Verbs are lexical units whose particle stays beside the verb.
- Passive teaches formation; Active vs Passive teaches communicative choice;
  Imperative teaches direct commands and instructions.
</KeyTakeaway>

This shelf collects patterns that change a verb's form, complement, focus, or
communicative job without turning them into time-form decisions.

## Choose by

| If you need to… | Start with… |
| --- | --- |
| recognize regular conjugation patterns | [Verb groups](./verb-groups/) |
| understand or place a stressed particle | [Particle verbs](./particle-verbs/) |
| express ability, obligation, desire, intention, or expectation | [Modal verbs](./modal-verbs/) |
| form and recognize an s-passive or participial passive | [Passive](./passive/) |
| decide whether active or passive focus fits | [Active vs passive](./active-vs-passive/) |
| give a direct instruction | [Imperative](./imperative/) |

## Learn in this order

1. [Verb groups](./verb-groups/) reveals recurring morphology.
2. [Particle verbs](./particle-verbs/) adds high-frequency lexical patterns.
3. [Modal verbs](./modal-verbs/) builds reusable two-verb constructions.
4. [Passive](./passive/) teaches the available forms, then
   [Active vs passive](./active-vs-passive/) teaches the choice.
5. [Imperative](./imperative/) completes the core command pattern.

## Watch for

- [Verb forms](../verbs-time-forms/verb-forms/) owns the general form chain;
  Verb Groups only organizes recurring morphology.
- Passive formation and active/passive choice are deliberately separate lookup
  questions.
