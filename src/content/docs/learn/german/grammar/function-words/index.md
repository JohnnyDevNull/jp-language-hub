Negation is not just a vocabulary choice between *nicht* and *kein*. The
useful question is scope: what exactly is being negated?

## Choose by negation problem

| If you need to negate... | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| the whole idea of negation in German | [Negation](./negation/) |
| an indefinite noun phrase such as *a book* or *any coffee* | [Nicht vs kein](./nicht-vs-kein/) |
| a verb, adjective, adverb, phrase, or whole clause | [Nicht placement](./nicht-placement/) |
| nobody, nothing, never, or nowhere | [Negative pronouns and adverbs](./negative-pronouns-and-adverbs/) |

## Fast rule

- Use *kein* when the thing being negated is an indefinite noun phrase:
  *kein Kaffee*, *keine Zeit*, *kein Problem*.
- Use *nicht* when you negate a verb, adjective, adverb, definite noun phrase,
  prepositional phrase, or whole clause.
- Place *nicht* by asking what it negates, then check the
  [verb bracket](../sentence-structure/verb-bracket/) if the sentence has late
  verb pieces.
