Use dieser, jener, der, and das to point clearly at people, things, and ideas.

## Use this now

They carry gender, number, and case like article words. Use an attributive
form before a noun (<span lang="de">dieses Buch</span>) or a standalone form
when the noun is understood (<span lang="de">Ich nehme dieses</span>).

## Core rule

<span lang="de">dieser</span> means this/these; forms of <span lang="de">der</span>
can also stand alone as “that one / he / she” in speech. Choose the ending from
the noun phrase role:

| Case | Masculine | Feminine | Neuter | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nominative | <span lang="de">dieser</span> | <span lang="de">diese</span> | <span lang="de">dieses</span> | <span lang="de">diese</span> |
| accusative | <span lang="de">diesen</span> | <span lang="de">diese</span> | <span lang="de">dieses</span> | <span lang="de">diese</span> |
| dative | <span lang="de">diesem</span> | <span lang="de">dieser</span> | <span lang="de">diesem</span> | <span lang="de">diesen</span> |



<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Ich nehme diese Buch."
	correct="Ich nehme dieses Buch."
	lang="de"
>
	<span lang="de">Buch</span> is neuter accusative, so the demonstrative is <span lang="de">dieses</span>.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
A demonstrative is an article signal with a pointing finger attached.
</MemoryTip>
