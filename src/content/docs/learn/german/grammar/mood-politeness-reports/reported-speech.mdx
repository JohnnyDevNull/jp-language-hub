<KeyTakeaway>
- Reported speech separates your voice from another source.
- In careful writing, German often uses Konjunktiv I for reported statements. In everyday speech, indicative or Konjunktiv II may appear.
- In everyday conversation, indicative is normal; use Konjunktiv I when formal source distance matters.
</KeyTakeaway>

Use reported speech to mark that information comes from someone else.

## Use this now

Start by making the source clear. In everyday speech, indicative is normal; in
careful reporting, Konjunktiv I can make the distance explicit.

## Core rule

The main task is source marking, not automatic tense backshifting.

| Context | Natural report |
| --- | --- |
| everyday conversation | <span lang="de">Er sagt, er hat keine Zeit.</span> |
| formal report | <span lang="de">Er sagt, er habe keine Zeit.</span> |
| distance from a source when Konjunktiv I is unclear | <span lang="de">Sie sagte, sie käme später.</span> |



<MistakeBox
		incorrect="Er sagt, ich habe keine Zeit."
		correct="Er sagt, er hat keine Zeit."
	lang="de"
>
		Change the pronoun when you report another person's statement. The indicative is natural in ordinary speech; formal writing may use <span lang="de">er habe</span> instead.
</MistakeBox>
