<KeyTakeaway>
- Use past perfect when one past event happened before another past reference point.
- Form it with <span lang="de">hatte</span> or <span lang="de">war</span> plus the past participle.
- The earlier event is past perfect; the later narrative event is usually perfect or preterite.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

German past perfect adds a clear “already happened” layer inside a past story.

## Use this now

Choose past perfect when two past events need an order:
<span lang="de">Nachdem ich gegessen hatte, ging ich spazieren.</span>
Eating happened first; going for a walk is the later reference event.

## Core rule

Form past perfect with the past tense of the auxiliary plus the past
participle:

| Verb type | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| most verbs | <span lang="de">hatte</span> + participle | <span lang="de">Ich hatte gearbeitet.</span> |
| movement or change-of-state verbs | <span lang="de">war</span> + participle | <span lang="de">Sie war gegangen.</span> |

In a main clause, the finite auxiliary stays in position two and the
participial bracket closes at the end:
<span lang="de">Ich hatte den Bericht schon gelesen.</span> In a subordinate
clause, the auxiliary moves to the end:
<span lang="de">..., weil ich den Bericht schon gelesen hatte.</span>

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Nachdem ich gegessen habe, ging ich spazieren."
	correct="Nachdem ich gegessen hatte, ging ich spazieren."
	lang="de"
>
	When the sentence explicitly orders two past events, use past perfect for
	the earlier one. Simple past or perfect can still be enough when the order is
	already clear from the context.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Past perfect is the past before the past: <span lang="de">hatte/war</span> plus
the participle.
</MemoryTip>
