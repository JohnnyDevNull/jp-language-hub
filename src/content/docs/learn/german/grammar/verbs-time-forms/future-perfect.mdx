Use German future perfect for something completed before a future reference point or for assumptions about the past.

## Use this now

Use it for completion before a deadline: <span lang="de">Bis Freitag werde ich es erledigt haben</span> or assumptions: <span lang="de">Er wird es vergessen haben</span>.

## Core rule

Form it with finite <span lang="de">werden</span> plus a perfect infinitive:
<span lang="de">werden</span> + participle + <span lang="de">haben/sein</span>.
Use <span lang="de">haben</span> for most verbs and <span lang="de">sein</span>
for the same movement/change-of-state verbs that use <span lang="de">sein</span>
in the perfect.



<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Bis Freitag werde ich es gemacht."
	correct="Bis Freitag werde ich es gemacht haben."
	lang="de"
>
	Future perfect needs the participle and auxiliary: <span lang="de">gemacht haben</span>, not a bare participle.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Future perfect means future looking back.
</MemoryTip>

## Reference use: assumption about the past

Without a future deadline, future perfect can express a confident guess about
an earlier event: <span lang="de">Er wird den Zug verpasst haben</span> means
“He has probably missed the train.” The meaning comes from context and
intonation, not from a literal future date.
