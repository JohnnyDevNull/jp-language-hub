German verb choices are not only about tense. A learner usually needs to know
where the finite verb goes, what sits in the right bracket, whether a particle
moves, which time form fits the situation, and whether the sentence is active,
passive, hypothetical, polite, or reported.

## Sentence shape first

German verbs become easier once you see the sentence as a bracket. The finite
verb often takes the early slot, while participles, infinitives, passive
parts, and separable particles can land late.

- [Verb bracket](../sentence-structure/verb-bracket/) is the main model.
- [Main clause verb second](../sentence-structure/main-clause-verb-second/)
  explains the finite verb in position two.
- [Subordinate clauses](../sentence-structure/subordinate-clauses/) shows why
  the finite verb moves to the end after words like *weil*.
- [Particle verbs](./particle-verbs/) applies the same bracket idea to verbs
  such as *anrufen*, *aufstehen*, and *mitkommen*.

## Time forms

Start with [Choosing time form](./choosing-time-form/) when you know the
meaning but are unsure which German form carries it.

### Present and past

- [Present tense](./present-tense/) covers facts, habits, current states, and
  many near-future events.
- [Perfect tense](./perfect-tense/) is the default spoken-past form for many
  completed events.
- [Preterite](./preterite/) is especially important in writing and with common
  verbs such as *sein*, *haben*, and modal verbs.
- [Present vs perfect vs preterite](./present-vs-perfect-vs-preterite/) is the
  contrast page for choosing between them.

### Future

- [Werden future](./werden-future/) covers prediction, assumption, promise, and
  explicit future emphasis.
- [Future perfect](./future-perfect/) covers completion before a future point
  or an assumption about a completed event.
- [Present tense for future](./present-tense-for-future/) explains the everyday
  pattern *Morgen fahre ich...*.
- [Choosing future forms](./choosing-future-forms/) compares the future
  options without duplicating the full tense pages.
