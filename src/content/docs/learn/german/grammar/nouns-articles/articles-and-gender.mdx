German articles are small, but they carry much of the grammar of the noun phrase.

## Use this now

Choose in this order: noun gender, singular or plural, definite or indefinite meaning, case from the sentence, then the visible article form.

## Core rule

The noun owns gender; the sentence assigns case; the article displays both. That is why <span lang="de">der Mann</span> can become <span lang="de">den Mann</span> or <span lang="de">dem Mann</span>.

| | masculine | feminine | neuter | plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| nominative | <span lang="de">der Mann</span> | <span lang="de">die Frau</span> | <span lang="de">das Kind</span> | <span lang="de">die Kinder</span> |
| accusative | <span lang="de">den Mann</span> | <span lang="de">die Frau</span> | <span lang="de">das Kind</span> | <span lang="de">die Kinder</span> |
| dative | <span lang="de">dem Mann</span> | <span lang="de">der Frau</span> | <span lang="de">dem Kind</span> | <span lang="de">den Kindern</span> |

The article is not chosen from gender alone: <span lang="de">der</span> can be
masculine nominative or feminine dative. Ask for the sentence role before
looking up the form.
