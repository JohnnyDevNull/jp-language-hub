<KeyTakeaway>
- Der, die, das mean “the”, but they also mark gender and case.
- Plural definite nominative and accusative use die.
- Masculine singular changes most visibly across cases: der, den, dem, des.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

The definite article is the clearest signal system in German noun phrases.

## Use this now

Start with nominative: <span lang="de">der Tisch</span>, <span lang="de">die Lampe</span>, <span lang="de">das Buch</span>, <span lang="de">die Bücher</span>. Then adjust for case.

## Core rule

Definite articles identify a known noun and display its gender, number, and case. They are the model for many adjective endings.



<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Ich gebe der Mann das Buch."
	correct="Ich gebe dem Mann das Buch."
	lang="de"
>
	A recipient is dative, so masculine <span lang="de">der</span> becomes <span lang="de">dem</span>.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Use definite articles as your case compass: der, den, dem, des.
