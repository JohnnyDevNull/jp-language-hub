Recognize German particle verbs and decide whether the particle separates.

## Use this now

Ask three questions:

1. Is the prefix separable, inseparable, or dependent on meaning?
2. Does the clause have a right bracket position?
3. Is the whole verb meaning different enough that you should learn it as one vocabulary item?

## Core rule

A meaning-bearing, stressed particle is often separable; an unstressed prefix is
usually inseparable. This is a useful first signal, not a substitute for
learning a verb with its meaning and principal forms.

The particle may move, but the meaning stays with the verb. <span lang="de">rufen</span> means "call/shout"; <span lang="de">anrufen</span> means "call by phone". Treat <span lang="de">anrufen</span>, <span lang="de">aufstehen</span>, <span lang="de">mitkommen</span>, and <span lang="de">anfangen</span> as whole entries.

Compare the meaning and clause pattern, not a German particle with an English
particle word for word. German may use a separable verb, an inseparable prefix
verb, or a different lexical verb.
