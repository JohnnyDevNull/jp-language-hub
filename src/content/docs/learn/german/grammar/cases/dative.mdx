<KeyTakeaway>
- The dative often marks the receiver or beneficiary.
- Many common prepositions always take dative: mit, nach, bei, seit, von, zu, aus.
- Two-way prepositions use dative for location.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Dative is the case of “to/for someone” and of many everyday prepositional phrases.

## Use this now

Check for a receiver, a fixed dative preposition, or a static location: <span lang="de">dem Kind</span>, <span lang="de">mit dem Zug</span>, <span lang="de">auf dem Tisch</span>.

## Core rule

Dative answers “to whom?”, “for whom?”, and often “where?” after a two-way preposition.

Some verbs select dative without an English “to” phrase, especially
<span lang="de">helfen</span>, <span lang="de">danken</span>,
<span lang="de">gefallen</span>, and <span lang="de">gehören</span>:
<span lang="de">Das Buch gefällt der Frau.</span> The static-location meaning
applies to two-way prepositions only; fixed dative prepositions such as
<span lang="de">mit</span> and <span lang="de">bei</span> always stay dative.
