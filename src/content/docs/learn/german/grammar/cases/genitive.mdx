<KeyTakeaway>
- The genitive often means “of” or possession.
- It is common in formal writing and fixed phrases, less central in everyday speech.
- Some prepositions require genitive in standard written German.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Genitive is less frequent than the other cases in conversation, but a grammar reference needs it for reading and formal style.

## Use this now

Recognize <span lang="de">des Mannes</span>, <span lang="de">der Frau</span>, <span lang="de">wegen des Wetters</span>, and similar formal patterns.

## Core rule

Genitive links a noun phrase to another noun phrase as owner, source, or relation. Masculine and neuter singular often add -(e)s to the noun.

Use <span lang="de">des</span> for masculine and neuter singular and add
<span lang="de">-s</span> or <span lang="de">-es</span> to the noun:
<span lang="de">des Autos</span>, <span lang="de">des Kindes</span>.
Feminine and plural use <span lang="de">der</span> without a noun ending:
<span lang="de">der Frau</span>, <span lang="de">der Kinder</span>.
In everyday speech, a phrase with <span lang="de">von</span> often replaces
genitive possession, but genitive remains common in writing and fixed phrases.
