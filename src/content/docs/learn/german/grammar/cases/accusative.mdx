<KeyTakeaway>
- The accusative marks the directly affected object.
- Masculine singular shows the clearest article change: der becomes den.
- Two-way prepositions use accusative for motion toward a target.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

The accusative is the case learners notice first because masculine articles visibly change.

## Use this now

Use accusative when something is seen, bought, read, opened, or moved into a target: <span lang="de">Ich kaufe den Kaffee</span>, <span lang="de">Ich gehe in den Park</span>.

## Core rule

Accusative answers “who or what is directly affected?” With two-way prepositions it answers “where to?”

Frequent accusative verbs include <span lang="de">sehen</span>,
<span lang="de">haben</span>, <span lang="de">kaufen</span>,
<span lang="de">lesen</span>, and <span lang="de">fragen</span>:
<span lang="de">Ich frage den Lehrer.</span> The case belongs to the object,
not to its position in the sentence.

| Noun phrase | Accusative form |
| --- | --- |
| masculine definite | <span lang="de">den Mann</span> |
| feminine definite | <span lang="de">die Frau</span> |
| neuter definite | <span lang="de">das Kind</span> |
| plural definite | <span lang="de">die Kinder</span> |
