Prepositions are the bridge between abstract case roles and real phrases.
Some always take one case, while two-way prepositions choose by direction or
location.

## Fixed case

- [Accusative prepositions](./accusative-prepositions/) covers high-frequency
  words such as *durch*, *für*, *gegen*, *ohne*, and *um*.
- [Dative prepositions](./dative-prepositions/) covers words such as *mit*,
  *nach*, *bei*, *seit*, *von*, and *zu*.
- [Genitive prepositions](./genitive-prepositions/) keeps formal and written
  patterns visible without making them the beginner entry point.

## Direction vs location

[Two-way prepositions](./two-way-prepositions/) is the page for *in*, *an*,
*auf*, *unter*, *über*, and similar words. The core question is simple:
movement into a target usually points to accusative; location usually points
to dative.

## Prepositional objects

[Prepositional adverbs](./prepositional-adverbs/) explains *da-* and *wo-*
forms such as *damit*, *dafür*, and *woran*. These matter because English and
Swedish often keep the preposition plus pronoun where German prefers a compact
compound.

## Dependent combinations

[Dependent prepositions](./dependent-prepositions/) is a phrase lookup for
frequent combinations such as <span lang="de">warten auf</span> and
<span lang="de">stolz auf</span>. It complements the case pages: the
combination tells you which preposition to learn with the verb or adjective,
while the preposition still determines the following case.
