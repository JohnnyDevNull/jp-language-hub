- Durch, für, gegen, ohne, and um normally take accusative.
- Bis often appears without an article or with another preposition.
- The preposition, not English meaning, decides the case.

Fixed accusative prepositions make case choice easier: once you see the preposition, the following noun phrase is accusative.

## Use this now

Memorize the core set as a phrase: <span lang="de">durch, für, gegen, ohne, um</span>. Example: <span lang="de">für den Freund</span>.

## Core rule

A fixed accusative preposition assigns accusative to its object even if English does not feel like a direct object.

The high-frequency core is <span lang="de">durch, für, gegen, ohne,
um</span>. <span lang="de">Bis</span> behaves differently: it is often used
without an article (<span lang="de">bis Montag</span>) or together with another
preposition (<span lang="de">bis zum Abend</span>), so do not force it into the
same list.



<MistakeBox
	incorrect="für der Freund"
