<KeyTakeaway>
- Aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, and zu take dative.
- Dative prepositions are extremely common in everyday German.
- Many useful contractions come from preposition plus article: zum, zur, vom, beim.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Dative prepositions are high-value chunks because they appear in travel, time, work, and social phrases.

## Use this now

Use dative after <span lang="de">aus, bei, mit, nach, seit, von, zu</span>: <span lang="de">mit dem Zug</span>, <span lang="de">zur Arbeit</span>.

## Core rule

A fixed dative preposition assigns dative to its object. The phrase may describe direction, origin, company, or time; the case stays dative.

Add the less frequent but useful forms <span lang="de">außer</span> and
<span lang="de">gegenüber</span>: <span lang="de">außer mir</span>,
<span lang="de">gegenüber dem Bahnhof</span>. Some prepositions can appear
before or after the phrase, but the case remains dative.



<MistakeBox
	incorrect="zu die Schule"

	<span lang="de">zu</span> takes dative, and <span lang="de">zu der</span> contracts to <span lang="de">zur</span>.
