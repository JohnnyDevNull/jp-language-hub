- Two-way prepositions choose accusative for “where to?”
- They choose dative for “where?”
- The verb often helps: stellen/legen/setzen are direction; stehen/liegen/sitzen are location.

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Two-way prepositions are the bridge between case and movement: the same preposition can take two cases.

## Use this now

Ask one question: <span lang="de">wohin?</span> gives accusative; <span lang="de">wo?</span> gives dative. <span lang="de">Ich lege das Buch auf den Tisch</span>; <span lang="de">Das Buch liegt auf dem Tisch</span>.

## Core rule

The two-way set is <span lang="de">an, auf, hinter, in, neben, über, unter, vor, zwischen</span>. Direction into a target takes accusative; static position takes dative.

Movement alone is not enough: <span lang="de">Ich laufe im Park</span> stays
dative because the park is the location, while <span lang="de">Ich laufe in den
Park</span> is accusative because it names the destination. Let the question
<span lang="de">wo?</span> or <span lang="de">wohin?</span>, not the verb alone,
decide.
