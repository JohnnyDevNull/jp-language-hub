German grammar is easiest when the pages answer a decision, not when they
throw endings at you first. Start with the sentence role, then choose the
visible signal, the verb position, the scope, the time form, the voice, and the
mood.

## Start by problem

| If you are trying to... | Open |
| --- | --- |
| find out who does what to whom | [Cases](./cases/) |
| know which case a small word triggers | [Prepositions](./prepositions/) |
| choose gender, article, plural, or compound-noun signals | [Nouns and articles](./nouns-articles/) |
| replace noun phrases with the right form | [Pronouns](./pronouns/) |
| place the finite verb or build a subordinate clause | [Sentence structure](./sentence-structure/) |
| negate a noun phrase, verb, quality, or whole clause | [Function words and negation](./function-words/) |
| choose time form, passive, modal, particle verb, or mood | [Verbs](./verbs/) |
| add endings, compare qualities, or use adverbs | [Adjectives and adverbs](./adjectives/) |

## Decision chain

1. [Cases](./cases/) names the role.
2. [Prepositions](./prepositions/) and [pronouns](./pronouns/) apply that role
   in real phrases.
3. [Nouns and articles](./nouns-articles/) shows how gender, number, and case
   become visible.
4. [Sentence structure](./sentence-structure/) puts the verb and clause pieces
   in order.
5. [Function words](./function-words/) handles negation and scope.
6. [Verbs](./verbs/) handles time, voice, mood, modals, and particle verbs.
7. [Adjectives](./adjectives/) completes noun-phrase description and
   comparison.
