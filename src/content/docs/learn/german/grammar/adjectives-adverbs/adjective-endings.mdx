Choose German adjective endings from article signal, gender, number, and case.

## Use this now

Use this four-step check before you look at a table:

1. Is the adjective before the noun?
2. Which case does the noun phrase have?
3. Does the article already show gender, number, and case clearly?
4. Add the adjective ending that completes the missing signal.

## Core rule

German adjective endings share the noun phrase's gender, number, and case
signal. The ending pattern depends on whether the determiner already shows
that information:

| Noun phrase type | Signal logic | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definite article: weak | The article shows the signal, so the adjective usually uses weak endings. | <span lang="de">der gute Kaffee</span>, <span lang="de">den guten Kaffee</span> |
| Ein-word: mixed | The article shows some forms but not all; the adjective supplies the missing signal. | <span lang="de">ein guter Kaffee</span>, <span lang="de">einem guten Kaffee</span> |
| No article: strong | The adjective carries the full visible signal. | <span lang="de">guter Kaffee</span>, <span lang="de">frisches Wasser</span> |

Predicate adjectives do not take these endings: <span lang="de">Der Kaffee ist gut</span>, not <span lang="de">*Der Kaffee ist guter</span>. The ending belongs to the adjective inside the noun phrase.

## Signal sharing

The useful mental model is signal sharing: definite articles trigger weak
endings, ein-words trigger mixed endings, and no determiner triggers strong
endings. Learn the pattern through the article signal before consulting a full
paradigm.
