## Time forms

Use [Choosing time form](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/choosing-time-form/) when you know the
meaning but are unsure which English form carries it.

- [Simple present](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/simple-present/) and
  [present progressive](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/present-progressive/) explain the two
  main present forms.
- [Simple present vs present progressive](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/simple-present-vs-present-progressive/)
  helps decide between stable/routine and temporary/ongoing meanings.
- [Simple past](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/simple-past/) and
  [present perfect](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/present-perfect/) separate finished past
  time from past-to-now meaning.
- [Simple past vs present perfect](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/simple-past-vs-present-perfect/)
  is the main page for the German *Perfekt* transfer trap.
- [Will future](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/will-future/),
  [going to future](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/going-to-future/),
  [future progressive](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/future-progressive/), and
  [future perfect](./grammar/verbs-time-forms/future-perfect/) show that English future
  meaning is split across several forms.

## Nouns and articles

[Articles](./grammar/nouns-articles/articles/) introduces *a/an*, *the*, and
zero article. [Countable and uncountable nouns](./grammar/nouns-articles/countable-and-uncountable-nouns/)
explains why English says *information*, not *informations*, and why article
choice often depends on countability. [Plural nouns](./grammar/nouns-articles/plural-nouns/)
and [possession](./grammar/nouns-articles/possession/) complete the noun phrase.
When a known noun phrase becomes a pronoun, use [Pronouns and roles](./grammar/pronouns-roles/)
to choose the form for its English sentence job.
