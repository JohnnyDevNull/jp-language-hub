## Fronting is not English V2
<LanguageComparison title="Time first" languages={['en', 'sv']}>
	<p slot="en">Yesterday, I met her.</p><p slot="sv">Igår träffade jag henne.</p>
</LanguageComparison>
<MistakeBox incorrect="Yesterday met I her." correct="Yesterday, I met her." lang="en">Swedish moves the verb after the fronted phrase; English normally keeps **I met** together.</MistakeBox>
## Noun phrases and time
Swedish definiteness can be carried by a noun ending, but English needs an article or another determiner: *I bought a book; the book is interesting.* For time, Swedish present forms can cover an ongoing situation; English often needs a present perfect: *I have lived here for three years.*
## Practical lexical and preposition traps
Do not assume a similar-looking pattern has the same scope: *in the bus* usually means physically inside a bus as a space, while ordinary travel is *on the bus*. For word-level lookalikes and fixed translation traps, use the shared [False friends](../../../../false-friends/) and [Direct translation errors](../../../../direct-translation-errors/) pages rather than expanding this diagnosis page.
