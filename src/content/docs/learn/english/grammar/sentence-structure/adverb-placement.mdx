<KeyTakeaway>
- Put frequency adverbs before a main verb, after <span lang="en">be</span>, and after the first auxiliary.
- In a neutral clause, manner usually comes before place, and place before time.
- Put one adverbial first only when it is the focus; keep the subject--verb frame after it.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

English has two useful placement decisions: adverbs near the verb, and longer
adverbials after the verb phrase. This page covers frequency, manner, place,
time, and the usual order when more than one appears.

## Use this now

1. Is it a frequency word such as *often* or *never*? Put it near the verb.
2. Is it how, where, or when something happens? Put it after the verb phrase.
3. Do you have several? Use **manner + place + time** as the neutral default.

For the basic subject--verb--object frame, see [sentence order](../sentence-order/).

Frequency adverbs sit inside the verb phrase. Manner, place, and time normally
come after the main verb and its object or complement.

## Frequency near the verb

With one main verb, put *always*, *usually*, *often*, *sometimes*, *rarely*,
or *never* before it: <span lang="en">I often take the bus.</span>

With <span lang="en">be</span>, put the adverb after it:
<span lang="en">The bus is often late.</span> With an auxiliary, put it after
the first auxiliary: <span lang="en">I have never missed it.</span>

*Already* usually follows the first auxiliary or comes before a main verb;
*yet* usually ends a question or negative: <span lang="en">Have you finished yet?</span>

## Manner, place, and time

After a verb phrase, use this helpful default:

> **verb + object + manner + place + time**

- <span lang="en">She explained the plan clearly at the meeting yesterday.</span>
- <span lang="en">We ate quietly at home after work.</span>

Not every clause needs every slot. A time phrase can move to the opening for
focus: <span lang="en">Yesterday, we ate at home.</span> It does not cause
German-style inversion; see [sentence order](../sentence-order/).

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Frequency with a main verb" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Ich gehe oft zu Fuß zur Arbeit.</p>
	<p slot="en">I often walk to work.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German allows <span lang="de">oft</span> later in the clause. In neutral
English, put <span lang="en">often</span> before the main verb.
