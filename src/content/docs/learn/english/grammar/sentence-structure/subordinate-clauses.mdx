<KeyTakeaway>
- A subordinating conjunction introduces a clause that depends on another clause.
- English keeps normal subject--verb order after *because*, *when*, *if*, and *although*.
- Put a comma after a subordinate clause when it comes first.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Use a subordinate clause to give a reason, time, condition, or contrast. The
connector signals the relationship; it does not send the English verb to the
end as German does.

## Use this now

- reason: <span lang="en">because</span>
- time: <span lang="en">when</span>, <span lang="en">before</span>, <span lang="en">after</span>
- condition: <span lang="en">if</span>, <span lang="en">unless</span>
- contrast: <span lang="en">although</span>

Choose the connector by meaning before choosing its position in the clause.

## Core rule

	caption="English subordinate clause order"
	rowHeader="Position"
	lang="en"
	columns={['Connector', 'Subject', 'Verb', 'Rest', 'Main clause']}
	rows={[
		{ label: 'Reason', cells: ['because', 'it', 'was', 'raining,', 'we stayed inside.'] },
		{ label: 'Time', cells: ['When', 'the train', 'arrives,', '', 'we will leave.'] },
		{ label: 'Condition', cells: ['if', 'you', 'need', 'help,', 'call me.'] },
	]}
/>

The subordinate clause still follows **subject + verb**. When it comes first,
separate it from the main clause with a comma. When it follows the main clause,
do not normally use a comma: <span lang="en">We stayed inside because it was raining.</span>

## Do not copy German verb-final order

<LanguageComparison title="Reason clause" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Wir bleiben drinnen, weil es regnet.</p>
	<p slot="en">We are staying inside because it is raining.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German puts the finite verb late after <span lang="de">weil</span>. English
keeps it after the subject: <span lang="en">because it is raining</span>, not
*because it raining is*.

## Future meaning after time and condition words

After <span lang="en">when</span>, <span lang="en">if</span>, <span lang="en">before</span>,
and <span lang="en">after</span>, English often uses a present form for future
meaning: <span lang="en">When she arrives, we will start.</span> The future
form belongs in the main clause, not in the time or condition clause.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="When she will arrive, we will start."
	correct="When she arrives, we will start."
	lang="en"
>
	For a future event introduced by a time or condition word, use a present form
	in the subordinate clause and put <span lang="en">will</span> in the main clause.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
After a connector, write a normal English clause: subject first, then verb.
</MemoryTip>
