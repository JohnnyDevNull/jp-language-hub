<KeyTakeaway>
- Use a relative clause to say which person or thing you mean, or to add extra information.
- Use <span lang="en">who</span> for people, <span lang="en">which</span> for things, and <span lang="en">whose</span> for possession; <span lang="en">that</span> works in many defining clauses.
- Use commas for extra information, not for information needed to identify the noun.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

A relative clause follows a noun and gives more information about it. English
uses normal subject--verb order inside the clause, so it is a focused kind of
[subordinate clause](../subordinate-clauses/).

## Use this now

| If the noun is ... | Use ... | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a person | *who* | <span lang="en">the colleague who called</span> |
| a thing | *which* or *that* | <span lang="en">the report that arrived</span> |
| a possessor | *whose* | <span lang="en">the student whose bag is missing</span> |
| a place | *where* | <span lang="en">the café where we met</span> |

## Core rule

	caption="English relative clause positions"

	rows={[
		{ label: 'Relative word is subject', cells: ['the colleague', 'who', '', 'called', 'me'] },
		{ label: 'Relative word is object', cells: ['the colleague', 'who', 'I', 'called', 'yesterday'] },
		{ label: 'Thing', cells: ['the report', 'that', 'we', 'finished', 'today'] },
	]}
/>

When the relative word is the subject, no second subject appears:
<span lang="en">the colleague who called</span>. When it is the object, the
clause needs its own subject: <span lang="en">the colleague who I called</span>.

## Identifying information or extra information

Use a **defining** clause without commas when it identifies the noun:
<span lang="en">The files that are on the desk are urgent.</span>

Use a **non-defining** clause with commas when the listener already knows the
noun and you add extra information: <span lang="en">My laptop, which is five years old, still works well.</span>
Do not use <span lang="en">that</span> in this comma pair; use
<span lang="en">who</span> or <span lang="en">which</span> instead.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="A person as the relative-clause subject" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Die Kollegin, die mich angerufen hat, arbeitet heute nicht.</p>
	<p slot="en">The colleague who called me is not working today.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German relative pronouns change with gender and case. English usually chooses
the relative word by meaning and clause role, not by the gender of the noun.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="My laptop, that is five years old, still works well."
	correct="My laptop, which is five years old, still works well."
	lang="en"
>
	Use <span lang="en">which</span>, not <span lang="en">that</span>, for a
	non-defining relative clause between commas.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
No commas means “which one?” Commas mean “by the way.”
</MemoryTip>
