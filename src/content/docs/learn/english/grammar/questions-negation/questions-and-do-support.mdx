<KeyTakeaway>
- Most English questions move an auxiliary before the subject: *Are you ready?*,
  *Can she come?*, *Have they arrived?*
- Present and past simple questions usually add *do*: *Do you work here?*,
  *Did he call?*
- When the question word is the subject, keep statement order and do not add
  *do*: *Who lives here?*
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

German can ask by moving the main verb to the front. English is stricter: most
questions need a helper verb in front of the subject.

## Use this now

Ask one question before thinking about the full rule:

| If the statement or question has... | Make the question with... | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *be* | *be* before the subject | *Are you tired?* |
| a modal verb | modal before the subject | *Can she drive?* |
| another auxiliary | auxiliary before the subject | *Have they left?* |
| present simple, no auxiliary | *do / does* before the subject | *Do you like it?* |
| past simple, no auxiliary | *did* before the subject | *Did he call?* |
| the question word is the subject | no do-support | *Who lives here?* |

The auxiliary carries tense and agreement. The main verb stays in its base
form after *do / does / did*: *Does she work here?*, not *Does she works here?*

## When do-support appears

Use do-support when the statement is present simple or past simple and has no
other auxiliary:

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| *You work here.* | *Do you work here?* |
| *She likes tea.* | *Does she like tea?* |
| *He called yesterday.* | *Did he call yesterday?* |
| *They know the answer.* | *Do they know the answer?* |

The form of *do* changes, so the main verb does not:

- *does + like*, not *does + likes*
- *did + call*, not *did + called*

See [simple present](../../verbs-time-forms/simple-present/) and
[simple past](../../verbs-time-forms/simple-past/) for the time forms behind this pattern.

## No do-support with be, modals, or auxiliaries

If there is already a helper verb, move that helper. Do not add *do*.

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| *She is ready.* | *Is she ready?* |
| *You can help.* | *Can you help?* |
| *They have finished.* | *Have they finished?* |
| *He will call.* | *Will he call?* |

The same applies to negative questions: *Isn't she ready?*, *Can't you help?*,
*Haven't they finished?*

This page owns **question formation**: which helper moves and when *do* is
inserted. [Auxiliary verbs](../../verb-patterns-voice/auxiliary-verbs/) explains
the wider system behind tense, aspect, voice, negation, and inversion; it does
not change the question-building decision here.

## Subject questions

When the question word itself is the subject, keep normal statement order after
it. Do not add *do* just because the sentence is a question.

| Meaning | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Someone lives here. Who? | *Who lives here?* |
| Something happened. What? | *What happened?* |
| Someone called you. Who? | *Who called you?* |

Use do-support when the question word asks about the object or another part of
the sentence: *Who did you call?*

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Yes/no question" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Wohnst du hier?</p>
	<p slot="en">Do you live here?</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Question word" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Wo wohnst du?</p>

</LanguageComparison>

German moves the main verb. English moves an auxiliary; if there is no
auxiliary, English creates one with *do*.

<MistakeBox incorrect="Where live you?" correct="Where do you live?" lang="en">
	This is German verb-fronting copied into English. Keep the English subject
	after the auxiliary, not after the main verb.
</MistakeBox>

<MistakeBox incorrect="Does she likes coffee?" correct="Does she like coffee?" lang="en">
	*Does* already marks third person singular, so the main verb stays in the
	base form.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Do not move the main verb in English unless the main verb is *be*. Move the
helper; create *do* only when there is no helper.
</MemoryTip>
