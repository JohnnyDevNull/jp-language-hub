<KeyTakeaway>
- Put *not* after the first auxiliary: *She is not ready*; *They have not left*.
- If a present or past simple clause has no auxiliary, add *do / does / did*:
  *I do not know*; *He did not call*.
- After *do / does / did*, use the base verb: *does not work*, not *does not
  works*.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

To say that an ordinary clause is not true, first find its helper verb. English
places *not* after that helper; simple present and past need *do*-support.

## Use this now

| Clause has… | Make it negative with… | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *be* | *be + not* | *She is not ready.* |
| a modal | modal + *not* | *We cannot stay.* |
| another auxiliary | auxiliary + *not* | *They have not arrived.* |
| simple present, no auxiliary | *do / does not* + base verb | *He does not work here.* |
| simple past, no auxiliary | *did not* + base verb | *She did not call.* |

The first auxiliary carries the negative marker. In a simple clause without an
auxiliary, *do* becomes that first helper. Contractions are normal in speech
and informal writing: *isn't, don't, doesn't, didn't, can't,* and *haven't*.

### Keep the main verb in the base form

*Does* and *did* already carry agreement or past time. The main verb therefore
does not repeat it:

- *She does not **like** coffee.* — not *does not likes*
- *They did not **go** home.* — not *did not went*

This page gives the clause-negation pattern. [Any vs no](../any-vs-no/) owns
the next choice: whether to say *not … any* or put *no* in the noun phrase.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Simple present negation" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Er arbeitet heute nicht.</p>
	<p slot="en">He does not work today.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German can negate the lexical verb directly. In English simple present and past,
use *do* before *not* unless *be* or another auxiliary is already available.

<MistakeBox incorrect="She not likes coffee." correct="She does not like coffee." lang="en">
In an ordinary simple-present clause, *not* needs *do / does*. Do not put it
directly before the main verb.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Find the first helper. Put **not** after it; if there is no helper in simple
present or past, make one with **do**.
</MemoryTip>
