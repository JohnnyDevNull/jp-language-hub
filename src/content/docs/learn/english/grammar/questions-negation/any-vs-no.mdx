<KeyTakeaway>
- Use *not … any* when *not* negates the clause: *I do not have any time.*
- Use *no* when the noun phrase carries the negative meaning: *I have no time.*
- Standard English normally uses one negative carrier, not *I do not have no
  time* for the same meaning.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Both patterns can describe absence. The useful choice is not about how much of
a noun you can count; it is about where the negative meaning sits in the
clause.

## Use this now

| Put the negative meaning in… | Use… | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the clause | *not … any* | *We do not have any tickets.* |
| the noun phrase | *no* | *We have no tickets.* |
| a question | usually *any* | *Do you have any tickets?* |
| a positive offer or request | often *some* | *Would you like some tea?* |

## Core rule

*Not … any* leaves the noun phrase indefinite while the clause is negative:

- *She does not have any money.*
- *There are not any seats left.*

*No* makes the noun phrase itself negative:

- *She has no money.*
- *There are no seats left.*

The two forms often report the same absence. *No* is compact and can sound more
firm; *not … any* keeps the ordinary clause-negation frame visible.

### Keep one negative carrier

Do not combine the two patterns when you mean one ordinary negative statement:

- *I do not have any questions.*
- *I have no questions.*
- not *I do not have no questions.*

For forms such as *someone, anything,* and *nobody*, use the separate
Indefinite pronouns lookup rather than treating this page as a pronoun list.
For *many, much, few,* and *little*, use Countable and uncountable nouns.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="No time" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Ich habe keine Zeit.</p>

German *keine* often maps to either English pattern. English does not need a
second negative when *no* already supplies the negative meaning.

<MistakeBox incorrect="I do not have no money." correct="I do not have any money. / I have no money." lang="en">
For standard English with one negative meaning, choose either clause negation
with *any* or the negative noun phrase with *no*.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Choose the home of the negative meaning: **not** in the clause, or **no** in
the noun phrase. Then use the other part as a normal indefinite expression.
</MemoryTip>
