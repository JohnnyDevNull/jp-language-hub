<KeyTakeaway>
- Questions move an existing auxiliary before the subject, or add *do* when a
  simple present or past clause has no auxiliary.
- Negation tells you how to make an ordinary clause negative; *Any vs no*
  tells you whether the negative meaning sits in *not* or in the noun phrase.
- Keep one negative meaning per standard clause: *I do not know anything* or
  *I know nothing*, not a double negative.
</KeyTakeaway>

This shelf separates three decisions that can look alike in German: asking,
negating a clause, and choosing the polarity of a noun phrase.

## Choose by

| If you need to… | Start with… |
| --- | --- |
| ask a yes/no or question-word question | [Questions and do-support](./questions-and-do-support/) |
| say that an ordinary clause is not true | [Negation](./negation/) |
| choose between *not … any* and *no* | [Any vs no](./any-vs-no/) |
| choose the ordinary statement frame first | [Sentence order](../sentence-structure/sentence-order/) |

## Learn in this order

1. [Questions and do-support](./questions-and-do-support/) gives you the
   reusable question frame and shows when *do / does / did* is needed.
2. [Negation](./negation/) lets you build ordinary negative clauses with
   *not* and the right helper.
3. [Any vs no](./any-vs-no/) adds the polarity choice between *not … any* and
   *no* without turning it into a countability lesson.

## Watch for

- Questions and do-support owns the question-building choice. A later
  Auxiliary verbs page will describe the full auxiliary system, but it is not
  required to form a question now.
- Negation owns ordinary clause negation. Any vs no owns the polarity choice
  inside that negative meaning.
- Countable and uncountable nouns owns quantity and countability; indefinite
  pronouns owns forms such as *someone, anything,* and *nobody*.
