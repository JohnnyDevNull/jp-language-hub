The simple present is the English form for what is generally true, repeated, or
scheduled. It is not the normal form for an action in progress right now.

## Use this now

| Use | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Habit or routine | <span lang="en">I start work at nine.</span> |
| General fact | <span lang="en">Water boils at 100 degrees Celsius.</span> |
| State | <span lang="en">She knows the answer.</span> |
| Schedule | <span lang="en">The train leaves at 8.</span> |
| Instruction | <span lang="en">You click Save.</span> |

## Core rule

The simple present uses the base verb. After
<span lang="en">he</span>, <span lang="en">she</span>, or
<span lang="en">it</span>, add <span lang="en">-s</span> or
<span lang="en">-es</span>.

| Subject | Positive | Negative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I / you / we / they | <span lang="en">I work.</span> | <span lang="en">I do not work.</span> |
| he / she / it | <span lang="en">She works.</span> | <span lang="en">She does not work.</span> |

In questions and negatives, the <span lang="en">-s</span> sits on
<span lang="en">does</span>, not on the main verb:

- <span lang="en">Does she work here?</span>
- <span lang="en">She does not work here.</span>

## Main uses

### Routines and habits

Use the simple present when something happens regularly:

- <span lang="en">We meet every Monday.</span>
- <span lang="en">He usually drinks coffee in the morning.</span>

### Facts and stable situations

Use it for things that feel generally true or stable:

- <span lang="en">The office closes at six.</span>
- <span lang="en">I live near the station.</span>

### Stative verbs

Some verbs usually describe a state, not an action in progress. They often stay
in the simple present:

- <span lang="en">I understand.</span>
- <span lang="en">She owns two bikes.</span>
- <span lang="en">This soup tastes good.</span>

### Schedules and instructions

The simple present can also point to the future when the sentence is about a
public schedule, a programme, or step-by-step instructions:

- <span lang="en">The course starts next week.</span>
- <span lang="en">First you open the file, then you choose Export.</span>

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Habit">
	<p slot="de">Ich arbeite freitags von zu Hause.</p>
	<p slot="en">I work from home on Fridays.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag arbetar hemifrån på fredagar.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

<LanguageComparison title="Action right now">
	<p slot="de">Ich arbeite gerade von zu Hause.</p>
	<p slot="en">I am working from home right now.</p>
	<p slot="sv">Jag arbetar hemifrån just nu.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German and Swedish can use the present tense for both meanings. English
normally separates the habit from the action happening now.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="She work here."
	correct="She works here."
	lang="en"
>
	After <span lang="en">he</span>, <span lang="en">she</span>, and
	<span lang="en">it</span>, the positive simple present needs
	<span lang="en">-s</span> or <span lang="en">-es</span>.
</MistakeBox>

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="I work on the report right now."
	correct="I am working on the report right now."
	lang="en"
>
	Use the present progressive for an action in progress now. Save the simple
	present for routines, facts, states, and schedules.
</MistakeBox>
