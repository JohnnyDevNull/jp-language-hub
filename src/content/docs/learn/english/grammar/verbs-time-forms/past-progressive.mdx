<KeyTakeaway>
- Form the past progressive with *was/were + -ing*: *I was cooking; they were waiting.*
- Use it for an action already in progress at a past moment or around another past event.
- Use the simple past for the completed event that interrupts or follows that background action.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

The past progressive shows a past scene from inside: it was ongoing rather than presented as one finished event.

## Use this now

| Meaning | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| action in progress at a time | *was/were + -ing* | *At eight, I was working.* |
| background + completed event | progressive + simple past | *I was working when she called.* |
| two simultaneous actions | progressive + progressive | *They were talking while I was driving.* |

## Core rule

Use *was* with *I/he/she/it* and *were* with *you/we/they*, followed by the **-ing** form. Negate with *wasn't/weren't*; ask by moving *was/were*: *Were you sleeping?*

The progressive action is often the background. The simple past names the completed event: *We were eating when the lights went out.* This page does not replace [Simple past](../simple-past/), which owns finished past events and their time signals.

<MistakeBox incorrect="I was work when she called." correct="I was working when she called." lang="en">
After *was/were*, use the **-ing** form, not the base verb.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>Use the past progressive to set the scene; use the simple past for what happened in that scene.</MemoryTip>
