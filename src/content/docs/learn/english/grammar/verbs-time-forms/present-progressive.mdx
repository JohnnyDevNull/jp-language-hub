<KeyTakeaway>
- The present progressive is <span lang="en">am/is/are</span> +
  <span lang="en">-ing</span>: <span lang="en">I am working</span>.
- Use it for actions happening now, temporary situations, changes in progress,
  and future arrangements.
- Do not use it just because a sentence is in the present; routines and facts
  usually take the simple present.
</KeyTakeaway>

The present progressive shows an action or situation as active, temporary, or
arranged. It often answers "What is happening now or around now?"

## Use this now

| Use | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Happening now | <span lang="en">I am writing an email.</span> |
| Temporary situation | <span lang="en">She is staying with friends this week.</span> |
| Change in progress | <span lang="en">The days are getting longer.</span> |
| Future arrangement | <span lang="en">We are meeting Anna tomorrow.</span> |

## Core rule

Build the present progressive with <span lang="en">am</span>,
<span lang="en">is</span>, or <span lang="en">are</span> plus the
<span lang="en">-ing</span> form.

| Subject | Positive | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | <span lang="en">I am working.</span> | <span lang="en">I am not working.</span> | <span lang="en">Am I working?</span> |
| he / she / it | <span lang="en">She is working.</span> | <span lang="en">She is not working.</span> | <span lang="en">Is she working?</span> |
| you / we / they | <span lang="en">They are working.</span> | <span lang="en">They are not working.</span> | <span lang="en">Are they working?</span> |

The auxiliary carries the question and negative pattern. Do not add
<span lang="en">do</span>:

- <span lang="en">Are you coming?</span>
- <span lang="en">I am not coming.</span>

## Main uses

### Actions happening now

Use the present progressive when the action is in progress at the moment of
speaking:

- <span lang="en">Please wait. I am checking the file.</span>
- <span lang="en">They are talking to the client.</span>

### Temporary situations

Use it when the situation is true for a limited time, even if it is not
happening at this exact second:

- <span lang="en">I am working from home this month.</span>
- <span lang="en">She is learning English for her new job.</span>

### Change in progress

Use it when something is developing:

- <span lang="en">The team is getting faster.</span>
- <span lang="en">Prices are rising.</span>

### Future arrangements

Use the present progressive for arranged future events, especially when another
person, booking, meeting, or plan is involved:

- <span lang="en">I am meeting Paul at six.</span>
- <span lang="en">We are flying to London on Friday.</span>

German and Swedish can often use the ordinary present where English chooses a
progressive form. The English form makes the action feel active, limited, or
in progress.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="I write an email right now."
	correct="I am writing an email right now."
	lang="en"
>
	<span lang="en">Right now</span> normally points to the present progressive,
	not the simple present.
</MistakeBox>

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="I am knowing the answer."
	correct="I know the answer."
	lang="en"
>
	Many state verbs stay in the simple present. Use
	<span lang="en">know</span>, <span lang="en">believe</span>,
	<span lang="en">understand</span>, <span lang="en">own</span>, and
	<span lang="en">need</span> carefully.
</MistakeBox>
