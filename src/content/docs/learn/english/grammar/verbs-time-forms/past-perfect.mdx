<KeyTakeaway>
- Form the past perfect with *had + past participle*: *She had left; we had seen it.*
- Use it when you need to show that one past event happened before another past reference point.
- Do not add it automatically to every sequence of past events; clear order often only needs the simple past.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

The past perfect looks back from a past moment to something already completed before it.

## Use this now

| Situation | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| earlier event matters at a later past point | *had + participle* | *The film had started when we arrived.* |
| result already true in the past | past perfect | *She was tired because she had worked late.* |
| sequence is already clear | often simple past | *I opened the door and sat down.* |

## Core rule

Use **had** with every subject, then the past participle: *had gone, had finished, had written*. In negatives, use *had not/hadn't*; in questions, move *had*: *Had they booked the table?*

The later past point may be stated (*when we arrived*) or understood from context. The past perfect makes the earlier relationship explicit; it is not simply a “more past” alternative to the simple past.

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Earlier than a past arrival" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Als wir ankamen, hatte der Film schon begonnen.</p>

German and English can both mark this earlier-past relationship. In English, choose the past perfect when the relationship needs to be clear to the reader or listener.

<MistakeBox incorrect="When we arrived, the film had start." correct="When we arrived, the film had started." lang="en">
*Had* needs a past participle, not the base form.
</MistakeBox>
