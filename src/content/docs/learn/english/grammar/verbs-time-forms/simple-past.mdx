<KeyTakeaway>
- Use the simple past when the action, event, or state belongs to a finished past time.
- Finished-time signals like *yesterday*, *last week*, *in 2019*, and *when I was a child* usually point to the simple past.
- Questions and negatives use *did* plus the base verb: *Did you call?* / *I did not call.*
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

The simple past is the normal English form for a finished story point. German
often uses the spoken <span lang="de">Perfekt</span> for the same meaning, so
German speakers must watch the time frame, not the German verb form.

## Use this now

Choose the simple past when you can answer **when?** with a finished time:

| Finished time cue | Simple past example |
| --- | --- |
| yesterday | I called her yesterday. |
| last week / last year | We met last year. |
| in 2019 / on Monday | They moved in 2019. |
| when + past situation | I lived there when I was a student. |

If the time is still connected to now, you may need another form. If the time is
finished, simple past is the default.

## Core rule

The simple past places an action, event, or state completely in the past.

| Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- |
| completed action | She finished the report yesterday. |
| completed event | The meeting started at 9. |
| past state | I knew the answer then. |
| repeated past habit | We visited my grandparents every summer. |

## Form

Regular verbs add **-ed**:

| Base verb | Simple past |
| --- | --- |
| work | worked |
| call | called |
| study | studied |
| stop | stopped |

Many frequent verbs are irregular, so learn them as useful verbs, not as one
giant list: *go → went*, *see → saw*, *make → made*, *have → had*, *be →
was/were*.

## Questions and negatives with did

In questions and negatives, **did** carries the past. The main verb goes back to
the base form.

| Sentence type | Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| question | Did + subject + base verb? | Did you see it? |
| negative | subject + did not + base verb | I did not see it. |
| short answer | Yes/No + subject + did/did not | Yes, I did. / No, I did not. |

Do not put past marking on both verbs: write *Did you see it?*, not *Did you
saw it?*

## Time expressions

These expressions usually make the time frame finished:

- *yesterday*, *the day before yesterday*
- *last night*, *last week*, *last summer*
- *in 2019*, *in March*, *on Monday*
- *ago*: *two years ago*
- *when I was a child*, *when we arrived*

With **when**, English normally asks for or gives a finished past point: *When
did you start?* / *I started in 2020.*

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="I have visited London last year."
	correct="I visited London last year."
	lang="en"
>
	German can say <span lang="de">Ich habe London letztes Jahr besucht</span>.
	English cannot use the present perfect with a finished-time expression like
	*last year*. Once the time is over, use the simple past.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
	If the sentence says **when in the finished past**, choose the simple past.
	Use **did + base verb** for questions and negatives.
</MemoryTip>
