The present perfect progressive connects ongoing or recent activity with the present moment.

## Use this now

| Meaning | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| continuing activity | *have/has been + -ing* | *We have been living here for two years.* |
| recent activity with a result now | *have/has been + -ing* | *You are wet. Have you been walking in the rain?* |
| completed result or experience | often present perfect | *I have written the report.* |

## Core rule

Use *have been* with *I/you/we/they* and *has been* with *he/she/it*, then the **-ing** form. *For* gives a duration; *since* gives the starting point: *I have been working for three hours / since nine.*

Many activity verbs fit naturally: *work, wait, study, rain, live*. Some state verbs usually do not: say *I have known her for years*, not normally *I have been knowing her*. For the completed-result and experience uses, see [Present perfect](../present-perfect/); [Choosing time form](../choosing-time-form/) remains the wider decision hub.

<MistakeBox incorrect="She has been study all morning." correct="She has been studying all morning." lang="en">
After *have/has been*, use the **-ing** form.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>Think “activity in view”: it started before now, and its duration or evidence still matters now.</MemoryTip>
