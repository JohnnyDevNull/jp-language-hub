<KeyTakeaway>
- Compare two things with a comparative and *than*: *lighter than*.
- Choose one from a group with *the* plus a superlative: *the lightest*.
- Short adjectives often take *-er/-est*; longer ones usually take *more/most*.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

## Use this now
| Need | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| two things | comparative + *than* | *This bag is lighter than mine.* |
| one in a group | *the* + superlative | *It is the lightest bag.* |
| equal | *as + adjective + as* | *This route is as fast as the train.* |
## Core rule
| Type | Comparative | Superlative |
| --- | --- | --- |
| short | *small → smaller* | *smallest* |
| vowel + consonant | *big → bigger* | *biggest* |
| ends in -y | *happy → happier* | *happiest* |
| longer | *more useful* | *most useful* |
Use *than* after a comparative and normally *the* before a superlative. Not *more easier* or *the most fastest*.
### Useful irregulars
Learn *good → better → best*, *bad → worse → worst*, and *far → farther/further → farthest/furthest* as whole sets. *Further* can also mean “additional.”
