<KeyTakeaway>
- Use zero conditional for general facts, first for a realistic future possibility, second for an unreal present or future, and third for an unreal past.
- In an *if* clause for the first conditional, use present simple, not *will*.
- Mixed conditionals are useful when a past cause has a present result; learn the main four patterns first.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Conditionals connect a condition with its result. The form signals whether you treat that condition as general, possible, imagined now, or impossible to change.

## Use this now

| Meaning | If clause | Result | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| general fact | present | present | *If you heat ice, it melts.* |
| real possibility | present | *will + base* | *If it rains, we'll stay in.* |
| unreal now/future | past | *would + base* | *If I had time, I would travel.* |
| unreal past | past perfect | *would have + participle* | *If I had known, I would have called.* |

## Core rule

The labels help, but the meaning comes first. The second conditional's past form marks distance from reality, not past time. The third conditional looks back at a past condition that cannot now be changed.

### One valuable mixed pattern

Use *if + past perfect* with *would + base* when an unreal past cause explains a present result: *If I had taken the job, I would live in Berlin now.* Do not add mixed patterns until the four main frames are comfortable.

Use present simple in the ordinary first-conditional *if* clause.
</MistakeBox>
