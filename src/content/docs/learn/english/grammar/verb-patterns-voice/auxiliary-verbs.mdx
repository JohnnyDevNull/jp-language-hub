<KeyTakeaway>
- <span lang="en">be</span>, <span lang="en">have</span>, and <span lang="en">do</span> help another verb carry grammar.
- The first auxiliary carries negation and inversion; the main verb supplies the central meaning.
- Modals such as <span lang="en">can</span> and <span lang="en">must</span> are auxiliaries with their own meanings.
</KeyTakeaway>
<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Auxiliaries are the system behind English tense, aspect, voice, negation, and
inversion. This page explains their jobs; [questions and do-support](../../questions-negation/questions-and-do-support/) owns question formation.

## Use this now
Use <span lang="en">be + -ing</span> for progressive forms, <span lang="en">have + participle</span> for perfect forms, and <span lang="en">do + base verb</span> when simple present or past needs a question or negative.

## Core rule
<SentenceSchema caption="Auxiliaries in an English verb phrase" rowHeader="Function" lang="en" columns={['Subject', 'Auxiliary', 'Main verb', 'Rest']} rows={[{ label: 'Progressive', cells: ['She', 'is', 'working', 'today.'] }, { label: 'Perfect', cells: ['They', 'have', 'finished', 'already.'] }, { label: 'Do-support', cells: ['I', 'do not', 'know', 'yet.'] }]} />

The first auxiliary is the one that moves or takes <span lang="en">not</span>: <span lang="en">Has she arrived?</span>, <span lang="en">She has not arrived.</span>

<MistakeBox incorrect="She not works today." correct="She does not work today." lang="en">With a simple present main verb, use <span lang="en">do/does</span> for ordinary negation.</MistakeBox>
