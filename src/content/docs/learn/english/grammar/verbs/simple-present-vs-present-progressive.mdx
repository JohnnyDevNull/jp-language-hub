This is a decision page. Use it when you already know the two forms but need to
choose the one that sounds natural in context.

## Use this now

| Ask yourself | Choose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is it generally true or repeated? | simple present | <span lang="en">I work from home on Fridays.</span> |
| Is it happening now? | present progressive | <span lang="en">I am working from home right now.</span> |
| Is it temporary? | present progressive | <span lang="en">I am working from home this month.</span> |
| Is it a state such as knowing, owning, needing? | simple present | <span lang="en">I know the answer.</span> |
| Is it an arranged future event? | present progressive | <span lang="en">I am meeting her tomorrow.</span> |
| Is it a timetable or schedule? | simple present | <span lang="en">The train leaves at 8.</span> |

## Core rule

The simple present presents something as a fact, habit, state, or scheduled
item. The present progressive presents something as active, temporary, changing,
or arranged.

Compare:

- <span lang="en">She lives in Berlin.</span> — this is her stable home.
- <span lang="en">She is living in Berlin.</span> — this sounds temporary or
  current-for-now.

The progressive does not just mean "present." It changes how the listener sees
the situation.

German and Swedish often let the time expression do the work. English often
marks the difference in the verb form itself.

## Common decision pairs

### always vs right now

- <span lang="en">I usually take the bus.</span>
- <span lang="en">I am taking a taxi today.</span>

The first sentence is a habit. The second sentence is a temporary departure
from the habit.

### state vs action

- <span lang="en">This soup tastes good.</span>
- <span lang="en">I am tasting the soup.</span>

The first sentence describes a state or impression. The second sentence
describes an active test.

### schedule vs arrangement

- <span lang="en">The course starts on Monday.</span>
- <span lang="en">I am starting a new course on Monday.</span>

The first sentence reads like a timetable. The second sentence reads like a
personal arrangement.

## Better default

For a personal future arrangement, the present progressive is usually the better
default:

- <span lang="en">I am going to the doctor tomorrow.</span>

<span lang="en">I go to the doctor tomorrow</span> can be possible when the
speaker treats the appointment like a fixed schedule, but it is not the safest
learner default. Use the simple present for timetables and schedules:
<span lang="en">The appointment starts at 9.</span>
