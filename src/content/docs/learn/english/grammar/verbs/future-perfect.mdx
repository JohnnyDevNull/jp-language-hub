<KeyTakeaway>
- Use the future perfect for something completed before a future point.
- The form is <span lang="en">will have</span> + past participle:
  <span lang="en">I will have finished by Friday.</span>
- It looks forward from now, then back from that later point.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

The future perfect is for deadlines, milestones and "already completed by
then" meanings. It is less frequent than <span lang="en">will</span> or
<span lang="en">going to</span>, but very clear when a future completion point
matters.

## Use this now

Choose the future perfect when the sentence contains or implies a future limit:

| Future limit | Completed before it |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="en">by Friday</span> | <span lang="en">I will have finished the report.</span> |
| <span lang="en">by the time you arrive</span> | <span lang="en">They will have left.</span> |
| <span lang="en">in two years</span> | <span lang="en">She will have completed her degree.</span> |

The word <span lang="en">by</span> is a common clue because it marks the latest
point of completion.

## Core rule

**Pattern:** subject + <span lang="en">will have</span> + past participle

| Sentence type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| positive | <span lang="en">I will have finished.</span> |
| negative | <span lang="en">I will not have finished.</span> |
| contraction | <span lang="en">I won't have finished.</span> |
| question | <span lang="en">Will you have finished?</span> |

The past participle is the third verb form:
<span lang="en">finished</span>, <span lang="en">done</span>,
<span lang="en">seen</span>, <span lang="en">written</span>.

## Future perfect or future progressive?

Use the future perfect for completion before a future point:
<span lang="en">At eight, I will have finished dinner.</span>

Use the [future progressive](../future-progressive/) for an action in progress
at a future point:
<span lang="en">At eight, I will be having dinner.</span>

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="In progress then" languages={['de', 'en']}>

German often makes the completion point visible with
<span lang="de">bis</span>. In English, <span lang="en">by</span> is the
important signal: <span lang="en">by Friday</span> means completed no later than
Friday.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="I will have finish the report by Friday."

	lang="en"
>
	<span lang="en">I will finish</span> is often possible, but
	<span lang="en">I will have finished</span> makes the "completed before that
	future point" meaning explicit. When you use the future perfect, the verb
	after <span lang="en">have</span> must be the past participle.
</MistakeBox>
