<KeyTakeaway>
- Choose a pronoun family for the meaning first, then choose its form for the job it does.
- English does not have German-style case endings on nouns, but personal pronouns still distinguish forms such as *I/me* and *she/her*.
- Possessive, reflexive, demonstrative, and indefinite forms solve different noun-phrase jobs; they are not interchangeable labels.
</KeyTakeaway>

Start with the noun phrase you need, then check the sentence role only where English changes the pronoun form.

## Choose by

| If you need… | Start with… |
| --- | --- |
| a person or thing already known in the conversation | [Personal pronouns](./personal-pronouns/) |
| an owner or something belonging to an owner | [Possessive pronouns](./possessive-pronouns/) |
| the subject and object to be the same person or thing | [Reflexive pronouns](./reflexive-pronouns/) |
| this/that/these/those | [Demonstrative pronouns](./demonstrative-pronouns/) |
| an unspecified person or thing | [Indefinite pronouns](./indefinite-pronouns/) |
| the choice between *I/me*, *she/her*, or *they/them* | [Sentence roles and pronoun case](./sentence-roles-and-pronoun-case/) |

## Learn in this order

1. [Personal pronouns](./personal-pronouns/) gives the core forms you meet in every conversation.
2. [Possessive pronouns](./possessive-pronouns/) separates a possessor before a noun from an independent possessive form.
3. [Sentence roles and pronoun case](./sentence-roles-and-pronoun-case/) connects subject and object jobs to the remaining English form contrast.
4. Add reflexive, demonstrative, and indefinite pronouns as separate lookup tools.
