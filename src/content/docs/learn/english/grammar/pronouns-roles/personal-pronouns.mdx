<KeyTakeaway>
- Use *I, you, he, she, it, we,* and *they* to replace a known noun phrase.
- Use singular *they* for a person whose gender is unknown, irrelevant, or who
  uses *they*.
- The subject/object choice belongs to [Sentence roles and pronoun case](../sentence-roles-and-pronoun-case/).
</KeyTakeaway>
<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

Personal pronouns keep a conversation from repeating names and nouns. English _you_ works for one person and for several people; it is normally neutral in formality.

## Use this now

| Meaning               | Subject form           | Example            |
| --------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------ |
| speaker               | _I_                    | _I am ready._      |
| listener, one or more | _you_                  | _You are welcome._ |
| known person or thing | _he / she / it / they_ | _They are late._   |

## Core rule

Use a pronoun when the listener can identify its referent from the conversation: _Maria is here. She is ready._ Avoid _it_ for a person. Use _they_ when a person has not been identified by gender: _Someone left their bag; they may return._ Singular _they_ also respects a person who uses those pronouns.

<MemoryTip>
  Use the form a person asks you to use. In ordinary formal and informal
  English, singular *they* is established and useful.
</MemoryTip>
