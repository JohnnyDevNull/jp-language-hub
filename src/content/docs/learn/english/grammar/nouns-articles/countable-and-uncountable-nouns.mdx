<KeyTakeaway>
- Countable nouns have singular and plural forms: *one problem*, *two
  problems*.
- Uncountable nouns do not normally take **a/an** or a plural: *information*,
  not *an information* or *informations*.
- Article choice depends on countability: one countable thing can take
  **a/an**, but one unit of an uncountable noun needs a phrase such as *a piece
  of advice*.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

German and English do not always count the same ideas. Before choosing
**a/an**, **the**, plural **-s**, **much**, or **many**, decide whether English
treats the noun as countable or uncountable.

## Use this now

Ask whether the noun names separate items you can count directly.

| Test | If yes | If no |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Can you say <span lang="en">one/two</span> before it? | countable: <span lang="en">one problem, two problems</span> | uncountable: <span lang="en">some information</span> |
| Can it take plural <span lang="en">-s</span>? | countable: <span lang="en">three chairs</span> | uncountable: <span lang="en">furniture</span> |
| Do you need one unit? | <span lang="en">a problem</span> | <span lang="en">a piece of advice</span> |
| Do you ask quantity? | <span lang="en">many questions</span> | <span lang="en">much information</span> |

Once you know the noun type, choose the article on
[Articles](../articles/).

## Core rule

A **countable noun** names separate things: a person, an object, a task, an
idea that English treats as one item.

- <span lang="en">one question</span>, <span lang="en">two questions</span>
- <span lang="en">a chair</span>, <span lang="en">three chairs</span>
- <span lang="en">an idea</span>, <span lang="en">many ideas</span>

An **uncountable noun** names a mass, field, activity, quality, or abstract
substance. It does not normally use **a/an** or a plural form.

- <span lang="en">information</span>
- <span lang="en">advice</span>
- <span lang="en">furniture</span>
- <span lang="en">equipment</span>
- <span lang="en">homework</span>
- <span lang="en">research</span>
- <span lang="en">knowledge</span>
- <span lang="en">traffic</span>

## Common uncountable traps

| German-looking idea | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="de">Informationen</span> | <span lang="en">information</span>, <span lang="en">pieces of information</span> |
| <span lang="de">Ratschläge</span> | <span lang="en">advice</span>, <span lang="en">pieces of advice</span> |
| <span lang="de">Möbel</span> | <span lang="en">furniture</span>, <span lang="en">items of furniture</span> |
| <span lang="de">Ausrüstung</span> | <span lang="en">equipment</span>, <span lang="en">pieces of equipment</span> |
| <span lang="de">Hausaufgaben</span> | <span lang="en">homework</span>, <span lang="en">homework tasks</span> |

Use **much** with uncountable nouns and **many** with plural count nouns:
<span lang="en">much information</span>, <span lang="en">many questions</span>.
In positive everyday sentences, <span lang="en">a lot of</span> works with
both: <span lang="en">a lot of information</span>,
<span lang="en">a lot of questions</span>.

## Meaning can change countability

Some nouns can be countable or uncountable when the meaning changes.

| Uncountable meaning | Countable meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="en">I drink coffee.</span> | <span lang="en">Two coffees, please.</span> |
| <span lang="en">We had chicken for dinner.</span> | <span lang="en">There is a chicken in the garden.</span> |
| <span lang="en">The table is covered with paper.</span> | <span lang="en">She published a paper.</span> |

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="German plural, English uncountable" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Ich brauche mehr Informationen.</p>
	<p slot="en">I need more information.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German can make <span lang="de">Informationen</span> plural. English normally
treats <span lang="en">information</span> as uncountable, so it stays singular
in form.

<LanguageComparison title="One unit" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Sie gab mir einen Ratschlag.</p>
	<p slot="en">She gave me a piece of advice.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

English <span lang="en">advice</span> is uncountable. To count one item, add a
unit phrase such as <span lang="en">a piece of</span>.

<MistakeBox incorrect="Can you send me two informations?" correct="Can you send me two pieces of information?" lang="en">
	Do not let a German plural decide the English noun type. If English treats
	the noun as uncountable, count it with a unit phrase.
</MistakeBox>

<MistakeBox incorrect="She gave me an advice." correct="She gave me some advice." lang="en">
	Use <span lang="en">some advice</span> for an unspecified amount, or
	<span lang="en">a piece of advice</span> for one item.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
First classify the noun: singular countable, plural countable, or uncountable.
Then choose the article, plural, and quantity word.
</MemoryTip>
