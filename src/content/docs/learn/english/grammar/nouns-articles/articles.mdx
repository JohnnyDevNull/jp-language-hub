<KeyTakeaway>
- Use **a/an** for one non-specific countable thing: *a question*, *an idea*.
- Use **the** when the listener can identify the thing you mean: *the question
  from yesterday*.
- Use no article for many general plural and uncountable meanings: *Dogs need
  exercise*, *Information helps*.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

English articles are small, but they carry two decisions at once: what kind of
noun you have, and whether the listener can identify it. German articles are
not a safe shortcut, especially for generic plurals and uncountable nouns.

## Use this now

Ask these questions in order:

| Question | Choose | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is it one non-specific countable thing? | **a/an** | <span lang="en">I bought a book.</span> |
| Can the listener identify it? | **the** | <span lang="en">The book is on my desk.</span> |
| Is it a general plural? | no article | <span lang="en">Books are useful.</span> |
| Is it a general uncountable noun? | no article | <span lang="en">Information is useful.</span> |
| Is it a specific uncountable meaning? | **the** | <span lang="en">The information in this email is useful.</span> |

If you are unsure whether the noun is countable, start with
[countable and uncountable nouns](../countable-and-uncountable-nouns/).

## Core rule

English singular count nouns normally need a determiner. **A/an**, **the**,
possessives, demonstratives, and quantifiers can all fill that slot.

| Meaning | Natural English |
| --- | --- |
| one thing, not identified yet | <span lang="en">I need a chair.</span> |
| the listener knows which one | <span lang="en">I need the chair by the window.</span> |
| ownership identifies it | <span lang="en">I need my chair.</span> |
| pointing identifies it | <span lang="en">I need that chair.</span> |

Use **a** before a consonant sound and **an** before a vowel sound:
<span lang="en">a university</span>, <span lang="en">an hour</span>,
<span lang="en">an old idea</span>.

## General and specific meaning

English often uses no article for a whole class, especially with plural nouns
and uncountable nouns.

| Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- |
| dogs in general | <span lang="en">Dogs need exercise.</span> |
| specific dogs | <span lang="en">The dogs next door bark at night.</span> |
| coffee in general | <span lang="en">Coffee helps me focus.</span> |
| specific coffee | <span lang="en">The coffee in this cup is cold.</span> |

Do not translate German **der**, **die**, **das** mechanically as **the**.
First decide whether the English meaning is general or specific.

## Countability changes the article

Countability decides whether **a/an** is possible at all.

| Noun type | General | One unit | Specific |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| countable | <span lang="en">Books are useful.</span> | <span lang="en">a book</span> | <span lang="en">the book on my desk</span> |
| uncountable | <span lang="en">Information is useful.</span> | <span lang="en">a piece of information</span> | <span lang="en">the information in the email</span> |

For the noun-type decision, use
[countable and uncountable nouns](../countable-and-uncountable-nouns/).

## Direct comparison

<LanguageComparison title="Generic plural" languages={['de', 'en']}>
	<p slot="de">Die Hunde brauchen Bewegung.</p>
	<p slot="en">Dogs need exercise.</p>
</LanguageComparison>

German often uses a definite article for a generic plural. English normally
uses no article there, because the sentence talks about the class, not about a
specific group of dogs.

<MistakeBox incorrect="I am teacher." correct="I am a teacher." lang="en">
	After <span lang="en">be</span>, an English singular count noun still needs
	an article or another determiner: <span lang="en">a teacher</span>,
	<span lang="en">the teacher</span>, <span lang="en">my teacher</span>.
</MistakeBox>

<MistakeBox incorrect="The dogs need exercise." correct="Dogs need exercise." lang="en">
	Use no article when you mean dogs in general. Use
	<span lang="en">the dogs</span> only when the listener can identify the
	specific dogs.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
First ask: "Can the listener identify which one?" If not, use
<span lang="en">a/an</span> for one countable thing, or no article for a
general plural or uncountable idea.
</MemoryTip>
