<KeyTakeaway>
- Use *in* for an area or enclosed space, *at* for a point/activity place, and *on* for a surface or line.
- Use *to* for a destination, *into/onto* for movement inside or onto something, and *from* for an origin.
- Meaning matters more than a German one-word translation: ask location, destination, movement through a boundary, or source.
</KeyTakeaway>

## Use this now
| Meaning | Choice | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| inside / area | *in* | *in the room, in Berlin* |
| point or activity place | *at* | *at the station, at school* |
| surface / route | *on* | *on the table, on the bus* |
| destination | *to* | *go to London* |
| crossing a boundary | *into / onto* | *walk into the room; jump onto the table* |
| source | *from* | *come from Sweden* |
## Core rule
Location answers “where?”; direction answers “where to?” *She is in the kitchen* but *She walks into the kitchen.* Use *to* with ordinary destinations, but no *to* after *home, here,* or *there*: *go home*, *come here*. Transport is often *by bus/train* for the method but *on the bus/train* for location.
<MistakeBox incorrect="I go to home." correct="I go home." lang="en">*Home* normally takes no *to* after a movement verb.</MistakeBox>
