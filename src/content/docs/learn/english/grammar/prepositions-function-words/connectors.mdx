<KeyTakeaway>
- Choose *and/also*, *but/however*, *because/so*, or *if/unless* by the relationship between ideas.
- *Because, although, if,* and *unless* introduce clauses; *however* and *therefore* connect independent ideas in a more formal style.
- Connector position does not create German-style verb-final order; use the Sentence Structure pages for the clause frame.
</KeyTakeaway>

<TagList levels={frontmatter.levels} tags={frontmatter.tags} />

## Use this now
| Relationship | Everyday choice | More formal choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| addition | *and, also* | *in addition* |
| contrast | *but* | *however, although* |
| cause / result | *because / so* | *therefore, as a result* |
| condition | *if, unless* | *provided that* |
## Core rule
Use *because* for a reason and *so* for its result: *We stayed in because it rained. It rained, so we stayed in.* *Unless* usually means “if not”: *I will go unless it rains.* Use *however* after a full stop or semicolon in careful writing: *It rained. However, we went out.* For the exact clause order and commas, see [Subordinate clauses](../../sentence-structure/subordinate-clauses/) and [Sentence order](../../sentence-structure/sentence-order/).
<MistakeBox incorrect="Because it was late, went we home." correct="Because it was late, we went home." lang="en">A connector does not invert the English subject and verb.</MistakeBox>
