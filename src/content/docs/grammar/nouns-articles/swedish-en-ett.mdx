Most Swedish nouns belong to one of two grammatical genders. An **en-word** uses
<span lang="sv">en</span>; an **ett-word** uses <span lang="sv">ett</span>.
The noun's gender affects articles, definite forms, and adjective endings.

<TagList tags={['Swedish', 'A1', 'grammar', 'nouns', 'important']} />

## Core rule

| Gender | Indefinite singular | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| en-word | <span lang="sv">en bil</span> | a car |
| ett-word | <span lang="sv">ett hus</span> | a house |

There is no completely reliable rule that predicts the gender of every Swedish
noun. Learn the article together with the noun: not merely
<span lang="sv">bil</span>, but <span lang="sv">en bil</span>.

<RuleBox>
	Learn a Swedish noun together with its article. The article is part of the
	word's grammatical information: it records the noun's gender and helps you
	choose compatible forms elsewhere in the noun phrase.
</RuleBox>

## Indefinite and definite

Swedish normally uses a separate article for the indefinite singular but adds a
suffix to the noun for the simple definite form:

| Gender | Indefinite | Definite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| en-word | <span lang="sv">en bil</span> | <span lang="sv">bilen</span> |
| ett-word | <span lang="sv">ett hus</span> | <span lang="sv">huset</span> |

## Adjective agreement

The noun phrase's gender and number determine the basic adjective form. The
article makes that grammatical information visible:

- <span lang="sv">en <strong>stor</strong> bil</span> — a big car
- <span lang="sv">ett <strong>stort</strong> hus</span> — a big house
- <span lang="sv"><strong>stora</strong> bilar</span> — big cars
- <span lang="sv"><strong>stora</strong> hus</span> — big houses

The basic pattern is **base form** with an en-word, often **-t** with an ett-word,
and often **-a** in the plural. Adjectives also have irregular and spelling-
dependent forms; the canonical explanation is [Swedish adjective agreement](../../adjectives-adverbs/swedish-adjective-agreement/).

## Definite adjective constructions

When an adjective modifies a definite noun, standard Swedish normally uses a
free definite article, the definite adjective form, **and** the definite noun:

- <span lang="sv">den stora bilen</span>
- <span lang="sv">det stora huset</span>
- <span lang="sv">de stora bilarna</span>

This is called **double definiteness**. Compare English
<span lang="en">the big car</span>, which marks definiteness only once.

## Plural

The plural ending cannot be derived from en or ett alone:

- <span lang="sv">en bil → bilar</span>
- <span lang="sv">ett hus → hus</span>
- <span lang="sv">ett äpple → äpplen</span>

Learn the important plural forms with the noun. See the
[Swedish plural overview](../swedish-plural-overview/).

:::caution[Common mistakes]
Do not assume that an object's natural properties determine en or ett, and do
not use <span lang="sv">en</span> as a universal article.

<span lang="sv">❌ en hus</span>  
<span lang="sv">✅ ett hus</span>

Also keep the adjective consistent:

<span lang="sv">❌ ett stor hus</span>  
<span lang="sv">✅ ett stort hus</span>
:::

<MemoryTip>
	Treat the article as part of the word: learn
	<span lang="sv"><strong>en bil</strong></span> and
	<span lang="sv"><strong>ett hus</strong></span>. Add the four noun forms when
	you can: <span lang="sv">en bil, bilen, bilar, bilarna</span>.
</MemoryTip>
