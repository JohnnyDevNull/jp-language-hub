Swedish adjectives agree with the gender and number of the noun they describe.
Attributive adjectives inside noun phrases also reflect whether the phrase is
indefinite or definite. The central beginner pattern is **base form**, **-t**,
and **-a**.

**Level:** A1–A2  
**Focus:** Swedish adjectives

## Core pattern

| Noun phrase | Adjective form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| indefinite en singular | base form | <span lang="sv">en <strong>stor</strong> bil</span> |
| indefinite ett singular | often <span lang="sv">-t</span> | <span lang="sv">ett <strong>stort</strong> hus</span> |
| indefinite plural | often <span lang="sv">-a</span> | <span lang="sv"><strong>stora</strong> bilar</span> |
| definite | often <span lang="sv">-a</span> | <span lang="sv">den <strong>stora</strong> bilen</span> |

The same plural adjective form is used with en-words and ett-words:

- <span lang="sv">stora bilar</span>
- <span lang="sv">stora hus</span>

## Indefinite forms

Start from the dictionary or en-form:

<p lang="sv">stor</p>

Add <span lang="sv">-t</span> for the regular ett-form and
<span lang="sv">-a</span> for the regular plural:

<p lang="sv">stor → stort → stora</p>

Another regular example:

<p lang="sv">gul → gult → gula</p>

Spelling can change when endings are added, and some adjectives are irregular.
For example:

- <span lang="sv">glad → glatt → glada</span>
- <span lang="sv">liten → litet → små</span>
- <span lang="sv">bra → bra → bra</span>

Learn frequent exceptions through complete phrases.

## Definite noun phrases

Standard definite noun phrases with an attributive adjective normally use
double definiteness:

- <span lang="sv">den stora bilen</span>
- <span lang="sv">det stora huset</span>
- <span lang="sv">de stora bilarna</span>

The article marks gender or number, the adjective commonly takes
<span lang="sv">-a</span>, and the noun keeps its definite suffix. See
[Swedish noun forms](../../nouns-articles/swedish-noun-forms/).

Possessives are different: the adjective takes the definite form, but the noun
does not take its definite suffix:

<p lang="sv">min stora bil</p>

## After verbs such as *vara* and *bli*

Predicative adjectives agree with the subject but do not use double
definiteness:

- <span lang="sv">Bilen är stor.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Huset är stort.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Bilarna är stora.</span>

## Direct comparison

| Language | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="de">🇩🇪 Deutsch</span> | <span lang="de">ein großes Auto · große Autos · das große Auto</span> |
| <span lang="en">🇬🇧 English</span> | <span lang="en">a big car · big cars · the big car</span> |
| <span lang="sv">🇸🇪 Svenska</span> | <span lang="sv">en stor bil · stora bilar · den stora bilen</span> |

English adjectives do not agree with the noun. German adjectives agree too,
but German endings depend on case and article type as well as gender and number.
Do not transfer the German ending system directly to Swedish.

:::caution[Common mistakes]
Keep article, adjective, and noun compatible:

<span lang="sv">❌ ett stor hus</span>  
<span lang="sv">✅ ett stort hus</span>

In a standard definite phrase, do not omit either part of double definiteness:

<span lang="sv">❌ det stora hus</span>  
<span lang="sv">✅ det stora huset</span>
:::

:::tip[Memory tip]
Remember the three-part rhythm:
<span lang="sv"><strong>en stor — ett stort — flera stora</strong></span>.
For a definite phrase, use the familiar <span lang="sv">-a</span> form:
<span lang="sv">den stora bilen</span>.
:::
