Swedish word order carries grammatical meaning. The most useful starting point is
the neutral main-clause pattern, but that pattern changes when another sentence
element is placed first or when the clause is subordinate.

## Core rule

In a neutral declarative main clause, Swedish usually follows this order:

> **subject + finite verb + sentence adverb + remaining verb parts + object + adverbial**

<p lang="sv">Jag köper inte bilen idag.</p>

- <span lang="sv">Jag</span> — subject
- <span lang="sv">köper</span> — finite verb
- <span lang="sv">inte</span> — sentence adverb
- <span lang="sv">bilen</span> — object
- <span lang="sv">idag</span> — time adverbial

The finite verb carries tense and anchors the clause structure. Swedish verbs
do not change according to person:

- <span lang="sv">Jag arbetar.</span> — I work.
- <span lang="sv">Vi arbetar.</span> — We work.

## Direct comparison

| Language | Neutral statement |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="de">🇩🇪 Deutsch</span> | <span lang="de">Ich kaufe heute ein Auto.</span> |
| <span lang="en">🇬🇧 English</span> | <span lang="en">I am buying a car today.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">🇸🇪 Svenska</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag köper en bil idag.</span> |

The three languages can look similar in a neutral sentence. The important
difference appears when the first constituent changes: Swedish and German use
[V2 word order](../v2/), whereas English normally keeps the subject
before the verb.

## A constituent can contain several words

A sentence position is occupied by a **constituent**, not necessarily one word.

<p lang="sv">På måndag morgon börjar jag mitt nya jobb.</p>

The entire phrase <span lang="sv">på måndag morgon</span> occupies the first
position. The finite verb <span lang="sv">börjar</span> is therefore second and
the subject follows it.

## Questions

A yes/no question normally begins with the finite verb:

<p lang="sv">Köper du bilen idag?</p>

A question-word question normally begins with the question word or phrase,
followed by the finite verb:

<p lang="sv">När köper du bilen?</p>

The question phrase <span lang="sv">när</span> occupies the first position, so
the finite verb remains second.

## Main and subordinate clauses

Compare the placement of <span lang="sv">inte</span>:

| Clause | Swedish example | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main clause | <span lang="sv">Jag kommer inte idag.</span> | subject + finite verb + <span lang="sv">inte</span> |
| Subordinate clause | <span lang="sv">… eftersom jag inte kommer idag.</span> | conjunction + subject + <span lang="sv">inte</span> + finite verb |

The [BIFF rule](../biff/) is a useful way to remember this subordinate-
clause pattern.

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Idag jag köper en bil."
	correct="Idag köper jag en bil."
	lang="sv"
>
Do not copy English subject–verb order after a fronted Swedish adverbial.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Start with **who + does what**, then add the rest. If something else moves to
the front of a main clause, keep the finite verb in position two and move the
subject behind it.
</MemoryTip>
