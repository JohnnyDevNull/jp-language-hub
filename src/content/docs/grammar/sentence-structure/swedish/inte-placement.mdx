The position of Swedish <span lang="sv">inte</span> depends on the type of
clause. The central contrast is simple: it normally follows the finite verb in a
main clause but precedes the finite verb in a subordinate clause.

## Main clauses

In a main clause, <span lang="sv">inte</span> normally comes after the finite
verb and after a subject that follows that verb:

- <span lang="sv">Jag <strong>kommer inte</strong> idag.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Idag <strong>kommer jag inte</strong>.</span>
- <span lang="sv">Jag <strong>har inte</strong> köpt bilen.</span>

With a compound verb phrase, <span lang="sv">inte</span> follows the finite
auxiliary and precedes the non-finite verb:

<p lang="sv">Hon <strong>vill inte gå</strong> hem.</p>

## Subordinate clauses

In a subordinate clause, <span lang="sv">inte</span> normally comes before the
finite verb:

- <span lang="sv">… eftersom jag <strong>inte kommer</strong> idag.</span>
- <span lang="sv">… att hon <strong>inte har</strong> köpt bilen.</span>
- <span lang="sv">… om vi <strong>inte kan</strong> träffas.</span>

This contrast is summarized by [BIFF](../biff/).

## Questions

In a yes/no question, the finite verb comes first, followed by the subject and
then <span lang="sv">inte</span>:

<p lang="sv">Kommer du inte imorgon?</p>

With a question word:

<p lang="sv">Varför kommer du inte imorgon?</p>

The intonation and context can make a negative question sound surprised or as
if the speaker expected a positive answer.

## Imperatives and infinitives

With a negative imperative, place <span lang="sv">inte</span> after the verb:

<p lang="sv">Glöm inte nycklarna!</p>

When an infinitive itself is negated, <span lang="sv">inte</span> normally comes
before it:

<p lang="sv">Det är svårt att inte tänka på det.</p>

## Direct comparison

| Language | Main clause | Because-clause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="de">🇩🇪 Deutsch</span> | <span lang="de">Sie arbeitet heute nicht.</span> | <span lang="de">… weil sie heute nicht arbeitet.</span> |
| <span lang="en">🇬🇧 English</span> | <span lang="en">She is not working today.</span> | <span lang="en">… because she is not working today.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">🇸🇪 Svenska</span> | <span lang="sv">Hon arbetar inte idag.</span> | <span lang="sv">… eftersom hon inte arbetar idag.</span> |

<MistakeBox
	incorrect="Jag vet att hon arbetar inte idag."
	correct="Jag vet att hon inte arbetar idag."
	lang="sv"
>
Do not place <span lang="sv">inte</span> after the finite verb merely because the
clause contains an explicit subject. First decide whether it is a main clause or
a subordinate clause.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
**Main clause:** finite first, then <span lang="sv">inte</span>.  
**Subordinate clause:** <span lang="sv">inte</span> first, then finite.
</MemoryTip>
