---
title: Swedish Inversion
description: Place the subject correctly when a Swedish main clause begins with time, place, an object, or another constituent.
---

Inversion occurs when a Swedish declarative main clause begins with something
other than the subject. The finite verb remains in the second position, so the
subject follows the verb.

**Level:** A1–A2  
**Focus:** Swedish main clauses

## Core rule

Neutral order:

<p lang="sv"><strong>Jag</strong> <strong>jobbar</strong> hemma idag.</p>

Time first:

<p lang="sv"><strong>Idag</strong> <strong>jobbar</strong> <strong>jag</strong> hemma.</p>

Place first:

<p lang="sv"><strong>Hemma</strong> <strong>jobbar</strong> <strong>jag</strong> idag.</p>

The useful main-clause pattern is:

> **fronted constituent + finite verb + subject + rest of clause**

This is the visible consequence of the [V2 rule](../swedish-v2/).

## With more than one verb

Only the finite verb moves before the subject:

- <span lang="sv">Imorgon <strong>ska</strong> vi träffa Anna.</span>
- <span lang="sv">I går <strong>hade</strong> jag inte ätit frukost.</span>

The infinitive <span lang="sv">träffa</span> and the supine
<span lang="sv">ätit</span> remain later in the clause.

## After an initial subordinate clause

An entire subordinate clause can occupy the first position. The following main
clause then uses inversion:

<p lang="sv"><strong>När mötet är slut</strong> <strong>går</strong> vi hem.</p>

Compare German and English:

| Language | Example |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="de">🇩🇪 Deutsch</span> | <span lang="de">Wenn das Meeting vorbei ist, gehen wir nach Hause.</span> |
| <span lang="en">🇬🇧 English</span> | <span lang="en">When the meeting is over, we go home.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">🇸🇪 Svenska</span> | <span lang="sv">När mötet är slut går vi hem.</span> |

Swedish and German place the main-clause finite verb before the subject here.
English normally does not.

## Inversion and questions

Yes/no questions also place the finite verb before the subject:

<p lang="sv">Kommer du imorgon?</p>

However, it is useful to distinguish the two patterns:

- a statement keeps the verb in position two after a fronted constituent;
- a yes/no question normally starts with the finite verb.

:::caution[Common mistake]
Fronting an expression does not create an extra position before the ordinary
sentence. It changes the order of the main clause.

<span lang="sv">❌ Efter jobbet jag ska handla.</span>  
<span lang="sv">✅ Efter jobbet ska jag handla.</span>
:::

:::tip[Memory tip]
If the subject is not first, listen for the pair **verb + subject** immediately
after the opening block: <span lang="sv">Idag <strong>kommer jag</strong> …</span>
:::
