Swedish verbs do not change according to person or number. The same form is
used with <span lang="sv">jag</span>, <span lang="sv">du</span>,
<span lang="sv">hon</span>, and <span lang="sv">de</span>. The main learning
task is therefore recognizing the form and choosing the right tense.

## Core forms

Using <span lang="sv">köpa</span> (“buy”) as an example:

| Form | Swedish form | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Infinitive | <span lang="sv">att {buy.lemma}</span> | After modal verbs: <span lang="sv">Jag vill köpa boken.</span> |
| Imperative | <span lang="sv">{buy.morphology.imperative}!</span> | Commands: <span lang="sv">{buy.morphology.imperative} boken!</span> |
| Present | <span lang="sv">{buy.morphology.present}</span> | Now, generally, or repeatedly: <span lang="sv">Jag köper en bok.</span> |
| Preterite | <span lang="sv">{buy.morphology.preterite}</span> | Completed past events: <span lang="sv">Jag köpte en bok.</span> |
| Supine | <span lang="sv">{buy.morphology.supine}</span> | After <span lang="sv">har</span> or <span lang="sv">hade</span>: <span lang="sv">Jag har köpt en bok.</span> |
| Perfect | <span lang="sv">har {buy.morphology.supine}</span> | A compound tense built with <span lang="sv">har</span> + supine. |

:::note[One form for every person]
<span lang="sv">Jag köper</span>, <span lang="sv">du köper</span>, and
<span lang="sv">de köper</span> all use the same present form.
:::

## The form chain

Learning a new Swedish verb as a short form chain is more useful than learning
only its infinitive:

<table>
	<thead>
		<tr>
			<th scope="col">Infinitive</th>
			<th scope="col">Imperative</th>
			<th scope="col">Present</th>
			<th scope="col">Preterite</th>
			<th scope="col">Supine</th>
		</tr>
	</thead>
	<tbody>
		{verbs.map(({ data }) => (
			<tr>
				<td lang="sv">att {data.lemma}</td>
				<td lang="sv">{data.morphology.imperative}!</td>
				<td lang="sv">{data.morphology.present}</td>
				<td lang="sv">{data.morphology.preterite}</td>
				<td lang="sv">{data.morphology.supine}</td>
			</tr>
		))}
	</tbody>
</table>

These examples represent different [Swedish verb groups](../verb-groups/).
The group is useful as a pattern, but irregular forms still need to be learned.

## Building compound tenses

The supine combines with a finite form of <span lang="sv">ha</span>:

- Perfect: <span lang="sv">Jag har köpt en bok.</span> — I have bought a book.
- Pluperfect: <span lang="sv">Jag hade köpt boken.</span> — I had bought the book.

The supine is not the same as the infinitive or the preterite:

- <span lang="sv">Jag vill köpa boken.</span> — infinitive after a modal verb
- <span lang="sv">Jag köpte boken i går.</span> — preterite
- <span lang="sv">Jag har köpt boken.</span> — supine after <span lang="sv">har</span>

## Direct comparison

| Deutsch | English | Svenska |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="de">Ich kaufe ein Auto.</span> | <span lang="en">I am buying a car.</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag köper en bil.</span> |
| <span lang="de">Ich kaufte ein Auto.</span> | <span lang="en">I bought a car.</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag köpte en bil.</span> |
| <span lang="de">Ich habe ein Auto gekauft.</span> | <span lang="en">I have bought a car.</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag har köpt en bil.</span> |

English may use a progressive form where Swedish uses the ordinary present.
The similar-looking perfect constructions are useful bridges, but their usage
does not match perfectly in every context.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox
	incorrect={`Jag har ${buy.lemma} en bok.`}
	correct={`Jag har ${buy.morphology.supine} en bok.`}
	lang="sv"
>
	Use the supine after <span lang="sv">har</span> or <span lang="sv">hade</span>.
</MistakeBox>

<MistakeBox
	incorrect={`Hon ${buy.morphology.present} och jag ${buy.morphology.present}er.`}
	correct={`Hon ${buy.morphology.present} och jag ${buy.morphology.present}.`}
	lang="sv"
>
	Swedish verb forms do not change according to person or number. Do not infer
	the preterite or supine from the infinitive alone when a verb is irregular;
	learn the form chain.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Store each verb as at least three forms: present — preterite — supine. Add the
infinitive when it is not already obvious.
</MemoryTip>
