Swedish prepositions are best learned in complete expressions. A German or
English preposition can be a useful hint, but it is not a reliable one-to-one
translation.

## Place: where something is

| Preposition | Typical idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">i</span> | in a country, city, area, or enclosed place | <span lang="sv">Malmö ligger i Skåne.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">på</span> | on, at, or in many activity/place expressions | <span lang="sv">Hon väntar på caféet.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">vid</span> | by, at, next to | <span lang="sv">Bänken står vid dörren.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">under</span> | under | <span lang="sv">Väskan ligger under stolen.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">över</span> | over, above, across | <span lang="sv">Bron går över älven.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">mellan</span> | between | <span lang="sv">Caféet ligger mellan biblioteket och torget.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">mittemot</span> | opposite, across from | <span lang="sv">Apoteket ligger mittemot hotellet.</span> |

## Direction: where something goes

| Preposition | Typical idea | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">från</span> | from | <span lang="sv">Bussen kommer från Lund.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">till</span> | to | <span lang="sv">Vi går till biblioteket.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">mot</span> | towards, in the direction of | <span lang="sv">Spårvagnen mot centrum stannar här.</span> |

Use <span lang="sv">till</span> when the destination is the goal of the
movement. Use <span lang="sv">mot</span> when the phrase gives a direction,
line, sign, or route.

## Common fixed choices

Some frequent choices must simply be stored as phrases:

| Svenska | Deutsch | English |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">Jag bor på Sveavägen.</span> | <span lang="de">Ich wohne in der Sveavägen.</span> | <span lang="en">I live on Sveavägen.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">Vi sitter på tåget till Kalmar.</span> | <span lang="de">Wir sitzen im Zug nach Kalmar.</span> | <span lang="en">We are sitting on the train to Kalmar.</span> |
| <span lang="sv">Välkommen till kursen!</span> | <span lang="de">Willkommen im Kurs!</span> | <span lang="en">Welcome to the course!</span> |

Notice that German <span lang="de">in</span> and English <span lang="en">in</span>
do not always map to Swedish <span lang="sv">i</span>.

## In a sentence

Place and direction phrases often appear near the end of a neutral main clause:

<p lang="sv">Jag bor på Sveavägen.</p>

If a place or time phrase moves to the front, Swedish still keeps the finite
verb in position two:

<p lang="sv">I morgon åker jag till Kalmar.</p>

See [Swedish V2](../../../sentence-structure/swedish/v2/) for the word-order
rule.

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- Do not translate German or English prepositions mechanically.
	- Do not use <span lang="sv">i</span> as the default for every German
	  <span lang="de">in</span>.
	- After a fronted place or time phrase, keep the finite verb second:
	  <span lang="sv">I morgon åker jag</span>, not
	  <span lang="sv">I morgon jag åker</span>.
</MistakeBox>

<MemoryTip>
Store the whole expression first:
<span lang="sv"><strong>bo på en gata</strong></span>,
<span lang="sv"><strong>vänta på caféet</strong></span>,
<span lang="sv"><strong>gå till biblioteket</strong></span>.
</MemoryTip>
