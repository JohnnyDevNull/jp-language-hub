Swedish possessive pronouns often change form according to what is possessed.
For many forms, the choice is the same pattern learners already know from
<span lang="sv">en</span> nouns, <span lang="sv">ett</span> nouns, and plural
nouns.

## Core pattern

Choose the possessive form from the noun that follows:

| Possessor | <span lang="sv">en</span> noun | <span lang="sv">ett</span> noun | Plural noun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | <span lang="sv">min bil</span> | <span lang="sv">mitt hus</span> | <span lang="sv">mina böcker</span> |
| you (singular) | <span lang="sv">din bil</span> | <span lang="sv">ditt hus</span> | <span lang="sv">dina böcker</span> |
| he / she / they, own | <span lang="sv">sin bil</span> | <span lang="sv">sitt hus</span> | <span lang="sv">sina böcker</span> |
| we | <span lang="sv">vår bil</span> | <span lang="sv">vårt hus</span> | <span lang="sv">våra böcker</span> |
| you (plural) | <span lang="sv">er bil</span> | <span lang="sv">ert hus</span> | <span lang="sv">era böcker</span> |

<MemoryTip title="The possessed noun decides">
In <span lang="sv">min bil</span>, <span lang="sv">mitt hus</span>, and
<span lang="sv">mina böcker</span>, the possessor is always “I”. The form
changes because <span lang="sv">bil</span>, <span lang="sv">hus</span>, and
<span lang="sv">böcker</span> are different noun forms.
</MemoryTip>

## Forms that do not change

Some Swedish possessives do not change for <span lang="sv">en</span>,
<span lang="sv">ett</span>, or plural:

| Possessor | Form | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| he | <span lang="sv">hans</span> | <span lang="sv">hans bil</span>, <span lang="sv">hans hus</span>, <span lang="sv">hans böcker</span> |
| she | <span lang="sv">hennes</span> | <span lang="sv">hennes bil</span>, <span lang="sv">hennes hus</span>, <span lang="sv">hennes böcker</span> |
| they | <span lang="sv">deras</span> | <span lang="sv">deras bil</span>, <span lang="sv">deras hus</span>, <span lang="sv">deras böcker</span> |

These forms point to a possessor, but they do not show whether the possessed
thing is an <span lang="sv">en</span> noun, an <span lang="sv">ett</span> noun,
or plural.

## Min, din, and sin

<span lang="sv">Min</span>, <span lang="sv">din</span>, and
<span lang="sv">sin</span> follow the same noun pattern:

| Noun type | My | Your | Own |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">en</span> noun | <span lang="sv">min jacka</span> | <span lang="sv">din jacka</span> | <span lang="sv">sin jacka</span> |
| <span lang="sv">ett</span> noun | <span lang="sv">mitt rum</span> | <span lang="sv">ditt rum</span> | <span lang="sv">sitt rum</span> |
| Plural noun | <span lang="sv">mina skor</span> | <span lang="sv">dina skor</span> | <span lang="sv">sina skor</span> |

The grammar pattern is shared, but <span lang="sv">sin</span>,
<span lang="sv">sitt</span>, and <span lang="sv">sina</span> have an extra
meaning: they refer back to the subject of the same clause.

## Sin, sitt, and sina

Use <span lang="sv">sin</span>, <span lang="sv">sitt</span>, or
<span lang="sv">sina</span> when a third-person subject possesses something
and the possessive means “his/her/their own”.

| Swedish | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| <span lang="sv">Anna tar sin jacka.</span> | Anna takes her own jacket. |
| <span lang="sv">Anna tar hennes jacka.</span> | Anna takes another woman's jacket. |
| <span lang="sv">Erik säljer sitt hus.</span> | Erik sells his own house. |
| <span lang="sv">Erik säljer hans hus.</span> | Erik sells another man's house. |

See the full [Swedish sin, sitt, and sina](../sin-sitt-sina/) reference for the
clause-boundary rule and more examples.

## Direct comparison

German and English do not mark the same “own versus somebody else's”
distinction in the possessive form:

| Deutsch | English | Svenska |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <span lang="de">Ich nehme meine Jacke.</span> | <span lang="en">I take my jacket.</span> | <span lang="sv">Jag tar min jacka.</span> |
| <span lang="de">Du nimmst deine Jacke.</span> | <span lang="en">You take your jacket.</span> | <span lang="sv">Du tar din jacka.</span> |
| <span lang="de">Anna nimmt ihre Jacke.</span> | <span lang="en">Anna takes her jacket.</span> | <span lang="sv">Anna tar sin jacka.</span> |

The German and English third-person examples can be ambiguous without context.
Swedish usually uses <span lang="sv">sin</span> when the jacket belongs to
Anna herself.

## Common mistakes

<MistakeBox title="Common mistakes">
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">mitt bil</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">min bil</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">min hus</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">mitt hus</span>
	- ❌ <span lang="sv">din böcker</span>
	  ✅ <span lang="sv">dina böcker</span>
	- ❌ Choosing <span lang="sv">min</span>, <span lang="sv">din</span>, or
	  <span lang="sv">sin</span> from the owner. Choose the form from the thing
	  that is owned.
</MistakeBox>
